## E1 — desirable difficulty inverted u spacing proxy vs retention
- Date: 2026-10-08
- Project: local/sandbox-desirable-difficulty
- Tags: [teaching][performance][proxy][testing]
- What: Expected-value memory model of spaced practice: five study events per gap, recall p = exp(-gap/S), stability S grows by a bonus proportional to (1-p) when recall succeeds. In-session performance fell monotonically with gap (0.980 massed to 0.000 at gap 64) while delayed retention peaked at an intermediate gap of 4 (0.2533) and fell on both sides (massed 0.0000, gap 64 0.0046).
- Why built: The Bjork desirable-difficulties kernel says smooth in-session performance is a poor proxy for retention; I wanted to see where the claim stops holding rather than only where it holds.
- Design: Deterministic expected-value update (no sampling) over gaps 0 to 64 under two modes: a difficulty-gain mode and a constant-gain control where every study teaches equally. Reported mean in-session recall beside delayed recall at a fixed delay of 40.
- Got right: The proxy and the truth diverge exactly as the kernel says: the practice condition with the best in-session score (massed, 0.980) had the worst delayed retention (0.0000), and the best retention came from a condition scoring only 0.489 during practice.
- Where/why it failed: Two limits. Difficulty is desirable only up to a point: past gap 8 recall fails so the bonus vanishes and retention collapses (0.0129 at gap 16), an inverted U, not more is better. And my prediction that a constant-gain control would favour massed practice was wrong: all gaps tied at 0.0718, so in this model spacing has no effect at all without the difficulty-gain mechanism, meaning the result is carried by that assumed mechanism.
- Fix: Treat the spacing gap as a tuned quantity (here near where recall is about 0.5) rather than maximising it, and read in-session performance beside a delayed-retention readout instead of alone.
- Why this fix: Retention depends on the product of the gain bonus and the chance of a successful retrieval, which peak at an intermediate difficulty; the paired readout is the only thing that exposes the proxy flipping sign.
- Rule of thumb: In this model, in-session recall and delayed retention move in opposite directions up to the optimum gap and then both collapse, so choose spacing so practice recall lands near the middle of the range, and remember the benefit exists only if harder retrieval really builds more stability.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; the numbers are copied from its output. The difficulty-gain mechanism is an assumption built into the model, and no human learner data were used.
