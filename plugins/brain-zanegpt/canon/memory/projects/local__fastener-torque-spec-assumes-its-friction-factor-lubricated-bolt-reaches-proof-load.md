## E1 — fastener torque spec assumes its friction factor lubricated bolt reaches proof load
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Project: local/fastener-torque-spec-assumes-its-friction-factor-lubricated-bolt-reaches-proof-load
- Tags: [fastener-torque][sandbox][numerics][model]
- What: New domain FASTENER-TORQUE seeded from a bench scenario: an M10x1.5 class 8.8 bolt (proof load 33.6 kN, yield 37.1 kN) torqued to 75 percent of proof needs 50.5 N*m dry (nut factor 0.20); the same torque on a lightly oiled bolt (0.15) loads it to exactly 100 percent of proof, and on a heavily lubricated one (0.12) to 125 percent of proof, 113 percent of yield.
- Why built: Seeding a domain with no records. The common shop habit is to apply the quoted torque whatever the thread condition, and I wanted to see how much margin the quoted figure really has.
- Design: Short-form torque T = K x d x F with stress area 58 mm squared, proof strength 580 MPa, yield about 640 MPa; preload target 75 percent of proof; four thread conditions (dry 0.20, plated 0.17, oiled 0.15, heavy 0.12); then the dry torque under a 0.15 to 0.25 spread of K. Closed-form arithmetic, no randomness.
- Got right: The model computes the dry torque from the bolt data instead of assuming it (50.5 N*m, in line with published tables of about 45 to 50), and the preload ratio at a fixed torque is simply the dry K over the actual K.
- Where/why it failed: My break scenario predicted the dry torque would leave part of a K scatter outside a 60 to 95 percent window, and it did but only at one end: 4 of 5 K values were inside, the one outside being 0.15 (100 percent of proof). The scaled-torque result (0.75 at every K) holds by algebra of the model, so it shows the correction is consistent, not that real bolts follow it.
- Fix: Take the torque value together with the thread condition it was quoted for, and scale it by the actual nut factor over the quoted one when the condition differs; where a joint is critical, use angle or tension control instead of torque.
- Why this fix: Torque only measures friction plus clamp, so the clamp force follows the unknown friction factor; scaling by K restores the target within the model and tension control removes the unknown.
- Rule of thumb: A quoted bolt torque is only valid for the friction condition it was written for: using the dry value on an oiled bolt takes this M10 8.8 to 100 percent of proof and on a heavily lubricated one past yield, so reduce the torque by the nut-factor ratio or switch to a tension-based method.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox for the arithmetic; the nut factors, proof and yield strengths are recalled typical values, not measured or re-checked against a standard, and the short-form model ignores thread-pitch and underhead terms.
