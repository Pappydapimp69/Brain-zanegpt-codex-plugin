## E1 — sandbox missing laughter needs the response window
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/sandbox-missing-laughter-needs-the-response-window
- Tags: [humor][simulation][measurement][performance][sandbox]
- What: A deterministic model of a performer who tells one joke, waits a window W, counts audible laughs against a threshold, and reads "below threshold" as "the joke failed". It sweeps latency distribution (lognormal median and spread), audience size, ambient noise (masks real laughs and adds false laugh-like events), the performer's threshold, the assumed window versus the true one, and polite laughter on failed jokes. It reports miss rate (landed joke read as failed), recall (failed joke produces silence), balanced accuracy and the posterior that a silence means failure.
- Why built: The kernel "missing laughter is the reveal" assumes the absence of an answering laugh is legible. This tests when that is true and where silence is misread (late laugh, small room, text channel). It models measurement structure under stated assumptions only; it makes no claim about any real audience or person, and every parameter value is an assumed stand-in.
- Design: Exact arithmetic, no RNG and no timing. Laughs in the window are binomial with success p_resp x F(W) x audibility, plus Poisson noise events; F is the closed-form lognormal CDF; audibility is 1/(1+noise). The posterior uses an assumed prior of 0.3 that a joke fails. Eight predictions were written before the first run (P1-P8). After three were refuted, a clearly labelled post-hoc section (H1-H4) was added and is reported separately. Scenarios include a polite-laughter break case and a known-window-but-tiny-room case.
- Got right: Window too short is the dominant error: at 0.25x the true 95th-percentile window the miss rate was 0.532 and the posterior fell to 0.446 (balanced accuracy 0.734), while 0.5x to 8x were fine in a quiet room (balanced accuracy 0.999 to 1.000). Text-channel conditions (median 8s, spread 0.9, response 0.08, n=20) missed 0.544 of landed jokes even with the window known, and a live-room window applied to the text channel missed 0.993. Too-long windows hurt only with noise: at noise 2, balanced accuracy was 0.721 at 4x versus 0.933 at 1x.
- Where/why it failed: Three pre-registered predictions were refuted. P6 (a late-laugh audience read with a live-tuned window misses at least 0.30) gave 0.015 at n=20, because many people give redundancy; post-hoc, the same late laughs missed 0.944 at n=3 and 0.194 at n=20. P4 (a reliable single partner keeps posterior 0.95) gave 0.815, since one person skipping one laugh is 5 to 10 percent and a single trial cannot average it out; at prior fail 0.05 the posterior fell to 0.351. P7 (polite laughter on half the room) kept recall near 0 as predicted but the posterior was 0.942, just under the 0.95 I wrote.
- Fix: Replace "silence reveals failure only if the window is known" with a three-part reading. Silence means failure to the degree that (a) the window covers the late tail of this audience, (b) the room or channel yields many independent chances to laugh, and (c) the prior and the established baseline make a skipped laugh unlikely. Treat the strength of the reveal as a likelihood ratio against the established response, not as a binary.
- Why this fix: The sweep shows window knowledge is neither sufficient nor strictly necessary. In a quiet room any assumed window at or above the true one kept the posterior at or above 0.90, while with the window known balanced accuracy was still below 0.80 in 12 of 24 size-by-response cells. A likelihood-ratio reading explains both: on the repeated-hook sequence (n=1, response 0.95, 0.60, 0.30, 0.05) the ratio for a silence rose 1.0, 4.4, 7.3, 9.8, so the same silence means more after the response was established than at the first repeat.
- Rule of thumb: Missing laughter is evidence only against an established response model: it reads reliably when the window spans the late tail, the room supplies many chances, and the listener has already seen the laugh arrive. In small rooms, late-laughing audiences and text channels a silence is weak evidence, and with polite laughter it is rare but still readable. In a song, establish the answered joke first, then withhold the answer.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand: the sandbox ran here, every number above is its printed output, and the pre-registered tally was 5 of 8 held with the post-hoc section 4 of 4. Assumed by construction (kept apart from the run): lognormal latency, independent responders, the noise model, the prior of 0.3, and each parameter value. Real audiences were left unmeasured; this is a structure-and-scoring model that makes claims about measurement, and makes none about any person.
