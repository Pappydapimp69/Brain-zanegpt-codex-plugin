## E1 — role rule needs word identity not phoneme identity
- Date: 2026-09-30
- Project: local/sandbox-role-rule-needs-word-identity-not-phoneme-identity
- Tags: [songwriting][rhyme][testing]
- What: The section-role rule for repeated end words (exclude repetition in a verse, count it in a post-chorus) must detect repetition by word identity; built on phoneme identity it wrongly excludes rich rhymes such as night/knight, right/write and see/sea.
- Why built: Compose the finding that a phoneme-only detector cannot tell night/knight from night/night with the finding that the repeated-end-word treatment must depend on section role.
- Design: Hand-written phonemes for 16 words; a verse or post-chorus letterer that treats a same-end-word pair by role, with "same" judged either by phoneme string or by word; five sections: three rich-rhyme verses, one true repetition verse and a title x4 post-chorus, each with a hand-set expected scheme.
- Got right: Word identity lettered all 5 sections as expected; phoneme identity lettered all 3 rich-rhyme verses XAXA instead of ABAB, and both handled the repetition verse (XAXA) and the post-chorus (AAAA) correctly.
- Where/why it failed: Nothing contradicted the prediction. The result is close to true by construction once rich rhymes are phoneme-identical, so its value is in naming which identity check the role rule silently depends on.
- Fix: Implement the role rule with an explicit word-identity check (normalised spelling), and keep the phoneme comparison only for deciding whether two different words rhyme.
- Why this fix: Phoneme identity folds rich rhyme into repetition, so the verse rule deletes a deliberate perfect rhyme; word identity separates them in 5 of 5 sections here.
- Rule of thumb: Before applying the exclude-repetition-in-verses rule, check repetition by word identity; with phoneme identity every rich rhyme (night/knight, right/write, see/sea) is read as repetition and dropped, 3 of 3 here.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; letterings are copied from its output. Real dictionary pronunciations were not used; the phonemes are hand-written.
