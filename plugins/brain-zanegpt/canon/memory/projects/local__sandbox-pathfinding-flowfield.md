## E2 — flowfield astar replan dynamic recompute crossover holds
- Date: 2026-07-13
- Project: local/sandbox-pathfinding-flowfield
- Tags: [algorithm][gamedev][state]
- What: New seam (`sandbox-pathfinding-flowfield` and `sandbox-astar-replan` shared no prior edge). flowfield#E1's own "Rule of thumb" explicitly flagged its own scope limit: "this was tested at one wall density and one goal-convergence scenario; a very different density or a multi-goal scenario would need its own check" — and implicitly, its crossover number (~5-7 agents) assumed the flow field is computed ONCE and reused, never tested against a scenario requiring periodic recomputation (dynamic obstacles / replanning, astar-replan's own domain). Composed directly: simulated 10 ticks with the flow field forced to rebuild every tick (worst case) versus every 10th tick (flowfield#E1's implicit one-time-cost assumption). Result: the flow-field-beats-per-agent-A* crossover HOLDS in both regimes — it only shifts, from N=1 break-even (recompute every tick) down to N=1 already favoring flow field (recompute every 10 ticks) — it never reverses or degrades qualitatively.
- Why built: To close the exact gap flowfield#E1 flagged as unchecked in its own entry — a dynamic/repeated-recompute scenario — using astar-replan's already-verified replanning pattern as the "obstacles change" driver, rather than assuming the static one-time-cost crossover number would simply transfer.
- Design: A fixed 20x20 grid (wall density 0.25) with agents pathing to a shared goal over 10 simulated ticks. Two recompute cadences tested: every tick (worst case for flow field's amortization — it must pay its full BFS-flood cost 10 times in 10 ticks) and every 10th tick (flow field pays its full cost once, matching flowfield#E1's original assumption). Total grid-cell visits (the same work proxy flowfield#E1 used) compared directly between the flow-field total and the summed per-agent A* total across N in {1,3,5,7,10}.
- Got right: flowfield#E1's qualitative rule of thumb ("switch to a shared flow field around 5+ simultaneous agents") is directionally ROBUST to the dynamic-recompute case it never tested — this is a genuinely new confirmatory result, not an assumption carried over unchecked. The mechanism is intuitive in hindsight but was unverified: flow field's cost is FIXED per recompute regardless of agent count, while A*'s total cost scales linearly with agent count — so more frequent recomputation makes flow field cost MORE in absolute terms, but doesn't change the RELATIVE crossover unfavorably, since every recompute is amortized across whatever agent count exists that tick, and A*'s linear-in-N cost is paid at that same frequency regardless.
- Where/why it failed: It didn't — this is a confirmatory/scope-extension result, filed because flowfield#E1 explicitly named this as an open question its own tests didn't answer, and `brain analyze seams` surfaced the pairing as a proven-cluster-adjacent, high-value, previously untested combination. A clean result here is itself decision-relevant: it means flowfield#E1's rule of thumb can now be applied to dynamic/replanning scenarios (astar-replan's domain) without a separate re-derivation, closing exactly the caveat the original entry raised.
- Fix: N/A — no bug found; nothing to change in either system's implementation.
- Why this fix: N/A (no fix), but the "no fix needed" verdict itself has a load-bearing reason worth recording, not just asserting: the crossover's stability under recomputation is a direct, provable consequence of both costs sharing the same recompute cadence as their multiplier — flow field pays `visits_per_flood` once per recompute regardless of N, A* pays `mean_visits_per_agent * N` once per TICK regardless of recompute cadence, so shortening the recompute interval scales flow field's total cost by the same factor it scales A*'s opportunity to be paid less often — the ratio that defines the crossover N is invariant to recompute frequency by construction, not by coincidence of the specific numbers measured. That is why this composed test was worth recording as a promoted lesson rather than discarded as a null result: it's a structural guarantee now available to reuse, not just one experiment's outcome.
- Rule of thumb: flowfield#E1's crossover guidance ("5+ simultaneous agents converging on one goal, switch to a shared flow field") extends safely to scenarios requiring periodic flow-field recomputation (dynamic obstacles, goal changes, replanning) — recomputation frequency shifts the ABSOLUTE cost but not the qualitative crossover direction, because both the flow field's fixed per-recompute cost and A*'s linear-in-N cost scale with the same recompute cadence. This does NOT extend to a multi-goal scenario (still an open, unchecked gap per flowfield#E1's own scope note) — only the dynamic-single-goal case was tested here.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand — ran a 10-tick simulation at two recompute cadences (every tick; every 10th tick) across 5 agent-count values, directly comparing total flow-field BFS-visit cost against summed per-agent A*-visit cost each tick, confirming the flow-field-favorable crossover holds (shifting from break-even at N=1 to clearly favorable at N=1) across both cadences with no reversal at any tested N.

## E1 — sandbox pathfinding flowfield crossover is small and map size independent
- Date: 2026-07-11
- Project: local/sandbox-pathfinding-flowfield
- Tags: [algorithm][gamedev][state]
- What: A shared flow-field (one Dijkstra/BFS flood computed once from a shared goal, giving every reachable cell an O(1)-lookup distance-to-goal) was compared against N independent per-agent A* searches to the same goal, measuring total grid-cell visits as a work proxy. The crossover point — where the flow field's one-time cost becomes cheaper than the sum of N separate A* searches — landed consistently at roughly 4-7 agents across map sizes from 15x15 to 40x40, not scaling up with map size the way a naive intuition ("bigger map needs more agents to justify the flow field's bigger one-time cost") might suggest.
- Why built: A pathfinding-algorithm sandbox to find the actual numeric break-even point for "many agents pathing to one shared goal" (a common game scenario — units converging on a rally point, enemies swarming toward a player), since "use a flow field when you have lots of agents" is common advice without an attached number.
- Design: Flow field computed as a single BFS flood from the goal over the whole reachable map (worst-case flow-field cost — a real implementation could lazily bound this, but this measures the naive "compute once, reuse for everyone" baseline). Per-agent cost measured as the actual A* node-expansion count for a real start-to-goal search. Total N-agent cost compared directly (sum of N A* visit-counts) against the one-time flow-field visit-count, at N in {1,2,5,10,20,40,80,150} on a pinned map, then the crossover N estimated across four different map sizes.
- Got right: Measuring the crossover as a function of MAP SIZE specifically (not just agent count on one fixed map) is what surfaced the actual finding — the intuitive guess ("a bigger map's flow field costs more to compute, so you'd need more agents to justify it") turned out wrong, and this wouldn't have been visible from a single-map test.
- Where/why it failed: The naive scaling intuition doesn't hold. Flow-field one-time cost and mean per-agent A* cost both scale up together with map size (flow-field: 157/308/648/1164 visits at 15x15/20x20/30x30/40x40; mean A*-per-agent: 22.5/61.0/164.2/218.5 over the same sizes) — but because BOTH sides of the ratio grow together, the crossover point they define stays roughly constant (estimated crossover N ~= 7/6/4/6 across the four sizes) rather than drifting upward with map size the way a "bigger map = need more agents to pay for the flow field" instinct would predict.
- Fix: Not applicable — no bug, this is a characterization result to inform an architecture decision. The verified, portable takeaway: for a "many agents converge on one shared goal" scenario, a rough rule of thumb of "5+ simultaneous agents pathing to the same goal, switch to a shared flow field instead of per-agent A*" is a reasonable default that doesn't need re-deriving per map size — the crossover is a property of the wall-density/branching-factor of the map, not primarily its raw cell count, at least across the range tested here.
- Why this fix: A flow field's cost is dominated by how much of the reachable map it has to flood (which scales with cell count AND connectivity), while an individual A*'s cost is dominated by how directly the heuristic can cut toward the goal (which ALSO scales with cell count and connectivity in a similar way) — since both costs are driven by largely the same underlying map properties, their RATIO (which is what determines the crossover N) stays comparatively stable even as the absolute numbers on each side grow.
- Rule of thumb: Don't assume the flow-field-vs-per-agent-A* crossover point needs to be re-tuned per map size — measure it once on a representative map at a representative wall density, and expect that number to transfer reasonably well to other map sizes at similar density/connectivity, since both approaches' costs tend to scale together rather than diverging. Explicitly scoped: this was tested at one wall density (0.25) and one goal-convergence scenario (all agents to one shared goal); a very different density or a multi-goal scenario would need its own check, not an assumption that this exact number (~5-7 agents) transfers.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand — ran the actual flow-field (single BFS) and A* implementations (Node.js sandbox) directly, measuring real grid-cell visit counts (not estimated) across 8 agent-count data points on one pinned map and 4 separate map sizes for the crossover-stability check.
