## E1 — thermostat band below lag overshoot adds cycles not precision
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Project: local/thermostat-band-below-lag-overshoot-adds-cycles-not-precision
- Tags: [thermostat-control][sandbox][numerics][model]
- What: New domain THERMOSTAT-CONTROL seeded from a bench scenario: a 2 kW electric heater under on/off hysteresis control in a room (200 kJ/K, 60 W/K loss, 20 C setpoint) whose output lags the switch. With a 120 s lag the temperature swing is the band plus a constant overshoot of about 1.18 K (band 1.0, 0.5, 0.25 gave swings of 2.16, 1.68, 1.44 K), while with no lag the overshoot is 0.02 K.
- Why built: Seeding a domain with no records. The common advice for a swingy room is to narrow the hysteresis band, and I wanted to see what that buys when the heater lags.
- Design: Euler simulation at 1 s steps, 7 h with the first hour discarded; switch on below setpoint minus half the band, off above plus half; heater output equals the switch delayed by the lag. Bands 1.0, 0.5, 0.25 K at lags 0 and 120 s; swing is max minus min temperature, cycles counted per hour.
- Got right: At lag 0 the swing equalled the band plus 0.02 K and narrowing the band four times cut the cycle spacing by the same factor (8.67 to 33.83 cycles per hour); at lag 120 s the overshoot stayed within 0.03 K across the three bands.
- Where/why it failed: My prediction that narrowing the band at lag 120 s would cut the swing by under 0.3 K was wrong: it cut it by 0.72 K, the same absolute gain as at lag 0, because the overshoot is a constant added on top. What the lag changes is the relative gain (swing 2.16 to 1.44 K is a third, against three quarters at lag 0) and the cycle cost (+48 percent against +290 percent).
- Fix: Estimate the lag overshoot first (swing at a wide band minus the band) and set the band no narrower than the precision still wanted above that floor; spend effort on the lag or a predictive switch-off if the floor is the problem.
- Why this fix: The swing cannot go below the overshoot however narrow the band is, so band width only moves the part of the swing that is above that floor.
- Rule of thumb: Under on/off control with a delayed heater, swing is the band plus a lag-set overshoot (1.18 K at 120 s here); narrowing the band shaves the same absolute amount but cannot beat the floor, so measure the overshoot before trading cycles for precision.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; the room constants, the pure transport delay and the heater power are assumed round numbers, not measured on a real heater, and one lag value besides zero was run. This domain has no adjacent kernel or lesson in Brain, so the entry is isolated and cites nothing.
