## E1 — seeded rng save load vs shared stream coupling
- Date: 2026-10-08
- Project: local/seeded-rng-save-load-vs-shared-stream-coupling
- Tags: [determinism][testing][gamedev][state]
- What: Composes the saved seed-plus-count RNG discipline with the finding that tests on probabilistic outcomes couple to every other consumer of a shared stream.
- Why built: Exact save/load via seed and count holds, yet one shared stream still lets unrelated content shift a tested mechanic; the two lessons had never been run together.
- Design: Deterministic LCG; a 20-tick spread roll under test, with and without an unrelated per-tick consumer, on one shared stream versus separate per-mechanic streams; plus a save of seed and count after 50 draws and a resume.
- Got right: Resume from the saved seed and count reproduced the next 10 draws exactly.
- Where/why it failed: On the shared stream the added consumer changed the spread outcome in 9 of 20 ticks; split streams changed in 0.
- Fix: Give each mechanic its own seeded stream derived from a stable id, and save every stream's seed and count.
- Why this fix: Exact resume does not protect a test from stream shifts; isolation does, and per-stream counts keep save/load exact.
- Rule of thumb: Seed-and-count save/load gives exact resume but not isolation; use one stream per mechanic, otherwise adding any RNG consumer reshuffles every tested outcome.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; prediction (resume exact, shared stream changes some ticks, split streams change none) matched.
- Composed: test/E2, the-answering-deep/E3
- Polarity: compatible
- Sandbox: sandboxes/2026-10-08__seeded-rng-save-load-vs-shared-stream-coupling.py
- Prediction: Resume exact; shared-stream outcomes differ in at least one tick; split-stream outcomes differ in zero.
- Observed: RESULT: PASS resume exact=True shared-stream outcomes changed in 9/20 ticks, split streams in 0/20
- Scenarios: 1) save and resume after 50 draws; 2) shared stream with and without the extra consumer, built to break test stability; 3) split streams with and without it
