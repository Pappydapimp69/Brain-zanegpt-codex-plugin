## E1 — plain language scorer metamorphic check
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/plain-language-scorer
- Tags: [communication][plain-language][testing][teaching]
- What: A small plain-language scorer (average sentence length, share of words outside a common-word list of about 300 entries, digit and symbol count, jargon-word count) was tested by metamorphic checks on two labeled reply pairs (a "too technical" reply and the shorter rewrite that drew no complaint). It ranked both technical replies as harder (gaps 66.7 and 102.6 points), survived a shuffle control exactly, and improved when a technical reply was split and de-jargoned. But an averages-only version scored a 55-word run-on made of everyday words as plainer than before.
- Why built: Brain holds kernels about spoken register, defining terms for the reader, and cutting wasted effort, yet had no tested lesson about writing plainly. The user had twice called a reply too granular and technical. Before trusting any "plain English" checker, it needed a check that does not just assume the metric is right.
- Design: Stdlib Python, no randomness except one fixed-seed shuffle. Weights were fixed before the first run (sentence length + 40 x hard-word share + 0.5 x digits-and-symbols per 100 words + 2 x jargon per 100 words). Checks: (1) tech vs plainer in each pair; (2a) insert jargon, hard non-listed words, or a long clause into a plain reply and expect a worse score; (2b) hand-split a technical reply and swap its jargon for common words while keeping every number, expect a better score; (3) shuffle words inside each sentence and expect the word-based parts to stay identical; plus a break case that swaps every hard word for "thing". A side diagnostic (not in the score) counts contractions against full forms like "does not". A v2 adds 0.5 x longest sentence, added only after v1 failed.
- Got right: Both pairs ranked in the labeled direction by a wide margin. Swapping jargon and splitting sentences while keeping all 8 and 6 key numbers cut scores from 96.5 to 36.8 and from 125.1 to 32.8, so the gain came from packaging, not from deleting content. The shuffle control gave identical scores (delta 0.0000) on all 4 texts. Listed jargon inserted into a plain reply raised it by about +22. Plainer rewrites used more contractions (1 vs 0 and 2 vs 0), and expanding them added formal full forms.
- Where/why it failed: v1 failed 4 of 22 checks. A 55-word run-on of common words LOWERED the score (-1.0 and -1.2) because the share-based parts get diluted by extra easy words, so a plain-word run-on looks easier. Short extra sentences of hard words rose by only +1.3 and +2.0, under the +3 bar I had set, since a short sentence also drags the average length down. The break case (hard words replaced with "thing", 3-word sentences) scored 3.0, better than every real reply, so the metric can be gamed and says nothing about meaning. Also the labeled plainer rewrites dropped all 8 and 6 key numbers: they are plain partly by deleting content.
- Fix: Add a worst-sentence term (0.5 x longest sentence) so one long run-on cannot hide inside an average. With it the run-on rises +17.0 and +22.3, pairs stay ranked right (122.0 vs 39.9 and 133.1 vs 27.0), rewrites still improve, and the shuffle control stays exact. The two short hard-word inserts still miss the +3 bar (direction right, size small); that is reported, not tuned away.
- Why this fix: The failure came from a specific cause (averages dilute), so the repair targets that cause only, instead of re-weighting until the checks pass. This echoes the cognitive-load view that only wasted effort should be removed: long clauses and jargon are wasted effort, while the numbers are the real content and should be kept. It also echoes the curse-of-knowledge view that an author cannot see their own jargon, so the check has to be a deliberate list rather than instinct.
- Rule of thumb: When you check a draft for plain language, look at the worst sentence and the hard-word count, not only the averages, and keep the facts while shortening: a rewrite that scores well because it dropped the numbers is plain by deletion, not by clearer wording. Treat the score as a directional smoke test; two pairs from one rater show it agrees with that rater, not that it measures plainness in general.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox: the pair rankings and gaps, the 30 of 36 check tally (v1 18 of 22, v2 12 of 14), the dilution effect, the exact shuffle result and the gamed-text score. Assumed, taken on trust from the replies file: that the labels are right, that the common-word list and jargon list are sensible (I wrote them after reading the fixtures, so the jargon list partly overlaps the technical replies), and that people actually find the plainer rewrites easier. The hand rewrites were written by me.
