## E1 — origami single vertex kawasaki equals reflection composition identity
- Date: 2026-10-13
- Project: local/2026-10-13__origami-single-vertex-kawasaki-equals-reflection-composition-identity
- Tags: [origami][geometry][sandbox][testing][model]
- What: Single-vertex crease patterns with sector angles on a 15 degree grid and degree 3 to 6, tested for flat-foldability by composing the reflections across the creases and compared with Kawasaki's rule that the alternating angle sums are each 180 degrees.
- Why built: Seeding an origami domain from a real, citable result (Kawasaki's theorem for flat-foldable single vertices); I wanted to confirm the rule by an independent computation and see how a tolerance-based check misbehaves.
- Design: Enumerate every ordered sector composition of 360 into 3, 4, 5 and 6 parts in multiples of 15 degrees (44,528 patterns); fold test = the composition of the crease reflections is the identity; compare with the alternating-sum rule; add tolerance checks of 7.5, 15 and 30 degrees on the alternating sums.
- Got right: The reflection test and Kawasaki's rule agreed in all 44,528 patterns, and no odd-degree vertex was flat-foldable (0 of the degree 3 and 5 patterns).
- Where/why it failed: My predicted break did not happen at the tolerance I chose: a 7.5 degree tolerance accepted 0 wrong patterns because alternating sums on a 15 degree grid are multiples of 15, so only an exact match falls inside it. Tolerances equal to the grid step broke it: 15 degrees wrongly accepted 6,180 patterns and 30 degrees 12,030.
- Fix: Test flat-foldability of sector angles with exact arithmetic (integer degrees or rationals) or a tolerance strictly smaller than the smallest angle step the data can take.
- Why this fix: A tolerance wider than the quantum of the input is a different rule that happens to coincide only when the input is coarser than the tolerance, so the exact test is the only one that does not depend on the grid.
- Rule of thumb: A single vertex is flat-foldable exactly when its degree is even and each alternating angle sum is 180; check it with exact angles, since a tolerance as wide as the angle step wrongly accepts about 14 percent of grid patterns (6,180 of 44,528).
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox over every pattern in the grid; this covers single vertices only. Local conditions do not guarantee a whole crease pattern folds flat, and that wider claim was not tested. The theorem itself is from the published literature and the sandbox did not consult a source.
