## E1 — fake ad kernel source check
- Date: 2026-10-15
- Project: local/fake-ad-kernel-source-check
- Tags: [game-ads][comedy][research][verification]
- What: The fake-mobile-ad premise kernel checked against its own cited sources: all 6 numbers match
  (Homescapes 10 of 4,000-plus levels with pin pulling, Royal Match king ads since 2021 and $3
  billion spend, Penn State's 15 games and 2,500-plus reviews), but one of its five swap axes
  is misnamed: the study's third pattern is 'popular externalization', not 'borrowed fame'.
- Why built: Weakest-link train pick: an unapplied kernel whose substance is external facts, so per the
  training rule it is verified against the real sources rather than by a model; the kernel is
  used to generate parody premises, so a mislabelled axis would spread into every premise
  built on it.
- Design: Fetched sherwood.news (2024-06-14) and the Penn State news release (2025-01-13) and
  transcribed the relevant quotes into the sandbox; compared each kernel claim (six numbers
  and five pattern labels) with the transcribed source values.
- Got right: Every number in the kernel is exactly what its sources say, including the striking 10 of
  4,000-plus Homescapes levels, so the kernel's evidence for how rare the advertised gameplay
  is holds up.
- Where/why it failed: Pattern 3 is paraphrased under a different name: the kernel's 'borrowed fame' is the Penn
  State paper's 'popular externalization'. The meaning is close but a reader searching the
  paper for the kernel's label will not find it. I predicted all five labels would match.
- Fix: Quote taxonomy labels verbatim from the source and put any paraphrase in brackets (popular
  externalization, i.e. borrowed fame); when a kernel's value is in named categories, check
  the names against the source as well as the numbers.
- Why this fix: Category names are how later work cites and searches a taxonomy; a paraphrased label breaks
  that link silently while every number still checks out, so numeric spot checks alone miss
  it.
- Rule of thumb: Fact-check a kernel's labels as well as its numbers: the fake-ad kernel's 6/6 numbers
  matched its sources but 1 of 5 study patterns was renamed ('borrowed fame' for 'popular
  externalization').
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand against the fetched source pages (sherwood.news and psu.edu); the ACM
  paper itself was not opened, the Penn State release is the paper's institutional summary.
- Composed: ideas:COMEDY/fake-mobile-ad/premise-slot-machine-from-the-five-misleading-ad-patterns
- Picked-by: current-half
- Sandbox: sandboxes/2026-10-15__fake-ad-kernel-source-check.py
- Prediction: Written before fetching the Penn State page: every numeric claim and all five pattern labels
  in the kernel match the sources.
- Observed: RESULT: FAIL 6/6 numbers match the sources; labels 4/5 - kernel says 'borrowed fame', source
  says 'popular externalization'.
- Scenarios: 1) six numeric claims against sherwood.news and psu.edu; 2) five taxonomy labels against
  psu.edu, built to catch paraphrase drift.
