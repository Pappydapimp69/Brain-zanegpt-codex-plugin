## E1 — sandbox dbz merged cap eliminates burst niche
- Date: 2026-07-13
- Project: local/sandbox-dbz-merged
- Tags: [gamedesign][gamedev][testing]
- What: Combining two independently-verified lessons from two different sandboxes (this session's `local/sandbox-dbz-team-combat#E1`: a percent-of-defender-max-HP damage cap is needed to keep N-vs-1 team fights fair, and a different session's `local/sandbox-dbz-combat#E2`: a skill menu needs real Pareto diversity — each skill leading one axis, paying on another — to avoid collapsing to one dominant pick) surfaced a real interaction neither sandbox alone could see: applying the percent-max-HP cap doesn't just reduce a high-variance "burst" skill's (Big Bang Attack: high cost, high damage, high variance) win rate at high damage-thresholds — it can eliminate a threshold BAND entirely, and no other skill fills the gap. At a cap of 0.20 (the fraction verified safe for 1v1 in the source sandbox), a threshold band of 30%-45% of defender max HP went from "Big Bang Attack sometimes wins here" (uncapped) to "nothing wins here, ever" (capped) — because the cap makes exceeding 20% of max HP in one hit STRUCTURALLY IMPOSSIBLE regardless of roll, for every skill, not just the burst one.
- Why built: `brain analyze seams` (a new read-only analytics command) ranked `sandbox-dbz-combat` + `sandbox-dbz-team-combat` as the top untested pair adjacent to proven ground (6 shared tags: determinism/gamedesign/gamedev/input/state/testing) — this sandbox tested that specific seam directly rather than starting a new investigation cold.
- Design: A 5-skill menu built with explicit Pareto axes per the source sandbox's fix (Quick Strike: free/reliable/low; Ki Blast: mid-cost/mid-damage; Big Bang Attack: high-cost/high-variance/high-ceiling; Spirit Bomb: AoE/costly; Combo Rush: guaranteed multi-hit). For 400 randomized scenarios (defender max HP 300-1500, ki available 0-70, a damage THRESHOLD expressed as a FRACTION of defender max HP 5%-70%, matching how a percent-cap actually behaves), computed each affordable skill's success rate (500 trials each) at clearing that threshold in one hit, picked the argmax, and recorded wins both as an aggregate "ever-optimal" set AND broken down by threshold band.
- Got right: An initial pass (not preserved as a finding, treated as a dead end and discarded) used a damage-per-ki EFFICIENCY metric and found no cap effect at all — the metric itself was confounded (it favored cheap skills regardless of the cap, so the burst skill's actual niche never got measured). Redesigning around a binary threshold-clearance success rate, with threshold expressed as a fraction of defender max HP rather than an independently-randomized absolute number, is what made the cap's real, mechanical effect visible. The band-by-band breakdown (not just the aggregate "ever-optimal" count) is what revealed the actual finding — the aggregate count (3/4 single-target skills) was IDENTICAL across all three cap conditions tested, which would read as "the cap doesn't affect diversity" to anyone checking only the coarse metric.
- Where/why it failed: A skill's presence in an "ever-optimal" set says nothing about WHERE (which scenarios) it's optimal — Big Bang Attack stayed in the set at cap=0.20 only because it still won in lower threshold bands where the cap didn't bind, while its actual distinguishing niche (clearing thresholds above the cap fraction) was silently eliminated. This is structurally the same aggregation trap the source sandbox's own E3 entry already documented (a coarse metric can look unchanged while the underlying truth it's supposed to represent has actually shifted) — but manifesting in a NEW way: not a null/sentinel polluting a Set, but a legitimate member remaining in the Set while the specific thing that made it worth including quietly disappears.
- Fix: Not resolved in this sandbox as a game-design fix (no attempt made to redesign the cap or the skill menu to coexist) — the concrete, actionable finding is diagnostic: before shipping BOTH a percent-max-HP damage cap AND a high-variance "burst" skill archetype in the same combat system, check whether the cap fraction sits below any damage threshold the burst skill's design intends it to occasionally clear; if so, the skill's marketed niche is dead on arrival for any such threshold, regardless of how good the roll is.
- Why this fix: N/A — flagged as a diagnostic/characterization finding, not a verified resolution, since no specific redesign (e.g., a separate uncapped "execute" mechanic for burst skills, or raising the cap specifically for single-target attacker-count-1 cases) was built or tested here.
- Rule of thumb: When auditing a skill menu's "ever-optimal" diversity (per dbz-combat#E2/E3's methodology) in a system that ALSO applies a percent-max-HP damage cap (per dbz-team-combat#E1), check the WIN LOCATIONS (which scenario band each skill wins in), not just the aggregate ever-optimal set size — a cap can eliminate a high-variance skill's actual niche (any threshold above the cap fraction becomes unclearable by anyone, not just harder for that skill) while the aggregate metric stays unchanged because the skill still wins somewhere else. Two individually-correct design fixes (a defensive cap, a diverse skill menu) can silently conflict, and only a combined, band-aware measurement catches it.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand — ran the actual Node.js sandbox across 400 scenarios x 3 cap conditions (off, 0.20, 0.50), with 500 trials per skill-scenario success-rate estimate, directly observing the high-threshold band's win set go from {Big Bang Attack} (cap off) to {} (cap=0.20) to {Big Bang Attack} again (cap=0.50, since 0.50 exceeds that band's upper bound) — a clean, mechanically-explained, reproducible result (deterministic given the seed).
