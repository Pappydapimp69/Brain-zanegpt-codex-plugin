## E2 — loop combat quantize fix insufficient at extreme scale
- Date: 2026-07-13
- Project: local/sandbox-combined-loop-combat
- Tags: [determinism][architecture][state]
- What: Re-verifies loop-combat + power-scaling from a new angle. loop-combat#E1 established that combat damage must be quantized (floored) to an integer at the authoritative-state write boundary, since the raw formula (`power * rate`, capped at `%maxHp`) produces non-integer results 96% of the time. power-scaling#E1 established that compounding power (5.5x/arc) crosses `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER` around arc 18-21. Composed directly: with attacker power AND defender maxHp both compounding together (a realistic power-creep design where both sides scale), two mathematically-equivalent ways of computing the identical damage formula (`power * 0.05` vs `power / 20`) produced IDENTICAL quantized results through arc 24, then DIVERGED starting at arc 25 — differing by 32768 at arc 25 and by 134,217,728 at arc 30, despite being the same formula written two different (but exactly equal in real-number arithmetic) ways.
- Why built: loop-combat#E1's fix (quantize at the boundary) was verified using a realistic-but-bounded power range (800-2800) where the defender's maxHp stayed fixed, so the cap saturated well before precision loss could matter — this checks whether the fix still suffices once power values grow into the range power-scaling#E1 already flagged as float64-imprecise, and specifically whether a FIXED cap (which trivially masks precision loss once it saturates) is doing more of the safety work than the quantization fix itself once the cap ALSO scales into imprecise territory.
- Design: `power` and `defenderMaxHp` both multiplied by 5.5 per arc (power-scaling#E1's own compounding rate) for 30 arcs. Damage computed and quantized (`Math.floor(Math.min(rawDamage, defenderMaxHp*0.2))`) via two logically-identical expressions for `rawDamage` (`power*0.05` and `power/20`) at every arc, comparing the quantized results.
- Got right: With a FIXED defender maxHp (loop-combat#E1's original test condition), the two computation orders never diverged even well past power's own safe-integer crossover (checked through arc 30) — because the cap saturates almost immediately once power's exponential growth outpaces the fixed cap, and both formula variants compute a value so far above the cap that floating-point noise in the uncapped value never affects which side of `Math.min` wins. This is why loop-combat#E1's original verification, though limited to a bounded power range, didn't miss a live bug — it just didn't happen to exercise the case where the cap itself also loses precision.
- Where/why it failed: Once BOTH the raw damage and the cap threshold are large enough to have lost exact float64 precision, `Math.min(rawDamage, cap)` is comparing two already-imprecise numbers, and which one is smaller can depend on the exact rounding accumulated by each computation PATH — two expressions that are mathematically identical (`x*0.05` and `x/20`) round differently in IEEE-754 double precision at large magnitudes, so the `Math.min` comparison and the resulting quantized value depend on which arithmetic path was used to reach that magnitude, not just on the game-logic quantities involved. loop-combat#E1's fix (quantize the OUTPUT) cannot fix this, because the divergence originates in which of two equally-valid input expressions was used, before quantization ever runs.
- Fix: Not implemented as running code (a decision-layer finding about a design's safe operating range, matching the scope boundary of the related power-scaling#E1/recursion#E20 findings already in this project). The verified, portable diagnosis: loop-combat#E1's quantize-at-the-boundary fix is sufficient ONLY while at least one side of the `min()` comparison (raw damage or the cap) stays within safe-integer range — track BOTH sides' magnitude against `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`, not just the final quantized output's integer-ness, and if a design allows compounding growth on both attacker power and defender HP together, either cap the compounding (power-scaling's own established mitigation) well before arc 18-21, or perform the damage/cap comparison in a fixed-point or big-integer representation once both operands could plausibly exceed safe-integer range.
- Why this fix: This generalizes recursion#E20's already-stated finding ("logically-equivalent computation orders diverge in float64 near the safe-integer crossover, but real gates are plain integers so no live bug exists yet") to loop-combat's specific damage-quantization context: the risk isn't hypothetical here either — it requires the SAME two design choices recursion#E20 already flagged as jointly risky (compounding power AND a companion compounding value on the other side of a comparison) to actually manifest, and loop-combat's own quantization fix, verified only in a bounded-power regime, provides zero protection once that regime is exceeded.
- Rule of thumb: A "quantize the output to an integer" fix for a determinism/authoritative-state boundary is sufficient only within the FLOAT64-SAFE-INTEGER range of every value feeding the computation, not just the final result — before trusting such a fix at scale, check whether ANY compounding/growing input (not just the one already flagged, like power-scaling#E1's own attacker-power growth) could independently push a DIFFERENT operand in the same formula (a defender's HP, a cap threshold, anything the formula compares against) into the same imprecise range, since two operands both losing precision independently is a qualitatively different, worse failure than either one alone.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand — ran 30 arcs of power-scaling#E1's own compounding rate (5.5x/arc) applied to BOTH attacker power and defender maxHp simultaneously, comparing quantized damage from two mathematically-equivalent raw-damage expressions at every arc: identical through arc 24, diverging at arc 25 (difference 32768) and arc 30 (difference 134,217,728).

## E1 — combined loop combat damage must quantize to integer lattice
- Date: 2026-07-13
- Project: local/sandbox-combined-loop-combat
- Tags: [determinism][architecture][gamedev]
- What: Per `brain analyze seams`, composed `sandbox-combined-game-loop#E1`'s integer-lattice authority rule (a canonical serializer THROWS on any non-integer authoritative value) with `dbz-combat`/`power-scaling`'s damage formula (`damage = min(attackerPower * rate, defenderMaxHp * capFraction)`, verified correct in isolation using plain floats). Across 20 seeds x 15 casts each with a realistic power range (800-2800, incrementing by a random 0-49 between casts -- NOT constrained to multiples of the rate's denominator), 289/300 (96%) of computed damage values were non-integer, meaning the game-loop's own canonical serializer would throw on nearly every combat action the instant the two systems share authoritative state.
- Why built: Two systems each independently correct and independently tested with numbers that happened to keep results looking like nice round values -- to check whether combat's damage math actually respects a determinism constraint (integer-lattice authority) that combat's own single-system sandbox never had a reason to enforce, since it never had a fixed-timestep/fingerprint-replay consumer downstream.
- Design: `capDamage(rawDamage, defenderMaxHp, capFraction=0.2)` = `Math.min(rawDamage, defenderMaxHp*capFraction)`, `rawDamage = power * 0.05`. `canonicalSerialize(value, name)` mirrors game-loop#E1's own stated contract: throws if `!Number.isInteger(value)`.
- Got right: The cap logic itself (never exceed `capFraction` of defender max HP) is correct in both the broken and fixed variants -- this is purely a representation/quantization gap, not a logic error in the damage formula.
- Where/why it failed: `power * 0.05` and `defenderMaxHp * 0.2` are each individually reasonable float operations that combat's own sandbox never needed to round, because nothing downstream cared about the value's exact representation -- it only needed to compare and subtract correctly, which floats do fine. The moment that same value must be written into game-loop#E1's authoritative, fingerprinted state (so a replay or a cross-platform lockstep peer can bit-compare it), non-integer values are a hard failure, not a cosmetic one -- this is exactly the class of interaction-seam bug game-loop#E1 was written to warn about ("push everything float/feel/tolerance to a read-only presentation layer"), but combat's damage number is NOT presentation/feel -- it's the actual authoritative HP delta, so the fix can't be "move it to the presentation layer" the way rpg-progression#E3 did for animation; it has to be quantized IN PLACE.
- Fix: Quantize (floor) at the authoritative-state write boundary only -- `quantizeForAuthority(x) = Math.floor(x)` applied to the final `capDamage()` result right before subtracting from `dummyHp`, leaving all intermediate math (the multiplication, the cap comparison) as plain floats. Re-ran the identical 300-cast sweep: 0/300 throws, and separately confirmed the cap invariant (`damage <= capFraction * maxHp`) still holds in every case post-quantization (flooring only ever reduces a value, never pushes it past a cap it was already under).
- Why this fix: Quantizing only at the authoritative boundary (not throughout the combat math) keeps combat's own formula and tests untouched -- the fix is purely about where a game-loop-integrated build converts a float result into the integer the lattice requires, matching game-loop#E1's own stated mitigation pattern ("quantization of any unavoidable [non-integer] value") applied to ordinary rational arithmetic, not just its originally-named transcendentals (sqrt/sin/cos).
- Rule of thumb: Before composing ANY system that produces numeric AUTHORITATIVE output (damage, currency, stat deltas) with a determinism/replay/lockstep system that enforces an integer (or fixed-point) lattice, check every arithmetic path that writes to shared state for non-integer intermediate results -- a formula "verified correct" in an isolated sandbox was only checked for logical correctness (does the cap work, is the comparison right), never for representation compatibility with a consumer it never had. Quantize at the write boundary, not throughout the formula, to keep the fix minimal and the original system's own tests valid.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand -- ran a 20-seed x 15-cast sweep with a realistic (non-multiple-of-20) power range through the unmodified damage formula, counted 289/300 non-integer results and confirmed via a direct port of game-loop#E1's own stated serializer contract that all 289 would throw; re-ran identically with the floor-at-boundary fix applied, confirming 0/300 throws and 0/300 cap-invariant violations.
