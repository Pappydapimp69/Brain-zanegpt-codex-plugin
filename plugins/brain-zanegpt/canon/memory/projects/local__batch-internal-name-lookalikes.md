## E1 — invented names inside one batch lookalike each other and exact match misses them
- Date: 2026-10-16
- Project: local/batch-internal-name-lookalikes
- Tags: [comedy][game-ads][testing][sandbox]
- What: When every fake-ad video invents its own game name from 12 syllables, the names collide with each other inside one batch: at 30 videos of 3 syllables, 24% of batches held an exact duplicate but 100% held a pair one syllable apart (edit distance 2 or less), and at 2 syllables 96% held an exact duplicate.
- Why built: New-half train pick joining the every-video-gets-its-own-joke kernel, the fake-ad kernel source check and the invented-name check, whose lesson tested names against 17 real titles but not against the other names in the same batch.
- Design: Seeded simulation, 600 batches per setting, names drawn as 2, 3 or 4 syllables from a list of 12, batch sizes 8, 16 and 30, counting batches with an exact duplicate, a pair within edit distance 1, and a pair within edit distance 2 (one swapped syllable of 3 letters-pairs is at most 2 edits).
- Got right: The generator avoided real titles in the earlier lesson, and here longer names clearly helped: at 30 videos 4-syllable names dropped the lookalike-pair rate from 1.00 to 0.69 and the exact-duplicate rate from 0.24 to 0.01.
- Where/why it failed: I expected the exact-duplicate check to be the weak one only at 2 syllables; it already fails at 3 syllables because it flags 24% of batches while missing every batch whose names differ by one syllable, and with 12 syllables the lookalike count approaches certainty by batch size 16 (0.92).
- Fix: Check batch names against each other with an edit-distance screen and a closed syllable list that forbids two names sharing all but one syllable, or use 5 or more syllables, rather than relying on an exact-duplicate test.
- Why this fix: Invented names come from a small syllable pool, so near pairs follow from counting (30 names give 435 pairs), and an exact check only sees pairs that are fully identical.
- Rule of thumb: Screen invented names against each other in the batch, not only against real titles: 30 three-syllable names from 12 syllables had an exact duplicate in 24% of batches but a one-syllable-apart pair in 100%.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; the 12-syllable pool and batch sizes are my own assumptions and the lookalike threshold of 2 edits is a screen, not a judgement that humans see the names as the same.
