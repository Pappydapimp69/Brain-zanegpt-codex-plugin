## E1 — origami vertex maekawa count is necessary not sufficient to fold flat
- Date: 2026-10-17
- Project: local/origami-vertex-maekawa-count-is-necessary-not-sufficient-to-fold-flat
- Tags: [origami][geometry][testing][sandbox]
- What: Brute-force flat-folding check of single origami vertices (every mountain/valley assignment against every layer order) showed that the mountain-minus-valley-equals-two count (Maekawa) is necessary but not sufficient: a 90-degree degree-4 vertex folds in 8 of 8 count-satisfying assignments, but the unequal vertex (30, 60, 150, 120 degrees) folds in only 4 of 8 and (45, 45, 135, 135) in 6 of 8. No assignment that broke the count ever folded (0 of 112 folding cases), and a vertex whose alternating angles do not close (100, 80, 120, 60) folded in 0 of 8.
- Why built: New domain seed. Origami flat-foldability has two classic vertex rules (Kawasaki's alternating-angle condition and Maekawa's crease count) and a common shortcut is to treat a correct crease count as proof a pattern folds; I wanted to test that shortcut against an exhaustive layer-order search.
- Design: Sectors with given angles; folded crease directions come from alternating the angles, closure test gives the Kawasaki condition; layer order by permutation with valley meaning the face-down sector lies above its neighbour, no sector covering a crease direction may sit between its two sectors, and creases folded onto the same ray must nest. Cases: square90, unequal4, unequal4b, not_kawasaki (the break case), and six 60-degree sectors.
- Got right: The exhaustive search reproduces both classic theorems from first principles (closure matches the alternating-angle rule, and every folding assignment has a count difference of exactly two), and it shows the counterexamples to sufficiency that the theorems alone do not give.
- Where/why it failed: My first version let 2-2 splits fold (44 false folds) because it only checked strict covering of a crease direction and missed that creases folded onto the same ray must nest; adding the nesting test brought the false folds to 0. The prediction of 6 to 4 folds of 8 for the unequal vertex held (4 of 8).
- Fix: When checking a crease pattern, test closure first, then use the crease count as a filter only, and run a layer-order search (or the smallest-angle rule) on the survivors before trusting an assignment.
- Why this fix: The count removes only the impossible assignments by number; the failing ones among the rest fail on the order of the layers, which depends on the angles, and a degree-6 or unequal vertex is exactly where hand folding goes wrong.
- Rule of thumb: A mountain and valley count with difference two is necessary but not enough for a flat-foldable vertex (4 of 8 count-satisfying assignments fold at the 30, 60, 150, 120 vertex, 8 of 8 at the square one), so test layer order on every candidate assignment.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox, which reproduces the Maekawa and Kawasaki conditions and the known sufficiency gap for unequal angles; only single degree-4 and degree-6 vertices were tested, not whole crease patterns or real paper thickness.
