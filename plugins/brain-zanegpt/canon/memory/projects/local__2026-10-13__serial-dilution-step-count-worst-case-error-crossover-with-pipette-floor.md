## E1 — serial dilution step count worst case error crossover with pipette floor
- Date: 2026-10-13
- Project: local/2026-10-13__serial-dilution-step-count-worst-case-error-crossover-with-pipette-floor
- Tags: [lab-dilution][sandbox][numerics][model][measurement]
- What: A worst-case error model for making a 1000-fold dilution with a pipette whose relative error is max(0.5 percent, a/volume), comparing one 1000-fold step against two, three and six equal or unequal steps, each step ending at 1000 uL.
- Why built: Seeding a lab-dilution domain from a real bench scenario: the common advice is to dilute in several steps because a 1 uL transfer is imprecise, and I wanted to see whether more steps always means less error.
- Design: Per step the transfer volume is 1000 over the factor, error is the transfer error plus the diluent error scaled by the fraction of diluent; worst-case bounds add across steps; sweep the small-volume error constant a from 0 to 0.2 uL. The tolerance model is an assumption, not a vendor specification.
- Got right: At a=0.02 uL the single 1 uL step had 2.5 percent worst-case error against 1.95 percent for a 10 then 100 split, and the single step lost by 5.5 vs 1.95 at a=0.05 and 20.5 vs 3.45 at a=0.2; the crossover where three 1:10 steps first beat one step was a=0.024 uL.
- Where/why it failed: More steps is not monotonically better: with a=0 (a pure floor) one step won at 1.0 percent against 1.95, 2.85 and 5.05 for two, three and six steps, and six near-equal steps lost to every plan from a=0.02 up to 0.2 because each step adds its floor. The best plan moved from 2 steps (10 then 100) to 2 steps near the 32-fold balance as a grew (a=0.1: 1.97 vs 2.45).
- Fix: Choose the step count by computing the worst-case sum for the actual pipette's small-volume error, and balance the step factors so that no transfer falls in the region where a/volume exceeds the floor.
- Why this fix: Each extra step adds a fixed floor of error while shrinking the worst small transfer, so there is an interior optimum that depends on the pipette's own constant; only a computation shows where it sits.
- Rule of thumb: Two balanced steps beat one step once the smallest transfer's error exceeds the combined floor of the second step (crossover about a=0.024 uL here), and extra steps beyond two only add error; recompute with your own pipette constants before copying the split.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; the error model and its constants are assumptions and no real pipette or assay was measured. This domain has no adjacent kernel or lesson in Brain, so the entry is isolated and cites nothing.
- Composed: N/A
- Sandbox: sandboxes/2026-10-13__serial-dilution-step-count-worst-case-error-crossover-with-pipette-floor.py
- Prediction: At a=0.02 uL the 3x10 plan is no better than the single step (2.85 vs 2.5 percent); at a=0.05 the 3x10 plan wins; the crossover lies between 0.02 and 0.06 uL; with a=0 one step must win.
- Observed: RESULT: PASS floor-only best=1x1000; crossover a=0.024 uL where 3x10 beats 1x1000; a=0.2 best=2x32. Also found that two steps beat three and six at every a>=0.02 tested, which I had not predicted.
- Scenarios: 1) a=0 floor-only, built to BREAK the more-steps-is-better claim; 2) a=0.02; 3) a=0.05; 4) a=0.1; 5) a=0.2; 6) crossover sweep in 0.001 steps.
