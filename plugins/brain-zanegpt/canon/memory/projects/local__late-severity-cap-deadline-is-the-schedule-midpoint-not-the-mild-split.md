## E1 — late severity cap deadline is the schedule midpoint not the mild split
- Date: 2026-10-17
- Project: local/late-severity-cap-deadline-is-the-schedule-midpoint-not-the-mild-split
- Tags: [comedy][humor][testing][sandbox]
- What: Re-running the late severity cap test over schedule lengths 6, 9 and 12 and mild/severe splits 0.2, 0.3 and 0.4, the last cap-arrival beat that keeps the distance dial forgiving (7 or more of 11 distances within 95% of best) was 3 of 6, 4 of 9 and 6 of 12 for almost every split: about the midpoint of the schedule, not the beat where the severity crosses the mild split. Only two of nine cells fell within one beat of the split-crossing beat I predicted, and the single outlier was n 6, split 0.4, with 2.
- Why built: The late-cap lesson states its deadline as beat 3 of 6, which could be a function of the schedule length, the split, or both; I conjectured it follows the beat where severity leaves the mild zone.
- Design: Cover and laugh forms from the severity-cap lessons (mild beats prefer closeness 0.8 - 0.5 d, severe beats prefer distance 0.2 + 0.7 d, laugh falls off three times as fast past the cover), go-big severity i/n for the first L beats then a cap at 0.8 of the split scaled by i/n, 11 distances, 9 (n, split) cells, plus a break case with a cap level of 0.5 above the split.
- Got right: The earlier lesson's number is not a one-off: for n 6, 9 and 12 the deadline sits at 3, 4 and 6, so "state the cap by the middle of the set" is a usable form.
- Where/why it failed: My prediction failed on both counts: the deadline does not track the split (0.2, 0.3 and 0.4 give 3, 3, 2 at n 6 and the same 4 and 6 at n 9 and 12), and the break case did not break: with the cap level at 0.5, above every split, the window at L 0 was still 7 (forgiving), so a cap level above the mild zone was not enough to make the dial sharp in this scoring.
- Fix: State the severity cap before the middle of the escalation, and do not use the mild/severe split to fix the deadline; if the cap level is above the mild zone, test the dial directly.
- Why this fix: In this scoring the number of uncapped severe beats before the cap lands decides how many distances stay near the best, and that count grows with the beats, not with where the zone boundary sits.
- Rule of thumb: A severity cap keeps the distance dial forgiving only if it lands by about the middle of the escalation (beat 3 of 6, 4 of 9, 6 of 12), and that deadline did not move with the mild split in this toy model.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; the cover and laugh functions, the cap levels and the 95% window are assumed toy forms, not audience data.

- ZaneGPT verdict: weak — The n/2 pattern is consistent across nine cells, but the cells vary only n and split, so it is untested against the threshold, cap fraction and ramp shape that probably produce it, and the break case already contradicted the failed prediction's other half.
- Hidden assumption: The "midpoint" pattern is a real property of cap timing, but it may just be an artifact of the toy's coupled parameters: severity is i/n, the cap is 0.8·split·i/n, and the forgiveness threshold (7 of 11 within 95%) is fixed. Those make the crossover a function of n and the threshold alone, so the "deadline" reflects the design, not any general escalation behavior.
- Would prove it wrong: Move the threshold (e.g. 5 or 9 of 11, or within 90% or 98%) or the cap fraction (0.6 or 1.0 instead of 0.8), or use a non-linear severity ramp. If L* stays at n/2 only for the original settings and shifts with the others (or the lone n=6, split=0.4 cell stays at 2 while its neighbors stay at 3), the midpoint is a tuning artifact. Also, if n=15, 18 or 24 doesn't give 7, 9 or 12 (the n/2 rule), the rule fails to extrapolate.
- Break scenario: Hold n=12 and split=0.3 fixed, and sweep the cap level from 0.3·split to 1.5·split in small steps, with the forgiveness threshold at 6, 7 and 8 of 11. The rule says L* is about 6 regardless of cap level, so a cap-level sensitivity (L* sliding smoothly, or flipping from 0 to n) would refute it. Note the existing break case, which used a cap level of 0.5, is a single point, and its window of 7 (not the predicted 2 or fewer) already shows the "never forgiving" claim was wrong too.
