## E1 — pinhole compression is set by distance not focal length at fixed framing
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/sandbox-pinhole-depth-compression
- Tags: [film][camera][geometry][testing]
- What: An exact pinhole-projection model of the claim that a wide lens used close expands depth and a telephoto used far compresses it, with framing held constant. The background-to-subject image-size ratio is R = d/(d+delta), a function of camera-to-subject distance d and the subject-to-background gap delta only. Focal length never appears. At 50% subject height in a 24 mm frame and delta = 20 m, a 24 mm lens sits at 3.5 m (R 0.149) and a 200 mm lens at 29.2 m (R 0.593).
- Why built: The cinematography kernel says compression is set by distance paired with focal length, while the photography kernel says it is distance and never the lens, and a motion kernel says a zoom-in collapses the background but a dolly-in keeps perspective. Three kernels give three causal stories for one image, so a model had to say which variable does the work and where the lens does matter.
- Design: Pure-stdlib pinhole (x = f*X/Z), 24 mm frame height, 1.75 m subject, a same-size twin delta metres behind it. Nine checks: framing-held sweep over 8 focal lengths; same-distance focal sweep; crop-versus-optics comparison (wide lens at the tele position, rescaled, versus the tele image and versus a wide lens at the close position); the shot-size vocabulary ECU to ELS at 24 and 200 mm; a thin-lens blur comparison at fixed aperture; zoom versus dolly with the same 3x subject growth; a small-gap case; viewer-side depth inference by known size at three viewing distances and two screen widths; nose-to-ear magnification at face scale.
- Got right: Same distance, focal length 24 to 200 mm: R moves by 1e-16, only subject size changes. A 24 mm shot from the 200 mm camera position, rescaled, matches the 200 mm image to 5.6e-17, while a 24 mm shot from the close position is off by 0.467 subject-heights; the price of the crop is 8.33x linear resolution. A 3x zoom leaves R at 0.4286 and the background lateral offset at 0.735 subject-heights; a dolly-in to the same 3x subject size drops R to 0.2000 (2.14x) and the offset to 0.343. Viewer depth inference recovers exactly (d+delta)/d for any viewing distance and screen width. Nose-to-ear magnification is 1.200 at 0.5 m and 1.033 at 3 m. Each shot-size name (ECU, CU, MCU, MS, LS, ELS) fixes only the ratio f/d: the 200 mm camera sits exactly 8.33x farther than the 24 mm camera and R differs, e.g. MS 0.048 versus 0.294.
- Where/why it failed: Two of nine predictions were missed, both mine. P1 expected d = 3.6 m and R = 0.1525 and 0.600 because I did the arithmetic with a 1.8 m body; the model's 1.75 m gives 3.50 m, 29.17 m, R 0.1489 and 0.5932. P7 expected that with a person only 0.5 m from a wall the lens choice barely matters (R gap under 0.05); the gap is 0.108 (R 0.875 versus 0.983), and the gap passes 0.05 at a delta of only 0.22 m, because R is a ratio and a 0.5 m gap at 3.5 m is already 14% of the distance. Separately, the pinhole cannot see blur: with a thin lens at f/2.8 the background blur is 0.21% of frame height at 24 mm and 0.84% at 200 mm (3.98x), so the lens does change the look at fixed framing even though it does not change perspective. Real-lens distortion is not modelled and its size is not measured here.
- Fix: State the causal chain as framing fixes f/d, the shooter picks d, and d (with the subject-to-background gap) alone sets compression; "wide-close" and "tele-far" are the two common ways of choosing d, not an effect of the glass. When describing a look, name the distance or the gap-to-distance ratio, and treat shot-size names as silent about perspective. For a pure zoom in place, expect no change in relative sizes; for a dolly, expect a change.
- Why this fix: Every figure above follows from one line of geometry and was reproduced, and the crop test shows the equivalence directly: the same position gives the same image at any focal length up to a rescale. Naming d removes the apparent conflict between the two optics kernels (they describe the same chain from different ends) and the motion-camera kernel's wording, in which a zoom compresses the background while a dolly keeps perspective, is reversed under this model.
- Rule of thumb: For the same framing, compression is decided by camera distance relative to the subject-to-background gap, not by the lens: swapping focal length at one position only crops (the pinhole image is identical up to scale), so choose or describe the shot by distance, and remember a lens can still change blur (about 4x at 200 mm versus 24 mm at f/2.8 and the same framing).
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand: every number above is the output of the saved pinhole and thin-lens sandbox, run once; the pinhole figures are exact consequences of the model and the blur figure assumes a plain thin lens with a fixed f/2.8. Out of scope for this run: distortion, sensor size changes, subject depth other than the nose-and-ear case, and any perceptual study of what viewers report.
