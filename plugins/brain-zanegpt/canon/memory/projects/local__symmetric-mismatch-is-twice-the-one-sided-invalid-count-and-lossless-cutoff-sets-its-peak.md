## E1 — symmetric mismatch is twice the one sided invalid count and lossless cutoff sets its peak
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Project: local/symmetric-mismatch-is-twice-the-one-sided-invalid-count-and-lossless-cutoff-sets-its-peak

- What: For a legality wave t mod P under W, the symmetric mismatch count between a tick and the tick L later is exactly twice the one-sided invalid count, and the one-sided count equals (N/P) times min(L, W, P-L, P-W), in all 330 cells covering periods 10, 12 and 15, every width from 2 to P-2 (wide windows included) and lags 1 to P-1; lags above the period repeat the value for L mod P and multiples of P give 0.
- Why built: Train pick joining the lossless-at-largest-lag lesson, the gate-only worst-cutoff formula and the symmetric-versus-one-sided shared min term, to settle whether the shared min term is exact for wide windows and for lags above the period, which my earlier pass left open.
- Design: Brute-force counts over N = 8P ticks: one-sided is legal now and illegal after L, symmetric is any change of legality; compared with the closed formula over the whole grid, then lags 13, 14, 20, 24, 25 and 36 on period 12 width 5 against the value at L mod P.
- Got right: Zero mismatches in either identity over 330 cells including W above P/2, and the above-period lags matched L mod P (8, 16, 32, 0, 8, 0 on P12 W5, N 96).
- Where/why it failed: Nothing in the prediction failed; the symmetric factor of two is expected for whole periods, since legal-to-illegal and illegal-to-legal transitions must balance, so the value here is the confirmation of scope (wide windows, lags above the period), not a new mechanism. Horizons that are not whole periods were not tested here.
- Fix: Use the one-sided formula for stale-hint counts at any lag by reducing L mod P first, and read a symmetric mismatch as twice it, only when the horizon is a whole number of periods.
- Why this fix: Reduction by L mod P follows because the wave repeats with period P, and the factor two follows from transitions balancing over a full cycle.
- Rule of thumb: Over whole periods the symmetric mismatch is twice the one-sided count and both follow min(L, W, P-L, P-W) of L mod P, for narrow and wide windows alike; a lag that is a multiple of the period costs nothing.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox in this session on three periods and all widths 2 to P-2; horizons that are partial periods were not run here.

- Hidden assumption: The wave is a single, exactly periodic square window over a whole number of periods (N = 8P, no partial period), with integer P, W and L. Under that assumption the claim is cyclic-interval geometry, not an empirical finding. The 330-cell pass confirms an identity that can be proved directly, so it says little about waves that aren't periodic or ticks that don't fill whole periods.
- Would prove it wrong: One cell where symmetric ≠ 2×one-sided or one-sided ≠ (N/P)·min(L, W, P-L, P-W) under the same N = 8P setup. Equally, an above-period lag where the count differs from the value at L mod P. None appeared in the 330 cells or the six above-period lags.
- Break scenario: Keep P=12 and W=5, but set N to a non-multiple of P (for example N=100) and use lag L=7. The edge ticks break the symmetric = 2×one-sided balance and the N/P factor, because N/P is no longer an integer. Variants that also break it are a jittered or drifting period, two windows per period, or a lag measured in real time rather than integer ticks. The listed BREAK (lags 13–36) can't defeat it, because periodicity guarantees the L mod P repeat.
