## E1 — strip test speech features fail on short lines
- Date: 2026-09-30
- Project: local/sandbox-strip-test-speech-features-fail-on-short-lines
- Tags: [dialogue][speech][testing]
- What: Speech-pattern features (subject ellipsis, general extenders, contractions) let a nearest-centroid attributor pass the strip test perfectly when two characters really differ (10 of 10), give no signal at all for two characters written in one voice (10 of 10 ties), and on short clipped lines default to the plain speaker instead of tying.
- Why built: Compose the voice-differentiation strip test with the ellipsis and general-extender kernels, to test whether those speech patterns are enough to tell speakers apart once tags are removed.
- Design: Two hand-written characters of 10 lines each (A full sentences, B elided openings and extenders), trained on 5 lines each and tested on the other 5. A second pair written in one shared style, and a third test of 10 lines of 3 words or fewer. Three features per line: elided opening, extender present, contraction rate.
- Got right: With distinct speech patterns the attributor got 10 of 10, well above the predicted 8.
- Where/why it failed: For the author-voice pair I predicted chance (4 to 6 of 10); every line tied instead, because both characters' features are all zero. For short lines I predicted ties; instead they went to A, whose centroid is zero, giving 6 of 10 only by defaulting to the plain speaker.
- Fix: Treat a featureless line as unattributable rather than assigning it to the character with the plainest style, and add features beyond speech patterns (topic, metaphor family, what the speaker avoids) before running a strip test on clipped dialogue.
- Why this fix: An absent speech pattern is evidence only when the other speaker reliably shows it, and on short lines neither can; the strip-test kernel already names metaphor family and avoided topics as signals that survive short lines.
- Rule of thumb: Speech patterns pass a strip test only when characters really differ (10 of 10); one-voice pairs give no signal (all ties) and short clipped lines just default to the plainer speaker, so treat featureless lines as unattributable.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; counts are copied from its output. The characters and lines are hand-written; results would change with other feature sets.
