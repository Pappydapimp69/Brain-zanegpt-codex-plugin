## E1 — tone escalation factual drift toy model
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/proj
- Tags: [persuasion][conversation][verification][sandbox][model]
- What: A deterministic toy MODEL (not a measurement of any real system) of a 24-turn tone-escalation loop over a 40-claim document in 8 sections of 5, where each tone step leaks into claim strength (susceptibility 0.3 to 1.0, leak 0.15) and a claim built on a distorted earlier claim inherits part of its excess. It reproduces the drift half of the long-conversation-drift kernel and the value of a same-turn fact check, but refutes the kernel's cost-of-delay argument when cost is counted as edits.
- Why built: Train pass on the untested kernel claiming that chasing a tonal target produces incidental factual drift and should be corrected in the turn that introduces it. The kernel rests on one anonymized first-hand observation, so a model was needed to see which parts follow from the stated mechanism and where they stop.
- Design: Excess over source strength e_i per claim; distorted when abs(e_i) exceeds 1.0. Policies: none; sweep every turn (a "split" request: full tone step plus a separate fact clause); delayed sweep every 8 turns; local check of one complained-about section; averaged (tone step halved, no fact check). Controls: leak 0, flat tone, oscillating tone, and a break case with 25 percent of claims that have no checkable source. Edits count each reset plus each claim rewritten because it was built on a distorted parent. No RNG beyond a fixed-seed generator for susceptibility.
- Got right: Without checks the distorted fraction went 0.00 at turn 6 to 1.00 at turn 24 with mean excess rising every turn (634 distorted claim-turns). A per-turn sweep left 0.00 distorted after every check at full tone (24 of 24), while a delayed-8 sweep let 0.925 of claims be distorted before its check and left 158 claim-turns standing. Checking only the complained-about section kept it clean (max excess 0.94) while the other sections ended at 1.00 exactly as with no check (558 claim-turns). Halving the tone step (averaged) delivered half the tone (12 of 24) and still ended with 0.90 distorted. All three controls gave 0 drift.
- Where/why it failed: The predicted unwinding cost did not appear. I predicted delayed-8 edits would be at least 1.3 times per-turn edits because rewrites cascade through dependent claims; observed 91 versus 99 edits (ratio 0.92), and 56 versus 74 without dependencies (0.76). Per-turn checking re-fixes the same claims each time they drift back past tolerance, so it does more edits, not fewer. The kernel's claim that late correction costs more is therefore not supported by edit count in this model; the delay cost shows up as exposure (distorted claims standing between checks), which I added as a post-hoc metric after the failure. The 25 percent uncheckable break case left 0.25 distorted even with a per-turn sweep. Coefficients are my own choices, so the magnitudes mean little; only the orderings are the finding.
- Fix: Keep the kernel's rule but restate its reason: check the claims touched by a tone step inside the same turn because that holds exposure at zero, not because it is cheaper in edits (it was not here). Run the fact check over the whole document each time (the sibling whole-artifact lesson, exercised as sweep versus local), and split the tone clause from the fact clause rather than softening the tone (the sibling bundled-request lesson, exercised as split versus averaged).
- Why this fix: Sweep versus delayed differed by 0.925 versus 0.0 peak visible drift at equal tone, local checking left the other 35 claims untouched, and averaging lost half the tone and still drifted, so only a same-turn whole-document fact clause removed the exposure without sacrificing the target.
- Rule of thumb: In this toy model, a tone-escalation loop that leaks into claim strength drifts every unchecked claim, a same-turn whole-document fact check holds exposure at zero at full tone, and checking only the flagged section or softening the tone does not; but late correction was cheaper in edits than per-turn correction (0.92 and 0.76), so justify same-turn checks by exposure, not edit cost, and treat claims with no checkable source as the residual risk.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand: the sandbox ran twice with byte-identical output (same md5) and printed every number quoted here; 9 of 10 predicted items held and the RESULT line is FAIL because of the unwinding item. The leak, inheritance and tolerance values are my own choices; the entry cites only the three kernels the run exercised.
