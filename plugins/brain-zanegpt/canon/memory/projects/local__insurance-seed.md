## E1 — insurance pooling removes independent risk not correlated risk
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/insurance-seed
- Tags: [insurance][risk-pooling][correlation][simulation]
- What: Seeds the new INSURANCE domain. A toy loss pool (2% yearly claim chance, 10,000 claim, premium = expected loss + 30%, no capital) was solved with exact binomial arithmetic. Independent claims make ruin vanish as members are added (0.32 at n=100, 0.000017 at n=10,000), but a shared 2%-a-year catastrophe pins ruin at 0.0200 from n=3,000 on, and the obvious fix of splitting the pool into a few independent catastrophe zones made ruin worse, not better.
- Why built: To test the seed kernel that pooling removes only independent risk, and to find the cheapest repair for the correlated case before anyone relies on "more members" or "a few regions" as a safety argument.
- Design: Pure-stdlib sandbox; ruin = total claims above total premium. Independent pool = Binomial(n, 0.02). Correlated pool = mixture: with chance 0.02 every policy claims at 0.30, otherwise at 0.0143 (marginal stays 2%, pairwise rho = 0.082). Zone version = exact dynamic programme over m independent zones with truncation at the ruin threshold. A fixed-seed Monte Carlo cross-check (0.3307 vs exact 0.3324 at n=20) validates the calculator; the sd-of-mean formula sigma*sqrt(rho + (1-rho)/n) matched the exact mixture moments to 1e-9. A log-utility buyer and a deductible-versus-reserve table were added to exercise the two sibling kernels.
- Got right: Independent ruin fell monotonically and was below 1e-3 at n=10,000 (1.7e-5); correlated ruin was 0.0200 for n>=3,000 (1,162x the independent figure at n=10,000). The 1-in-100 capital was 1.17x the mean claim rate (independent) versus 15.0x (correlated, every n>=1,000). The break scenario held: with a 0.5% catastrophe the 99% capital looks safe at 1.11x yet ruin is still 0.0050. A log-utility buyer with wealth 20,000 pays up to 275.3 against a loaded 260; one with 1,000,000 pays 201.0 and declines. With a 4,000 reserve the largest fitting deductible tested was 2,500, saving 25% of premium.
- Where/why it failed: Prediction P5, written before the first run, said splitting 2,000 policies into 100 independent catastrophe zones would cut ruin at least 10x versus one zone. Observed: ruin rose from 0.020 (1 zone) to 0.183 (10 zones) to 0.112 (100 zones). Ruin is the chance that enough zones are hit together to push claims over the loaded premium, and the number of zones needed grows more slowly than the chance that at least one is hit. Re-run afterwards at n=10,000 (post-hoc): ruin 0.020 / 0.096 / 0.183 / about 0.32 at 1 / 5 / 10 / 20 zones, falling back below the single-zone figure only past about 100 zones (0.088) and reaching 0.0010 at 1,000 zones, still about 60x the independent pool because each zone stays internally correlated.
- Fix: Treat the catastrophe year as its own line item: hold capital or reinsurance sized to the worst shared event (about 15x the mean claim rate in this model), and only count diversification once the number of independent hazards is large enough that the required number of simultaneous hits is far above the expected number (here the single-zone figure is only beaten past roughly 100 zones). Check the zone count with the exact tail P(hits >= needed) before trusting it.
- Why this fix: Capital against the shared event is the only lever that did not depend on a count threshold; the zone result shows spreading is non-monotone, so a pool that spreads thinly across a handful of hazards sits at the worst point of the curve and can feel safer than it is.
- Rule of thumb: Pooling shrinks only independent loss; the shared-event year sets the floor. Do not assume that a few independent regions repair it: in this model 5 to 50 zones were worse than one, and a 99% capital target is blind to any catastrophe rarer than 1%. Size reserves to the worst shared event, then count zones against the exact hit tail.
