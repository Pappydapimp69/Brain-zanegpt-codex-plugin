## E1 — exact zero voice termination becomes a permanent phantom eating realtime budget
- Date: 2026-07-20
- Project: local/sandbox-phantom-voice-eats-budget
- Tags: [dsp][realtime][testing]
- Composed: local__sandbox-brain-loop1-denormal-swept/E1, local__sandbox-combined-audio-realtime/E1, local__sandbox-combined-audio-realtime/E2
- What: `brain train` flagged `sandbox-brain-loop1-denormal-swept + sandbox-combined-audio-realtime` as a single-source edge to re-verify. The existing composed claim there (the subnormal-fixed-point mechanism is domain-independent) is already independently confirmed 5 times over (audio-realtime#E2, bloom-audio#E5, dog#E33, denormal-swept#E1, denormal-swept#E2) — re-testing THAT again would duplicate existing work. Instead composed audio-realtime#E1's own subject (a realtime callback's per-frame drain budget must be bounded against a bursty producer) with the fixed-point mechanism (audio-realtime#E2) from an unexplored angle: a decaying voice terminated by an EXACT-ZERO check never actually terminates, so it becomes a permanently-alive phantom pool entry that silently consumes one unit of the bounded per-callback budget forever — a SECOND, independent mechanism (bad termination logic, not a bursty producer) that threatens the exact same budget invariant E1 names, and compounds with a real burst rather than being separate from it.
- Why built: audio-realtime#E1 and #E2 were each recorded from investigations of a single mechanism (bounded drain vs. decay convergence) with no scenario in scope where a badly-terminated decaying voice sits inside the SAME bounded-drain pool E1 describes. The fixed-point mechanism is well-established in isolation; whether it interacts with a realtime callback's own budget invariant specifically (not just "does the value ever hit exact zero") was untested.
- Design: Deterministic Node script (`sandbox_phantom_voice_eats_budget.mjs`), no RNG, no wall-clock. `makeVoicePool(terminationCheck)` holds a list of `{gain}` voices; `runCallback(decay)` mirrors audio-realtime#E1's bounded-drain shape exactly — processes at most `BUDGET_PER_CALLBACK=32` voices per call, decaying each (`Math.fround(gain*decay)`, float32) and removing any for which `terminationCheck(gain)` is true. Three scenarios: (A) exact-zero termination (`gain===0`, the buggy check) on one long-lived voice across 1000 callbacks; (B) control — epsilon-threshold termination (`Math.abs(gain)<1e-5`, the fix) on the same setup; (C) the phantom (scenario A's stuck voice) sharing a pool with a genuine 40-voice burst arriving in one callback, checking how much of the 32-slot budget the burst can actually use.
- Got right: Scenario B (control) confirms the pool mechanism itself is correct — with the epsilon-threshold fix, the voice properly frees itself and the phantom-callback counter stays at 0 across the same 1000-callback run, isolating the failure to specifically the exact-zero check, not the pool/budget logic.
- Where/why it failed: Scenario A: the voice was still occupying the pool after all 1000 callbacks (`activeVoices=1`), and from callback 201 onward it consumed one budget unit on every single subsequent callback (799/799, the entire remaining window) with zero chance of ever freeing itself — matching the fixed-point finding exactly (a `gain===0` check can never trip once `gain` has stabilized at a nonzero subnormal). Scenario C: with the phantom already resident, a 40-voice burst arriving in one callback could only get 31 of its 40 voices serviced that callback (not 32), because the phantom silently occupied one of the 32 budget slots — the phantom's tax and the burst's own pressure compound arithmetically, exactly one-for-one, not independently.
- Fix: Same fix as audio-realtime#E2 (epsilon-threshold termination, never exact-zero equality) — now with a concretely demonstrated SECOND consequence beyond "the loop technically never converges": in a realtime system with a bounded per-callback work budget, an incorrectly-terminated decay entry doesn't just waste CPU on itself, it silently REDUCES the effective budget available to every other legitimate consumer of that same bounded resource, one-for-one, for as long as it stays stuck (which, per the fixed-point finding, is forever).
- Why this fix: audio-realtime#E1's bounded-drain design assumes the budget's occupants are all genuinely transient work; a phantom entry violates that assumption silently, because the pool's OWN bookkeeping (count of active voices) still looks bounded and reasonable — the leak isn't in list size, it's in one slot being permanently unavailable to real work, which no simple "how many voices are there" health check would catch without also checking whether any of them have been resident far longer than physically plausible for a decaying signal.
- Rule of thumb: In any realtime system with a bounded per-callback work budget (a drain loop, an event queue, a scheduler), an incorrectly-terminated entry (exact-zero equality on anything that decays, or any other "done" check that can silently never trip) isn't just wasted work on that one entry — it's a permanent tax on the SHARED budget, reducing headroom for every other consumer by exactly one unit per stuck entry, indefinitely. When auditing a bounded-budget system for overrun risk, check not just "is the producer bursty" (audio-realtime#E1's original concern) but also "can any entry get permanently stuck occupying a budget slot" — both are independent, compounding threats to the same invariant.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand — `sandbox_phantom_voice_eats_budget.mjs` run to completion for all three scenarios, deterministic (no RNG, no wall-clock, fixed callback-count loops, `Math.fround` for float32 semantics matching the original fixed-point finding), each scenario's active-voice count, phantom-callback tally, and burst-servicing count printed and checked programmatically (an `allPass` gate across all three; exits 1 and prints "CLAIM DID NOT REPRODUCE — do not file" if any scenario disagreed with the prediction — none did).
