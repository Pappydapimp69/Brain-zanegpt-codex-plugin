## E4 — sandbox combined gen6 urgent override inside capture lock
- Date: 2026-07-12
- Project: local/sandbox-combined-integration
- Tags: [ai][gamedev][algorithm]
- What: Generation 6 layered an RPG ki-economy (flee costs ki/step, a "charge" action regens ki, ki-starvation can strand an urgent decision) onto the Gen1-5 baseline that already carries two separately-verified lessons: E1's urgent-override bypass (any action whose raw, pre-hysteresis score >= 0.98 is chosen immediately) and E3's pursuit-evasion finding (once distance <= 1, an equal-speed reactive pursuer's next A* step lands on the evader's pre-move cell almost certainly — escape is already ~impossible). Direct instrumentation of the Gen6 sandbox (20 seeds x 300 ticks) shows the urgent override does fire for `flee` — 5374 of 6000 ticks, entirely legitimate, not stolen by `explore` (which only claims urgent 350 times, and only in the pre-crisis window, `raw~0.999` at tick 0 while the map is still almost entirely unexplored) — yet flee was never the *executed* action before first engagement in any of the 20 seeds (`seeds where NPC fled at least once BEFORE first engagement: 0/20`).
- Why built: To check whether a fix already verified in isolation (E1's urgent-override, confirmed in Gen4/5 to correctly restore flee's ability to fire despite hysteresis) still does what it was built for — provide the NPC a chance to escape — once a third, independently-developed system (the ki-economy) is composed on top, since neither E1 nor E3 was ever tested together with a third layer before.
- Design: `flee`'s raw consideration is `min(1, max(0, 1 - distance/12))`. Solving `1 - d/12 >= 0.98` (the urgent threshold) gives `d <= 0.24`. E3 independently established the capture-lock radius as `d <= 1` (the tick at which the pursuer's greedy A* step becomes reliably unavoidable, verified via 0/20-seed escape rate and per-seed streak tracing). `0.24 < 1`, so `{d : d <= 0.24}` is a strict subset of `{d : d <= 1}` — flee's urgent-fire zone is entirely nested inside E3's already-unescapable zone, by construction of the two formulas, not by chance of any particular seed.
- Got right: The urgent-override mechanism itself works exactly as E1 designed it — it does fire for flee, at the mathematically-predicted distance, without being silently out-competed or stolen by another action's saturated raw score (explore's near-1.0 early raw was the first, wrong hypothesis for why flee "never seems to help"; direct per-action urgent-fire counting ruled it out cleanly: explore's 350 urgent fires are confined to the pre-crisis window, disjoint in time from flee's 5374).
- Where/why it failed: E1's fix and E3's diagnosis were each verified correct in their own generation, against their own target metric (E1: does the override fire and select flee — yes; E3: is d<=1 actually unescapable — yes, 0/20 escapes). Composing them exposes a relationship neither single-system test could see: the override's own saturation point is a *function of the same raw formula* used to drive flee's urgency, and that formula's 0.98 threshold happens to land numerically inside the radius E3 already proved is a point of no return. The override does not need to be "beaten" by hysteresis (E1's original bug) to be functionally useless here — it can fire perfectly correctly and still arrive too late, because "correctly" was only ever verified as "fires and is chosen," never as "fires while there is still time left to act on it."
- Fix: Not implemented in this sandbox (out of scope for a decision-layer investigation, matching E3's own scope boundary). The portable diagnosis: an urgent-override's saturation threshold must be checked against any other verified terminal/lock-in radius that shares its underlying metric — either widen the raw formula so saturation occurs at a distance safely outside the capture-lock radius (e.g. make flee urgent at d<=2 or d<=3, not d<=0.24), or drive urgency from a signal that isn't purely instantaneous distance (rate-of-closure / predicted-ticks-to-capture), since a static distance-only formula can saturate arbitrarily close to a lock-in boundary discovered in a completely different investigation.
- Why this fix: A saturation threshold picked in isolation (E1 chose 0.98 to solve a hysteresis-suppression bug, with no reference to pursuit-evasion geometry) has no guarantee of leaving any actionable margin once a downstream terminal condition is later discovered elsewhere — the two numbers were never designed against each other, they only happen to interact once both systems are running in the same sandbox. Checking the relationship directly (solve both inequalities, compare the two distance thresholds) is cheap and conclusive; re-running the sweep only confirms what the algebra already guarantees.
- Rule of thumb: When composing an urgent-override / interrupt threshold (any "score >= X fires immediately" rule) with a separately-verified terminal or lock-in condition that shares the same underlying metric (here: Euclidean distance), solve for the exact value at which the override saturates and compare it directly against the terminal condition's boundary — do not assume "the override fires" means "the override was useful." A mechanism that is individually correct (fires when it should, isn't stolen by a competing action) can still be functionally inert if its trigger point is nested inside a boundary a different, independently-true lesson already declared a point of no return.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand — instrumented Gen6 to count urgent-fires per action (`explore:350, flee:5374, charge:0`) across the full 20-seed x 300-tick sweep, confirmed explore's urgent fires are confined to early low-visited-fraction ticks, confirmed via a separate counter that flee was never the executed action before first engagement in any of the 20 seeds, and independently solved both raw-formula inequalities (`d<=0.24` for flee's urgent threshold vs. E3's already-verified `d<=1` capture-lock radius) to confirm the nesting is a formula property, not a sampling artifact of these particular 20 seeds.

## E3 — sandbox combined integration speed parity dominates flee ai
- Date: 2026-07-11
- Project: local/sandbox-combined-integration
- Tags: [ai][gamedev][algorithm]
- What: Across three successive fix attempts at the sandbox's flee-AI catch-rate problem — Gen 3 (baseline, diagnosed as sub-goal flicker), Gen 4 (added a margin-gated "sticky" flee-target to stop the flee-goal cell from flickering tick-to-tick), and Gen 5 (added an urgent-override to bypass hysteresis and let flee engage instantly at maximal urgency) — the total `caughtCount` across the identical 20-seed x 300-tick sweep came back EXACTLY 4488 in all three variants, byte-for-byte identical, despite `fleeGoalFlickerCount` genuinely dropping (2379 -> 998 -> 1884 with overrides re-adding some churn) and `urgentOverrides` genuinely firing (72 times). Every decision-layer fix that measurably changed its own target metric left the actual outcome (time spent caught) completely unchanged.
- Why built: Gen 3 found a real, novel sub-goal-thrashing bug and Gen 4's fix for it was assumed to be the resolution; re-measuring the actual outcome metric (not just the flicker proxy) after each fix was the only reason this was caught rather than being reported as "fixed."
- Design: The threat pursues via its OWN A* call toward the NPC's current position each tick, consuming `result.path[0]` (one grid-step, since A* corner-cutting/weighted-heuristic behavior does not change how many cells are traversed per tick); the NPC's flee/explore/rest movement likewise consumes one A* path-step per tick. Both agents therefore have an identical, fixed per-tick movement budget (1 grid-step, diagonal costing the same single "step" as orthogonal) regardless of any weight/heuristic/goal-selection tuning.
- Got right: A direct per-seed trace (not just aggregate totals) confirmed `episodes=1` and `maxStreak`~93% of remaining ticks for every seed that had any capture at all — capture is not many short catch-and-lose cycles being masked by averaging, it is a SINGLE capture event after which the pursuer and evader stay glued together (or oscillate distance 0<->1) for effectively the rest of the simulation, in both the sticky-subgoal and non-sticky variants identically.
- Where/why it failed: Once distance between a reactive greedy pursuer and an equal-speed evader closes to 1 grid cell, the pursuer's next A* step (toward the evader's position at the START of that tick, before the evader has moved) lands exactly on the evader's old cell — this triggers "caught" regardless of which direction the evader chooses to flee, and because both agents move exactly one path-step per tick with no speed differential, the evader's flee step cannot open a gap the pursuer's next single greedy step won't immediately re-close in an open area. All three fix attempts operated purely on WHICH cell the evader picks to flee toward (Gen4) or WHEN it starts fleeing (Gen5) — neither changes HOW FAR each agent moves per tick, so neither could touch the actual mechanism causing the near-permanent capture lock.
- Fix: Not implemented in this sandbox (out of scope for a decision-layer investigation) — the verified, portable diagnosis is that this class of problem requires a MECHANICS-layer fix (give the evader a real per-tick speed/distance advantage over the pursuer, e.g. a dash/sprint budget, a slower pursuer tick-rate, or a minimum-separation teleport/blink escape), not further tuning of goal-selection or action-switching logic.
- Why this fix: This is the standard cop-and-robber / pursuit-evasion graph-theory result showing up empirically: with equal per-turn movement budgets and a pursuer that always has current information about the evader's position, the pursuer can generally maintain or close distance regardless of the evader's strategy quality in an open (non-maze-like) area — decision-layer intelligence only matters once there is an actual speed or information asymmetry for it to exploit.
- Rule of thumb: When debugging a "the AI keeps getting caught / can't escape" complaint, measure the actual outcome metric (total time-in-caught-state, capture-episode count) before and after each decision-layer fix, not just the proxy metric the fix targets (flicker count, action-switch count) — a fix that visibly improves its own target metric can leave the real-world outcome completely unchanged if the true bottleneck is a step-budget/speed parity between agents, a mechanics-layer property no amount of goal-selection or hysteresis tuning can touch. Check relative movement speed FIRST, before tuning flee-target selection or urgency thresholds.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand — ran the identical 20-seed x 300-tick sweep across three separate implementations (Gen 3 baseline, Gen 4 sticky-subgoal, Gen 5 urgent-override) in the same Node.js sandbox, comparing exact aggregate and per-seed `caughtCount`/`episodes`/`maxStreak` values, and traced the tick-by-tick position/distance sequence around a real capture event to confirm the mechanism (threat's A* step landing exactly on the NPC's pre-move position when distance=1, every tick, regardless of the NPC's chosen flee direction).

## E2 — sandbox combined integration path vs greedy movement
- Date: 2026-07-11
- Project: local/sandbox-combined-integration
- Tags: [algorithm][gamedev][state]
- What: A combined integration sandbox deliberately composed four already-proven-correct memory/idea lessons (CA-cave-gen + connectivity fixup, corner-cutting-safe weighted A*, canonical state-hash determinism, utility-AI with hysteresis) into one NPC simulation (explore/flee/rest on a generated cave). A* was called correctly and always found a valid path (confirmed: 0 "unreachable" events across 20 seeds, 300 ticks each), but the caller derived the NPC's actual per-tick movement from an INDEPENDENT greedy heuristic (`Math.sign(goal.x-npc.x)`, trying 3 candidate cells) instead of consuming A*'s own first path waypoint. Result: 478 "stuck" events (no valid greedy step existed) across 20 seeds, despite A* proving a real path existed every single time.
- Why built: To hunt for integration-level bugs that isolated single-system sandboxes (the CA-gen sandbox, the pathfinding sandboxes, the NPC-SIM utility-AI research) cannot see by construction, since each tested its own component in isolation with a trivial/synthetic caller.
- Design: `astar()` originally returned only `{found, expansions, pathLen, gCost}` — no actual path array — so the calling simulation code, needing SOME direction to step in, wrote its own ad-hoc greedy movement (compare goal.x/y to npc.x/y, try the diagonal step then each orthogonal fallback, with its own separate corner-cut guard).
- Got right: The corner-cutting guard was correctly duplicated into the greedy fallback (avoiding a repeat of the already-known corner-cutting lesson), and the underlying A* implementation itself was never at fault — direct diagnostic repro (`start=(19,6) goal=(33,17)`, A* pathLen=22, a real path existed) confirmed the greedy heuristic's three candidate steps were ALL structurally invalid at that position even though the real A*-discovered path routed through a corridor the greedy heuristic could never find, since greedy movement only ever considers direct-line-toward-goal candidates, not the actual shortest-path topology.
- Where/why it failed: Computing a correct path and then discarding it in favor of a cheaper "just walk toward the goal" heuristic is a classic false economy — it looks like a harmless simplification (goal.x/y are already known, so why bother storing/reading the path?) but silently reintroduces exactly the pathfinding failures A* was built to solve, in any map topology where the direct-line direction is blocked and the real route requires a detour A* found but the greedy heuristic structurally cannot represent (it only ever tries 3 candidate cells per tick, all biased toward the straight-line direction).
- Fix: Changed `astar()`'s return contract to include the actual reconstructed `path` array (walking `cameFrom` from goal back to start, then reversing), and changed the simulation to consume `result.path[0]` as the next move directly, deleting the greedy-heuristic movement code entirely.
- Why this fix: The pathfinder already computes the correct route as a side effect of finding `gScore[goal]` — reconstructing and returning it costs O(path length) extra work, negligible next to the search itself, and eliminates an entire class of "path exists but movement can't find it" bugs by construction, rather than trying to make the greedy fallback smarter.
- Rule of thumb: If a pathfinding function proves a path exists, the caller must consume THAT path, not re-derive movement direction from the goal coordinates via a separate heuristic — any "cheaper" caller-side movement heuristic that ignores the already-computed path can silently strand an agent in exactly the corridor/obstacle cases pathfinding exists to solve, and this failure is invisible to unit tests of the pathfinder itself (which only checks "found: true/false"), since it only manifests through the caller's integration code.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand — ran the actual combined Node.js sandbox across 20 seeds x 300 ticks, counted the 478 stuck events directly, isolated and reproduced the exact failing case with a standalone diagnostic script, applied the fix, and re-ran the identical 20-seed sweep confirming 0 stuck events post-fix with determinism preserved (0/20 hash mismatches, re-running each seed twice).

## E1 — sandbox combined integration hysteresis defeats saturated veto
- Date: 2026-07-11
- Project: local/sandbox-combined-integration
- Tags: [ai][gamedev][state]
- What: The same combined sandbox composed two independently-verified NPC-SIM utility-AI ideas — multiplicative consideration combination (to preserve veto power) and a flat additive hysteresis bonus (to prevent action-thrashing) — into one `pickAction()`. On a large mostly-unexplored 40x40 cave, direct tick-by-tick tracing at the moment a pursuing threat became CO-LOCATED with the NPC (flee utility = 1.0, the theoretical maximum) showed the NPC's action stayed `explore` (scores: flee=1.0, explore=1.136) instead of switching to `flee`, because `explore`'s own raw utility was already 0.986 (little of the map was explored yet) and the +0.15 hysteresis bonus pushed it past flee's absolute ceiling.
- Why built: To check whether two individually-correct, individually-sandbox-verified decision-layer techniques from the same domain remain correct when composed, since neither prior sandbox tested them together.
- Design: `pickAction(considerations, currentAction, hysteresisBonus)` — each action's score is the product of its considerations (multiplicative, so any single zero-scoring consideration vetoes it), then `hysteresisBonus` is added ONLY to whichever action equals `currentAction`, before taking the max.
- Got right: The multiplicative-combination property held exactly as designed — a zero-scoring consideration does veto an action (confirmed separately in this sandbox: `restUtility=0` correctly zeroed `rest`'s score even with the hysteresis bonus applied). The bug is not in multiplicative combination itself.
- Where/why it failed: Multiplicative combination guarantees a ZERO-scoring consideration vetoes an action; it says nothing about a MAXIMAL (1.0) consideration losing to a merely-high competitor once a flat additive bonus is layered on top. On this map, `explore`'s utility curve (fraction of the map still unexplored) stays near-saturated for a long time simply because the map is large, so it was already within 0.15 of flee's ceiling before hysteresis was even applied — the hysteresis bonus, sized to solve small near-tie oscillations, was large enough to permanently override a maximal life-critical consideration once the two ranges overlapped. Empirically this was not a rare edge case: across 20 seeds x 300 ticks, the NPC entered a caught-and-stuck-in-explore state and stayed there for the rest of the run in most seeds (single capture episode, `maxStreak` ~93% of remaining ticks) — the two AI techniques, each correct alone, actively fought each other once combined on a map shape (large, sparse) neither prior single-system sandbox used.
- Fix: Added an "urgent override" — if any action's RAW (pre-hysteresis) score is >= 0.98, it is chosen immediately, bypassing the hysteresis comparison entirely, regardless of the incumbent action's score. This models the standard game-AI "interrupt" pattern: hysteresis should only govern tie-breaking among comparable options, never suppress a saturated/critical one.
- Why this fix: A flat additive hysteresis bonus is only "safe" when the action-switching decision is between options whose utility RANGES don't already overlap near saturation; on any utility curve that can independently reach values close to 1.0 for non-urgent reasons (e.g. "still lots of world left to explore"), a fixed bonus can't distinguish "near-tie, worth damping" from "genuinely urgent, must never be suppressed" — an explicit saturation-based bypass restores that distinction without weakening hysteresis's normal tie-breaking job.
- Rule of thumb: Before combining multiplicative-veto utility AI with additive hysteresis, check whether any two actions' utility curves can both independently approach 1.0 under realistic conditions (not just at the moment of the urgent event) — if so, a flat hysteresis bonus can permanently suppress a maximal-urgency action once its raw score first loses a comparison to the incumbent, and the failure will look like "the AI never even tries to flee" rather than an obvious thrash/oscillation bug, making it easy to miss without direct tick-by-tick score tracing.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand — traced exact per-tick utility scores at a real capture event in the running Node.js sandbox (flee=1.0, explore=1.136, chosen=explore), then implemented and re-ran the urgent-override fix across the same 20-seed sweep confirming the override fires (72 times/20 seeds) without breaking determinism (0/20 hash mismatches) or reintroducing earlier stuck/unreachable bugs.
