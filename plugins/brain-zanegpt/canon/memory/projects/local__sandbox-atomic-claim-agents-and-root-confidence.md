## E1 — atomic claim agents isolate blocked sources but need root tracing and alias handling to rate confidence
- Date: 2026-10-11
- Project: local/sandbox-atomic-claim-agents-and-root-confidence
- Tags: [research][process][simulation][method]
- What: A deterministic model of 12 claims verified by one batch pass versus one agent per claim, with confidence rated either by the count of source names or by distinct roots after following derived-from chains, normalizing aliases and guarding cycles.
- Why built: The domain-seeding kernel says one atomic agent per claim forces a real fetched quote and isolates friction, and the root-tracing kernel says confidence should count roots; I wanted to see whether atomicity alone is enough to make the confidence rating trustworthy.
- Design: Claims carry sources with derived-from, alias and fetchable fields. The batch pass has a fetch budget of 6 and aborts at a blocked primary; the atomic agents each get their own budget and a verdict needs a verbatim quote. Claims include two false echo chains, one false claim with two independent but equally wrong roots, a true claim with a blocked primary, and one with an alias pair plus a citation cycle.
- Got right: Atomic agents filed quotes for 11 of 12 claims and stated the single blocked one as unverified, while the batch pass filed 2 quotes and 10 recalled verdicts with no quote. The false echo chains scored 4 and 3 by name count but 1 by roots.
- Where/why it failed: The batch numbers follow from my stated assumptions (abort on a block, recall past the budget), so they show the structure of the failure, not its size. The built breaker landed: the false claim with two independent roots scores 2 by roots, so atomic fetching plus root counting still rates it as corroborated.
- Fix: Keep atomic agents but report roots as a lower bound on independence, not as truth: record the quote and the root ids per claim, normalize aliases before counting (raw count 2, normalized 1), guard cycles, and mark independent-root agreement as unreviewed when the roots could share a common error.
- Why this fix: Atomicity fixes provenance per claim and root counting fixes repetition, but neither tests whether independent sources are right, so the report must expose the roots for a reader to judge.
- Rule of thumb: One agent per claim made every fetched quote auditable and contained a blocked source to one claim in this model, but a confidence rating still needs distinct roots with aliases normalized and cycles guarded, and two independent roots can both be wrong, so show the root list instead of a score.
