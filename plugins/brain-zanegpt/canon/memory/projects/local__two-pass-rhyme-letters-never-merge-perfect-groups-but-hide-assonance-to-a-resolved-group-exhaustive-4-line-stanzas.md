## E1 — two pass rhyme letters never merge perfect groups but hide assonance to a resolved group exhaustive 4 line stanzas
- Date: 2026-10-19
- Project: local/two-pass-rhyme-letters-never-merge-perfect-groups-but-hide-assonance-to-a-resolved-group-exhaustive-4-line-stanzas
- Tags: [songwriting][rhyme][simulation][method]
- What: An exhaustive enumeration of all 1296 four-line stanzas (line = vowel from three times coda from two) comparing one-pass any-rhyme letter assignment with two-pass assignment (uppercase for perfect-rhyme groups first, lowercase for assonance among the leftover lines).
- Why built: The two-pass letter-assignment kernel was verified on hand-picked stanzas; I wanted every stanza of a small space to see how often one pass fails and whether two passes have a failure of their own.
- Design: Perfect rhyme is same vowel and coda, assonance is same vowel only. One-pass gives the first earlier vowel match its letter. Two-pass letters perfect groups uppercase, then groups the still unlettered lines by vowel in lowercase. Ground truth is the set of perfect-rhyme groups of the stanza.
- Got right: Two-pass uppercase groups equalled the true perfect-rhyme groups in 1296 of 1296 stanzas, while one-pass merged lines that are not perfect rhymes in 834 of 1296 (64.4%).
- Where/why it failed: Two-pass is not lossless: in 312 of 1296 stanzas (24.1%) a line left unlettered shares its vowel with a line in a resolved uppercase group, so the assonance link between the loose line and the resolved group is not shown by the letters at all.
- Fix: Keep the two-pass assigner for the stable/unstable reading, but when a report must show every link, add a second note listing leftover lines that are assonant with a resolved group instead of leaving them unmarked.
- Why this fix: The uppercase groups are already exact, so adding a separate list of unmatched-but-assonant lines restores the missing information without touching the letters that earn the kernel its legibility.
- Rule of thumb: Two-pass lettering gave exact perfect-rhyme groups in all 1296 four-line stanzas where one pass merged non-perfect lines in 64.4%, but it left an assonance link to a resolved group unmarked in 24.1%, so list leftover assonant lines separately when every link must show.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the saved sandbox, PASS with deterministic output; vowel and coda are abstract symbols, not real English phonemes, and stanzas longer than four lines were not enumerated.
- Composed: ideas:SONGWRITING/rhyme-tooling/two-pass-letter-assignment-keeps-schemes-legible, sandbox-lyrical-composition/E7, suno-builder/E9
- Picked-by: current-half
- ZaneGPT verdict: weak — the 100% exactness is tautological because uppercase is defined by the ground truth, and the 64.4% and 24.1% are descriptive counts from one tiny alphabet, not a tested rule that "list leftover assonant lines separately" fixes the hidden-link problem.
- Hidden assumption: The "lesson" is mostly a property of the toy space, where 6 sounds (3 vowels × 2 codas) across 4 lines make collisions near-certain, and it assumes the ground truth (same vowel and coda) and "merge" mean the same thing the original lettering was meant to express, so the 64.4% and 24.1% describe this alphabet and not real rhyme.
- Would prove it wrong: Rerunning the enumeration with a larger inventory (say 5 vowels × 4 codas) or longer stanzas, where the one-pass merge rate falls well below 30% or the two-pass hidden-assonance rate leaves the 5–40% band, would show the percentages don't generalize. A two-pass uppercase group that differs from the perfect groups in even one stanza would also refute the 100% claim.
- Break scenario: Take the stanza (a,x)(a,y)(a,x)(a,y). Two-pass gives A B A B and no lowercase letters, because every line is already in a perfect group. Assonance between the A and B lines is hidden entirely, and a reader who treats uppercase and lowercase as the only rhyme signal would see two unrelated rhymes. The 24.1% "unmarked" count may also undercount, since singleton leftover lines that are assonant with a resolved group still get lowercase letters that don't point to that group.
