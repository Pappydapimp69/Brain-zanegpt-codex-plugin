## E1 — sandbox combined mirage waiting city hold selector turns veto into livelock
- Date: 2026-08-29
- Project: local/sandbox-combined-mirage-waiting-city-hold-selector-turns-veto-into-livelock
- Tags: [architecture][ai][state][testing]
- What: A deterministic sandbox (fixed agents, fixed contested cell, no RNG, no wall-clock) running an agent under a commit-time occupancy veto with two selector policies — mirage#E14's first-eligible-and-HOLD, and a re-evaluating availability-aware selector — across 20 ticks where the eligible-target predicate widens at tick 5.
- Why built: waiting-city#E19 records that movement-stack#E1's commit-time occupancy veto is correctly implemented (0/48 overlaps live-verified) but silently converts into a wasted AI tick. mirage#E14 separately records that a first-eligible-and-HOLD selector inverts when its predicate widens, latching onto a trivial early candidate while staying fully operational. This tests whether the two compound: whether a correct veto plus a hold selector turns E19's bounded single wasted tick into something unbounded.
- Design: Agent A statically occupies the contested cell. Agent B selects a target each tick and attempts a commit-time veto check. At ticks 0-4 the only eligible target is the contested cell; from tick 5 two free targets also become eligible (mirage#E14's predicate-widening). The HOLD selector latches its first choice and never re-evaluates; the comparison selector re-selects each tick preferring an unoccupied target, which is mirage#E14's own prescribed acquire-ordering fix.
- Got right: The first construction did NOT reproduce — the no-hold selector was still order-blind (always picking targets[0]), so both policies wasted all 20 ticks and the comparison was meaningless. Rebuilding the comparison selector to be availability-aware, matching mirage#E14's stated fix (acquire ordering worth 95%, preemption 0%), isolated the actual variable.
- Where/why it failed: Under the HOLD selector, agent B latches onto the contested cell at tick 0 when it is the only eligible candidate, the veto correctly refuses it every subsequent tick, and because the hold suppresses re-selection B never observes the free targets that appear at tick 5 — 20 of 20 ticks wasted, 0 work completed, still latched on 'contested' at the end. The re-evaluating selector completes 15 units of work with 5 wasted ticks over the identical schedule. Critically, every invariant either source lesson checks still reports healthy: 0 overlaps, the veto functioning exactly as designed, the agent's FSM fully operational.
- Fix: A commit-time veto and a hold-style selector must not be adopted independently — either the selector re-evaluates its target whenever a veto refuses it (releasing the hold on refusal), or the veto must publish its refusal back into the selector's acquire ordering so the held target stops ranking first. Instrument consecutive-vetoes-per-agent, not just total overlaps.
- Why this fix: waiting-city#E19's cost analysis ("the veto costs a wasted tick") is implicitly bounded by an assumption the entry does not state — that the agent will select something different next tick. A hold selector removes exactly that assumption, so the same correct veto stops being a one-tick tax and becomes a permanent livelock. Neither lesson's own verification would surface it: overlap counts stay at 0 because the veto is working, and the selector stays "fully operational" in mirage#E14's own words.
- Rule of thumb: A commit-time occupancy veto (waiting-city#E19) is a bounded one-tick cost only while the selector re-evaluates — composed with mirage#E14's first-eligible-and-HOLD selector the same correct veto becomes an unbounded livelock that reports 0 overlaps and a healthy FSM; release the hold on veto refusal and instrument consecutive vetoes per agent, not just overlap counts.
