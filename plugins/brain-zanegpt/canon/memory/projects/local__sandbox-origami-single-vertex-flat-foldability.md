## E1 — single vertex flat foldability maekawa and kawasaki are necessary not sufficient
- Date: 2026-10-14
- Project: local/sandbox-origami-single-vertex-flat-foldability
- Tags: [origami][geometry][simulation]
- What: A brute-force sandbox folded a degree-4 crease vertex under all 16 mountain/valley assignments and four angle sets. Foldable counts were 8 for (90,90,90,90), 4 for (45,90,135,90), 0 for (100,90,100,70) which breaks Kawasaki, and 4 for (30,60,150,120), while 8 assignments satisfied Maekawa in every set; every foldable assignment satisfied Maekawa (0 exceptions).
- Why built: Seeding the origami domain with a claim I could run: the two counting rules for a flat-foldable vertex are widely quoted, and I wanted to see whether passing both is enough.
- Design: Python, stdlib, no RNG. Fold directions by alternating-sign angle sums (closure is Kawasaki), sectors alternate face-up and face-down so a mountain crease sends the next sector behind or in front depending on parity, then a search over all 24 stack orders requiring each crease's order, no crease piercing an in-between sector, and nesting (no interleaving) of creases on the same folded line.
- Got right: The enumeration reproduced the known single-vertex counts (8, 4, 0, 4) and found Maekawa necessary with no exception across 64 assignments.
- Where/why it failed: Three checker bugs showed up before the numbers made sense. My first stacking rule ignored face-up/down alternation (90 degree set gave 14 foldable), my first generic angle set (60,120,60,120) did not satisfy Kawasaki at all, and without the nesting rule creases on the same line could interleave. Each was found because a count disagreed with the theory; the 4-of-8 target was derived from the smallest-angle rule before the final run.
- Fix: Validate a crease vertex in three steps: Kawasaki on the angles, Maekawa on the marks, then a layer-order search with a pierce rule and a nesting rule; for generic angles expect only the assignments that give the smallest angle's creases opposite marks.
- Why this fix: The run shows Maekawa passing for 8 assignments in every set while the foldable count fell to 4 or 0, so the counts alone over-accept.
- Rule of thumb: Maekawa and Kawasaki are necessary, not sufficient, for a flat-foldable vertex: in this degree-4 enumeration 8 Maekawa-valid assignments gave 8, 4, 0 and 4 foldable ones, so finish with a layer-order check, and verify your checker against a known count before trusting it.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the saved sandbox; counts are its printed output. Scope is degree-4 single vertices only, and the stack model is mine, checked against the known counts rather than an independent folder; multi-vertex global flat-foldability is NP-hard and was not tested.
