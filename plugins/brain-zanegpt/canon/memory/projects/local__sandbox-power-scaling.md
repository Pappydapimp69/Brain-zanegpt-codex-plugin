## E2 — sandbox power scaling percent max hp cap fixes one shot
- Date: 2026-07-12
- Project: local/sandbox-power-scaling
- Tags: [gamedesign][gamedev][state]
- What: A sandbox modeled a DBZ-style combat system where a single scalar "power level" doubles as both an offense stat and (via HP derived from it) a survivability stat — the naive design implied by DBZ's own canon, where "power level" represents overall combat strength. Five different damage formulas (linear difference, raw ratio, ratio capped at 5x, log-scaled, sqrt-scaled) were simulated across attacker:defender power ratios from 1x to 100,000x (the range DBZ canon actually uses, vs. the 1.1x-2x gaps typical RPG balance assumes). ALL FIVE formulas collapsed to a 1-2 turn fight (effectively a one-shot kill) the instant the ratio exceeded roughly 5x-10x, regardless of the formula's mathematical shape.
- Why built: To find out whether a damage-formula choice alone (a common first instinct: "just use a gentler curve like sqrt or log") can keep multi-turn, meaningful fights possible across DBZ-typical power gaps, before recommending an approach to a future build.
- Design: `simulateCombat(attackerPower, defenderPower, damageFormula)` runs a simple turn-based exchange where each side's HP pool IS their power-level number, and damage per hit is `damageFormula(atk, def)`. A follow-up test decoupled HP from raw power via sub-linear growth (`HP = sqrt(power)*100` and `HP = log(power+1)*2000`) while keeping damage directly proportional to attacker power, then a third test added a hard cap: damage per hit limited to a fixed percentage (20%) of the DEFENDER's own max HP, independent of the attacker's raw power magnitude.
- Got right: Isolating the two decoupling attempts (sub-linear HP alone vs. percent-max-HP damage cap) made the actual mechanism visible: sub-linear HP growth alone does NOT fix the problem, because even though HP grows slower than power, damage (still proportional to raw attacker power) grows FASTER than defender HP as the ratio increases, so the damage/HP ratio remains unbounded and fights still collapsed to 1-2 turns past ratio~10x in that variant too.
- Where/why it failed: Any formula where per-hit damage is a function of the ATTACKER's raw power (linear, ratio, capped-ratio, log, sqrt — all five tested, plus a sixth "sub-linear HP only" variant) inevitably lets a sufficiently stronger attacker's single hit exceed the defender's entire HP pool once the power ratio is large enough, because nothing in those formulas bounds a hit's damage relative to the TARGET's own survivability — the formula shape only changes at what ratio the collapse happens, not whether it happens. At DBZ-typical ratios (10x-100,000x are common across a single story arc), every tested formula was already past its collapse point.
- Fix: Cap per-hit damage as a fixed percentage of the DEFENDER's own max HP (`damage = min(attackerRawDamage, defenderMaxHP * 0.2)`), independent of the attacker's raw power magnitude for the purposes of the cap. Verified this produces a STABLE, ratio-independent fight length: exactly 5 turns at every tested ratio from 1x to 100,000x, versus 1-2 turns for every uncapped variant at ratio>=10x.
- Why this fix: A percent-max-HP damage cap directly targets the actual failure mode (a single hit exceeding total defender HP) rather than trying to indirectly dampen it through the damage formula's growth curve — since the cap is defined relative to the DEFENDER's own stats, it scales automatically with any defender regardless of the attacker's power, guaranteeing a minimum number of hits-to-kill by construction rather than as an emergent (and, as tested, unreliable) property of the damage math.
- Rule of thumb: For any combat system spanning power ratios beyond roughly one order of magnitude (DBZ-style escalation, prestige/rebirth systems, any game where "the final boss's stat sheet dwarfs the tutorial enemy's"), do not expect a gentler damage-formula CURVE (sqrt, log, capped ratio) to prevent one-shot outcomes on its own — verify with an actual ratio sweep, because in this test every curve-based approach still collapsed by ratio~10x. Instead cap per-hit damage as a percentage of the DEFENDER's own max HP, which is the only tested approach that produced ratio-independent, stable fight length across the full 1x-100,000x range.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand — ran the actual Node.js sandbox across 9 power ratios (1x-100,000x) x 5 damage formulas x the two decoupling variants, comparing exact turn-counts and survivor outcomes; the percent-max-HP-cap result (flat 5 turns at every ratio) was reproduced deterministically across all 9 ratios in a single run.

## E1 — sandbox power scaling float64 safe integer headroom
- Date: 2026-07-12
- Project: local/sandbox-power-scaling
- Tags: [determinism][gamedev][state]
- What: A sandbox modeled a DBZ-style "power level" stat compounding multiplicatively across successive story-arc power jumps (canon-inspired multiplier ranges of roughly 1.1x-200x per arc), then measured directly, via a compounding-multiplication loop, how many such arc-jumps it actually takes a plain float64 `number` to (a) lose exact-safe-integer representation (cross `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`, 2^53≈9.007e15) and (b) overflow to `Infinity` (cross ~1.8e308), using a representative geometric-mean multiplier (5.5x) derived from the sandbox's own 12-arc canon-inspired growth model.
- Why built: DBZ-style power numbers are famously huge (into the billions/quadrillions), which invites an assumption that a live power-level stat needs BigInt or fixed-point arithmetic to stay safe — this sandbox checked whether that assumption is actually justified for a game of plausible content length, rather than architecting around it preemptively.
- Design: Straight-line compounding loop (`power *= 5.5` per iteration) checking `Number.isSafeInteger(Math.round(power))` and `Number.isFinite(power)` after each iteration, run to find the exact first-crossing iteration for each boundary.
- Got right: Using a representative MEAN multiplier (5.5x, derived from the sandbox's own documented 12-arc canon-inspired ranges) rather than a single worst-case or best-case multiplier makes the arc-count estimate directly comparable to a real game's likely total count of major power-tier-jump events across its full content lifetime.
- Where/why it failed: N/A — this is a characterization result, not a bug fix. The concrete numbers: the safe-integer boundary (2^53) is crossed after only 21 compounding arc-jumps at this representative multiplier (value at crossover: ~1.76e16), while float64 overflow (Infinity) isn't reached until 416 compounding arc-jumps (astronomically beyond any plausible game's content). This means the "numbers get huge, therefore need BigInt" instinct is right for exact-integer semantics but for a subtler and later reason than raw magnitude: overflow itself is essentially unreachable, but losing exact-integer representability happens within a very plausible content lifetime (a game with 20+ major power-escalation beats across its full run — comparable to Dragon Ball's own canon history end-to-end — could plausibly cross this boundary), even though the FLOAT VALUE remains accurate to ~15-17 significant decimal digits and still displays/computes correctly for ordinary gameplay math well past that point.
- Fix: Not a bug — the finding recommends a nuanced default: plain `number` power-level arithmetic is fine for display and ordinary combat-math purposes for the practical lifetime of most single games (games would need ~20+ multiplicative story-arc jumps to even approach the safe-integer boundary, and effectively never reach true overflow), but any system doing EXACT-integer-sensitive operations on the power-level value — equality checks, save-diff/patch generation, deterministic state hashing (canonical fingerprinting, as this project's own the-game-prologue#E1 memory already establishes for other state fields), leaderboard exact-tie detection — should not assume the stored number is exactly representable once a long-running or content-heavy game crosses roughly 20 compounding growth events, and should use BigInt or a scaled-fixed-point representation for THOSE specific operations rather than defaulting to it for the whole stat.
- Why this fix: Precision loss past 2^53 doesn't corrupt gameplay math (float64 still tracks the true value to ~15-17 significant figures, so combat/display arithmetic looks and behaves correctly) — it specifically breaks operations that require bit-exact equality or hashing, which is a narrower and easy-to-miss risk surface than "the number might get too big to use," and scoping the fix to just those operations avoids paying BigInt's performance/ergonomics cost everywhere a power-level number is touched.
- Rule of thumb: Before assuming a fast-growing numeric game stat needs BigInt/fixed-point arithmetic throughout, measure the actual crossover point for YOUR game's realistic growth-event count and representative multiplier — the crossover for "loses display/gameplay accuracy" (float64 overflow, ~1.8e308) is essentially unreachable, but the crossover for "loses exact-integer identity" (2^53) can be much closer than intuition about "huge DBZ numbers" suggests, and only matters for the subset of operations (hashing, exact equality, diffing) that actually require bit-exact integers rather than accurate-enough floats.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand — ran the actual compounding-multiplication loop in Node.js, directly checking `Number.isSafeInteger`/`Number.isFinite` at each of 500 iterations and recording the exact first-crossing iteration and value for both boundaries.
