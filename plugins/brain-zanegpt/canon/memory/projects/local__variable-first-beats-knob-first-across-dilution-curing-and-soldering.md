## E1 — variable first beats knob first across dilution curing and soldering
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Project: local/variable-first-beats-knob-first-across-dilution-curing-and-soldering
- Tags: [numerics][sandbox][model][measurement]
- What: Running the dilution, curing and soldering models on one grid each, the common knob-first advice (six dilution steps, dose as percent of total weight, hold the iron 10 s) fails somewhere in every domain, in 7 of 7, 3 of 4 and 1 of 4 cells, while computing the hidden variable first (pipette floor, dose weight base, steady-state temperature) and then choosing fails in none.
- Why built: Train pick joining three isolated bench lessons that share a shape, to see whether the shape is a real common pattern or three unrelated stories.
- Design: Dilution step error max(0.5 percent, a over volume) plus a diluent term, target 3 percent over seven values of a; curing ppm on meat target 156.25 within 10 percent over four water ratios; solder first-order joint melt within 2 s over four board conductances, preheating to 100 C when the steady state is under 217 C.
- Got right: The variable-first policy met its target in every cell where a plan exists, and in the benign cells of curing (water 0) and soldering (small pad) both policies agreed, so the knob is not wrong everywhere.
- Where/why it failed: My prediction that knob-first would fail in at least 30 percent of every domain was wrong for soldering (25 percent, 1 of 4 cells), and the benign tie did not hold for dilution, where six steps failed even at zero small-volume error because six floors add up.
- Fix: State the shared pattern as variable-first beats knob-first wherever the knob has a range, but report the failing share per domain instead of one number, since it depends on how much of the grid lies outside the knob's range.
- Why this fix: The share of failing cells is set by how the grid straddles each hidden variable's threshold, so a single percentage would be an artifact of the grid I chose.
- Rule of thumb: Before turning a procedure's usual knob, compute the hidden variable that bounds it; in these three toy models that fixed every failure the knob-first advice produced, though how often the knob fails depends on where your case sits relative to the threshold.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox in this session; all three models use the earlier lessons' assumed constants and no real pipette, cure or iron was measured.

- Hidden assumption: The "hidden variable" and the "knob-first advice" were both defined by the same author who also built the simulators, so the toy models encode the answer: variable-first can't fail because the threshold it computes is the same one the pass criterion is scored against, which makes 0 failures a property of the design and not evidence about real procedures.
- Would prove it wrong: A grid where the hidden variable is misestimated by the amount real inputs are uncertain (pipette floor ±30%, meat water ratio ±10%, pad conductance ±25%) and variable-first still fails in some cells at a rate comparable to knob-first, or where a blind knob change (nine dilution steps, 8 s hold) passes all cells without the variable computed.
- Break scenario: Perturbed-input run: draw the hidden variable from a noisy estimate (the dilution pipette floor is mis-stated by 30%, the steady state is off by 40 C near the 217 C threshold), keep the pass thresholds fixed, and add a third arm of "knob-first with a tuned knob", such as nine dilution steps or a 15 s hold. If variable-first drops to knob-first's failure rate, or the tuned knob matches it, the rule collapses into "know your model."
