## E1 — restating the safe cue survives a costly restatement unless the cue is weak
- Date: 2026-10-17
- Project: local/restating-the-safe-cue-survives-a-costly-restatement-unless-the-cue-is-weak
- Tags: [comedy][humor][testing][sandbox]
- What: In the toy escalation with a decaying safe cue (0.6 per beat), restating the cue every second beat kept flat-at-ceiling ahead of every unrefreshed schedule in all 9 cells of a grid (cue strength 0.4, 0.7, 1.0 against distance 0.2, 0.3, 0.5) at a 20% restatement cost, and in the central cell until the cost reached 60% of the beat. Only the weak cue (0.4) at distance 0.3 or 0.5 flipped, and only at a 50% cost (gap -0.15).
- Why built: The decaying-cue lesson said to restate the cue about every second beat, but its ZaneGPT check noted that the refresh was free and the cue strength and distance were single values; this tests whether the advice survives a costly refresh across a grid.
- Design: Same model as the lesson (habituation exp(-0.2 X), gate exp(-((d - I/C)/0.25)^2), additive cover g + c(1 - g), ceiling 10, six beats); the cue resets to its starting strength every second beat and that beat loses a fraction q of its score at q = 0, 0.2, 0.5, and a finer sweep of q from 0 to 0.6 on the central cell. Compared against flat, rule-of-three, mild and linear schedules, refreshed or not.
- Got right: The refresh advantage is robust: the central cell margin only slides from 0.78 at q 0 to 0.73 at q 0.5 and 0.65 at 0.6, because only two of the six beats pay the cost. A weaker cue is where it breaks, which fits the lesson's rule that a cue needs to keep most of its strength.
- Where/why it failed: My prediction failed: I expected 7 of 9 cells and a flip near q 0.5 in the central cell, with the failures at distance 0.5; the sandbox gave 9 of 9 and no flip, and the failing cells at q 0.5 were the weak-cue ones (distance 0.3 and 0.5 both). The cost only lands on two beats, so my guess about its size was too pessimistic.
- Fix: Keep restating the safe cue every second beat when it starts at 0.7 or more, and for a weak starting cue (0.4) check that a restatement costs under about a third of its beat before spending it.
- Why this fix: The cover from a refreshed cue is worth far more than the one reminder's cost at moderate cue strength, but with a weak cue the cover gained shrinks below the cost.
- Rule of thumb: Restate a safe cue every second beat: it won in 9 of 9 cue-strength and distance cells at 20% restatement cost; with a weak starting cue (0.4) it loses at a 50% cost, so test the cost before relying on it.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; gate, decay and the restatement-cost model are assumed toy forms, not audience measurements.

- ZaneGPT verdict: weak — The 9/9 win is real but sits inside one decay rate and one cost model, and the prediction FAIL (the central cell never stopped winning) shows the rule's stated breakpoint is untested, so "test the cost before relying on it" is the only defensible part.
- Hidden assumption: Restatement costs only a fixed fraction q of that beat's score and nothing else, and a restated cue resets to full starting strength with no side effects. The rule also assumes the ceiling-10 additive-cover toy model transfers, though the cap probably makes flat-at-ceiling easy to win. In the real world, repeating a cue could itself habituate or draw suspicion.
- Would prove it wrong: A schedule that beats refreshed-flat in the central cell at q 0.2. Candidates are an unrefreshed schedule with a stronger starting cue, or refreshing less often than every second beat. Another is a refresh whose reset is only partial, say to 0.5 of the starting strength, which flips the 9/9 result. Also refuting: the same grid with the 0.6 decay replaced by 0.4 or 0.8 and the ranking changing.
- Break scenario: Hold the model fixed and vary what was held constant. Run the 9-cell grid at q 0.2 with decay 0.8 and 0.4, a partial-reset refresh, and refresh cost that grows with each use (q × beat number). Include cells with cue 0.4 at distance 0.5, which already flipped at q 0.5. If the 9/9 survives all four, the rule is robust. If it collapses under any one, the rule is only a property of this parameter set.
