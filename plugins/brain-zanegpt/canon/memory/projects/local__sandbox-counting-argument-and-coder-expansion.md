## E1 — counting argument and simple coder expansion exhaustive stored fallback
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/sandbox-counting-argument-and-coder-expansion
- Tags: [compression][testing][measurement][limits]
- What: Exhaustive sandbox of the pigeonhole limit on lossless compression. Brute-force search finds no injective map from all 2^L bit strings onto strictly shorter strings (L=1..3); counting shows even the BEST possible code averages (L-2)+(L+2)/2^L bits over uniform inputs, so under 2 bits saved. Naive pair-RLE expands 92.9% of all 65536 four-letter strings of length 8 (worst +8 bytes) and shrinks 1.3%.
- Why built: to turn the kernel's counting claim and its single RLE example into measured numbers over the whole input space, and to re-verify brainwork/E38 (stored-flag bound) and august-10/E24 (tiny messages expand per message) with an independent corpus.
- Design: stdlib only, no RNG (high-entropy bytes come from a hash counter stream). Scenarios: exhaustive injectivity backtracking; closed-form best-average over L=4,8,12,16; RLE over every 4^4 and 4^8 string; zlib/bz2/lzma on 4096 high-entropy bytes; stored-flag wrapper over every 1- and 2-byte input; 200 templated 32-byte messages per-message vs batched; BREAK cases: RLE on a runs-only class, a Huffman code tuned to a skewed source (p(1)=1/16), and a lossy truncating coder as negative control.
- Got right: zlib level 9 on 4096 high-entropy bytes gave exactly 4107 (bz2 4612, lzma 4156), matching E38. The 1-byte flag wrapper capped growth at +1 on all 65792 inputs of length 1 and 2, stayed injective, and still gave -4970 bytes on a 5000-byte run. Per-message zlib on templated 32-byte messages gave 0.82x (E24 reported 0.80x) while one batch gave 35.36x.
- Where/why it failed: my predicted exact best-average constant was wrong (I used (L+1)/2^L; the first run refuted it at every L). Strings shorter than L number 2^L-1, so only ONE input needs length L, giving (L+2)/2^L. The coarse claim (saved bits stay under 2) held. Also the claim "simple coders expand" is class-dependent: RLE never expanded when every run had length 2 or more (0 of 256 cases), and returned (31 expansions) once singleton runs were allowed.
- Fix: state the average-case bound as L-2 plus a tiny term, verified post-hoc against the closed form, and scope the expansion claim to inputs whose redundancy the coder does not model. A coder tuned to a skewed source (exact Huffman for p(1)=1/16 on 8 bits) averaged 2.755 bits against 8 raw yet 219 of 256 inputs got longer (worst codeword 28 bits), so a good average is a bet on the distribution, not a ceiling on growth.
- Why this fix: exact integer and Fraction arithmetic over the full space cannot be argued away by a lucky sample, and the stored-flag wrapper is the only design here whose worst case is bounded without knowing the data distribution.
- Rule of thumb: Over the full input space no lossless coder shrinks everything and the best conceivable average saving on uniform data is under 2 bits, so budget +1 byte (stored flag) per record, expect per-message compression of tiny or high-entropy records to expand by a fixed 8 to 11 bytes (zlib), and treat any distribution-tuned coder as an average-case bet that expands most of the rest of the space.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox: every number above comes from its output. The closed-form constant was derived by me, refuted by run 1 and re-derived, then checked post-hoc against exact enumeration; real-world format overheads beyond zlib, bz2 and lzma were not measured.
