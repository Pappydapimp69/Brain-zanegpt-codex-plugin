## E1 — painters centroid sort vs zbuffer yaw sweep bbox overlap not sufficient
- Date: 2026-10-11
- Project: local/painters-centroid-sort-vs-zbuffer-yaw-sweep-bbox-overlap-not-sufficient
- Tags: [geometry][visibility][rendering][testing]
- What: A deterministic 96x96 orthographic rasterizer with two truly piercing triangles (A flat at z=0, B leaning from z=-14 to +14), swept over camera yaw 0 to 170 degrees in 10-degree steps. For each yaw it compares both whole-face draw orders and the centroid-z sort against a per-pixel depth test, and records whether the 3D bounding boxes overlap.
- Why built: To test two ideas kernels together: no whole-face order can be right for piercing geometry, and bounding-box overlap is necessary but not sufficient evidence of a visible artifact. Also re-checks the earlier geometry-visibility entry that the centroid sort picks the lesser of two bad orders.
- Design: Triangle rasterization with barycentric coverage, larger camera-space z is nearer. Truth is the per-pixel nearer triangle over the overlap. Wrong pixels counted for A then B, B then A and the centroid order. A control copy of B translated 60 units sideways (no bounding-box overlap) is run over the same sweep.
- Got right: Bounding boxes overlapped at all 18 yaws, so the necessary condition always held. At yaw 0 the orders were wrong on 460 and 154 of 614 overlap pixels and the centroid sort picked the lesser (154). The centroid sort picked the lesser order at all 18 yaws. The separated control had 0 wrong pixels at every yaw.
- Where/why it failed: The claim that a centroid sort can resolve a piercing pair correctly across the views in use did not reproduce in the non-degenerate views: the centroid order was wrong on a positive number of pixels at 17 of 18 yaws (from 142 at yaw 10 down to 4 at yaws 80 and 100). The only view with 0 wrong pixels was yaw 90 where the pair is edge-on and the overlap is 0 pixels, so the safe case is a nonexistent overlap, not a correctly sorted one. Scope: one triangle pair, orthographic projection, a single rotation axis.
- Fix: Treat bounding-box overlap as a screening test only, and judge visibility by rendering the pair at the camera angles in use and counting wrong pixels against a depth test; where wrong pixels are small (4 to 10 of 8 to 28 overlap pixels near edge-on) the artifact is a sliver, which is when eyeballing is believable.
- Why this fix: Wrong-pixel count fell smoothly with the visible overlap area (614 to 0), so the artifact size is set by how much of the intersection faces the camera, which bounding boxes do not measure.
- Rule of thumb: For a truly piercing pair, bounding-box overlap was true at all 18 yaws while the centroid-sort error ran from 154 of 614 overlap pixels face-on down to 4 of 8 and 0 only edge-on, so use the box test to find candidates and the rendered wrong-pixel count at your own camera angles to decide.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; all counts are exact and deterministic. Not tested: perspective projection, more than one pair, pitch rotations, rigs with deliberate socket overlap.
