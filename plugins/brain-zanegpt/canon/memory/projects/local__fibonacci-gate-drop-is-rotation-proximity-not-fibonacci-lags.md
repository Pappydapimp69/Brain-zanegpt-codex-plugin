## E1 — fibonacci gate drop is rotation proximity not fibonacci lags
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Project: local/fibonacci-gate-drop-is-rotation-proximity-not-fibonacci-lags
- Tags: [ui][input][state][gamedev][testing]
- What: On a Fibonacci-word legality signal the gate-only invalid count tracks the distance of lag times phi to the nearest integer (correlation 0.98 over lags 1 to 120), so non-Fibonacci Lucas lags 47 and 76 also fall (29 and 17 against a window median of 158), while a Bernoulli signal of the same 0.618 density stays flat near 142 at lags 55 and 89.
- Why built: Train pick joining the lossless-cutoff-is-worst rule and its one-sided formula with the Fibonacci companion, using the companion's own break scenario to find what the low count at Fibonacci lags is really a property of.
- Design: Fibonacci word by substitution 1 to 10, 0 to 1, 730 ticks; invalid(L) counts t in 0..599 legal at t and illegal at t plus L; lags 1 to 120; Lucas lags 4 to 76 as non-Fibonacci low-distance lags; 20 seeded Bernoulli(0.618) signals as density-matched aperiodic control; period 12 width 5 control for the closed formula.
- Got right: Fibonacci lags gave 229 down to 3, Lucas lags 47 and 76 gave 29 and 17 (well under a quarter of the median), the Bernoulli signal showed no drop (142 at both lags), and the periodic control matched (N/P) times min(L, W, P-L, P-W) on lags 1 to 11.
- Where/why it failed: Nothing in the prediction failed, but the Lucas drop is gentler than the Fibonacci drop, so the Fibonacci lags are the extreme case of a smooth proximity effect and not a special set; one Sturmian word and one density were run, so other slopes are untested.
- Fix: State the worst-cutoff rule as a periodic-signal result and, for aperiodic legality, scan the gate-only count per lag, expecting dips where lag times the signal's rotation number is near an integer; do not expect them on unstructured random legality.
- Why this fix: The dip needs a rotation structure, which the Bernoulli control lacks, and it is not specific to Fibonacci numbers, which the Lucas lags show, so the per-lag scan is the only safe test.
- Rule of thumb: On Fibonacci-word legality the gate-only invalid count follows the distance of lag times phi to an integer, dipping at Fibonacci and Lucas lags alike; on random legality of equal density it stays flat, so scan per lag instead of assuming the periodic worst-cutoff rule.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox in this session on one Fibonacci word, 20 Bernoulli seeds and one periodic control; other quasiperiodic signals were not run.

- ZaneGPT verdict: weak — The dip is real and the Bernoulli control rules out density alone, but the control is too far from the hypothesis (no other irrational slope, no shuffled-structure control, one window), so "Fibonacci and Lucas lags alike" is not yet separated from "any rotation word at its own slope".
- Hidden assumption: The dip is read as Fibonacci-word structure (three-distance/Sturmian rotation by phi), but the rule assumes the Bernoulli control isolates that structure. A Bernoulli signal removes all correlation, not just periodicity, so "flat" shows only that correlation matters, not that the phi-distance form is the cause. Also assumed: the invalid count depends on lag alone, not on the 0..599 window or the 730-tick length, and r=0.98 across 120 lags is not inflated by the few deep dips (the plot is mostly non-dips).
- Would prove it wrong: A Sturmian word with a different irrational slope (for example sqrt2-1 or e-2) whose count tracks the distance of lag times that slope to an integer and not the distance to phi. That would make the rule a general rotation fact and not a Fibonacci one. Equally damaging: the correlation falling below about 0.8 when the window is shifted (t in 100..699) or when the 12 lags with near-zero distance are excluded, or Lucas 47 and 76 failing to dip on a longer word.
- Break scenario: A non-Sturmian but deterministic control with the same 0.618 density: a random-phase rotation word, or a Fibonacci word with the order of blocks shuffled while 0.618 density is kept. If the dips persist, the cause is quasi-periodic structure, not Fibonacci substitution. If they vanish, the claim holds. Add 20 Fibonacci-word variants with phase offsets to test whether the lag effect survives a start-point change.
