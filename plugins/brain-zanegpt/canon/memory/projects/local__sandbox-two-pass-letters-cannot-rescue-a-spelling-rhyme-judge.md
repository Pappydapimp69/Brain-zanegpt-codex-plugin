## E1 — sandbox two pass letters cannot rescue a spelling rhyme judge
- Date: 2026-09-30
- Project: local/sandbox-two-pass-letters-cannot-rescue-a-spelling-rhyme-judge
- Tags: [songwriting][rhyme][testing]
- What: Deterministic sandbox composing two songwriting lessons: the two-pass letter-assignment kernel and the finding that the suno-builder judges rhyme from spelling. It shows the two fixes are orthogonal and that the second cannot compensate for the first.
- Why built: The music group had never been exercised together, and it was unclear whether two-pass assignment would repair the wrong schemes a spelling-based judge produces.
- Design: 17-word hand-written phoneme fixture (vowel plus coda). A spelling judge compares the last two letters. Single-pass assignment joins a line to the first group matching perfect or assonance; two-pass assigns perfect groups first. Lines tested: truck, mud, blood, flood.
- Got right: Phonetic truth is A B B B. Spelling judge gives A B C C under both single-pass and two-pass, identical, so the assignment order changes nothing; it recognised 2 of 7 phonetically perfect pairs. A phonetic judge with single-pass collapses to A A A A, and only phonetic plus two-pass gives A B B B.
- Where/why it failed: Fixing the assignment order first would have looked like progress on paper yet left the output unchanged, because the judge's spelling error occurs before any lettering happens.
- Fix: Repair the rhyme judge (pronunciation-based matching) before, or together with, two-pass assignment; test that the output differs from the old scheme on a fixture where truck and mud share only a vowel.
- Why this fix: The judge decides which lines are perfect; assignment order only decides how assonance is treated after that, so each fix targets a different stage.
- Rule of thumb: A rhyme-scheme labeller needs a phonetic judge and perfect-first assignment together: spelling plus two-pass gives ABCC, phonetic plus single-pass gives AAAA, only phonetic plus two-pass gives ABBB.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running sb/m1.py with a hand-made 17-word phoneme fixture, not real CMUdict data; the direction of the result is confirmed, the recall figure applies to that fixture only.
