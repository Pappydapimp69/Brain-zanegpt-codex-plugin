## E1 — gate invalid count is per period constant and cutoff scales it linearly
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Project: local/gate-invalid-count-is-per-period-constant-and-cutoff-scales-it-linearly
- Tags: [gamedev][input][state][testing]
- What: For a gate that skips validity on period-12 width-5 legality, the invalid-press count equals (N/P) times min(L, W, P-L, P-W) exactly in all 44 cells of lags 1 to 11 and horizons N = P, 2P, 3P, 4P, so the invalid rate per tick is identical at every multiple of P, and the summed invalid count rises monotonically with the cutoff from 4 at cutoff 1 to 140 at cutoff 11.
- Why built: Train pick joining the lossless-cutoff-is-worst lesson and the one-sided gate formula with the age-cutoff roll-up, to see whether the roll-up's flat-at-every-multiple-of-P finding carries over to the gate's invalid count.
- Design: Legal(t) is t mod 12 under 5; a hint issued at t-L is invalid at use t iff it was legal then and is illegal now. Counts were taken over horizons starting at base 3P, compared with the closed formula, then rates compared across k, then cutoff totals summed over lags up to each cutoff.
- Got right: Zero mismatches in 44 cells, the per-tick rate matched for k 1 to 4, and the cumulative total was nondecreasing in cutoff, matching the earlier claim that a higher cutoff admits more invalid presses.
- Where/why it failed: My first breaker, a horizon of kP plus 3, did not break the formula because the extra ticks all fell in the legal window; and the roll-up's base-0 break did not transfer, since base 0 gave 0 mismatches here. A tail of kP plus 7, which includes illegal ticks, broke 6 of 11 lags.
- Fix: State the formula's scope as horizons that are whole multiples of the period (any base tested), and treat a partial-period tail as needing a direct count rather than the floor of N over P.
- Why this fix: The formula counts whole periods only, so a tail contributes whatever the phase of its ticks gives, which can be zero or not by luck of where the tail falls.
- Rule of thumb: Gate-only invalid counts are exactly (N/P) times min(L, W, P-L, P-W) when N is a multiple of P, at base 0 as well as later bases, but count a partial-period tail directly instead of rounding down.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox in this session at P 12 width 5 only; other periods and widths are not claimed beyond the earlier recorded P10W3, P12W5 and P15W7 results.

- ZaneGPT verdict: weak — The result is exact but covers a single (P, W) pair and a single legality shape, so "gate-only invalid counts" is generalized from one data point and the rule of thumb is unproven beyond P=12, W=5.
- Hidden assumption: Legality is a pure function of t mod 12 with a fixed window of width 5 and no state, so every period is identical and the count is (N/P) times the per-period count. The "exact in all 44 cells" result tests only P=12, W=5, so the closed form min(L, W, P-L, P-W) has never been checked against other P or W, and it may not hold as stated.
- Would prove it wrong: Any (P, W, L) combination outside P=12, W=5 where brute-force counting disagrees with (N/P)·min(L, W, P-L, P-W). Another candidate is a lag of 12 or more, or L=0, where the formula should give 0 or wrap. A non-integer N/P, or a gate that is not purely t mod P, would also refute it.
- Break scenario: Brute-force P=12, W=7 and P=10, W=3 over lags 1 to P-1. Add a second legal window, or a gate with a phase offset, and use a tail of kP+7 at base 3P. For W=7 the formula needs min(L, 7, 12-L, 5), and I expect the min(L, P-L) part to fail where the two windows interact. The kP+3 tail broke nothing, so the "count the partial tail directly" advice is untested as a warning. It was never shown to matter at the one tail length that was meant to defeat the formula.
