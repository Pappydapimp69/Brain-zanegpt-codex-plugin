## E1 — sandbox cache size sweep loop cliff and ram ceiling
- Date: 2026-10-15
- Project: local/sandbox-cache-size-sweep-loop-cliff-and-ram-ceiling
- Tags: [sandbox][performance][caching][tradeoff]
- What: LRU and FIFO caches swept over 1 to 8 slots on a 100-access loop of 5 items, with a cost model (hit 1, miss 100, slots beyond a 6-slot memory ceiling cost 150 per access), plus the classic 12-access trace that exposes FIFO's anomaly.
- Why built: The cache-eviction kernel says size to the loop, not the policy; brainwork/E226 says a size sweep must keep a hard memory ceiling beside the headline metric; the latency kernel gives the orders-of-magnitude miss penalty. The run puts them together.
- Design: Stdlib simulators (OrderedDict LRU, deque FIFO); loop trace i%5; cost = hits*per-hit + misses*100 with per-hit 1 up to 6 slots and 150 above; Belady trace 1,2,3,4,1,2,5,1,2,3,4,5 at 3 and 4 slots for both policies.
- Got right: The kernel held: below 5 slots both policies score 0 hits of 100, at 5 both score 95, and policy never mattered on the loop. Hit rate alone picks the largest cache (8 slots) but the cost model picks 5 because 7 and 8 slots fall off the ceiling (cost 14750 vs 595).
- Where/why it failed: The kernel's wording that policy is irrelevant either side of the capacity line does not hold on every trace: on the Belady trace FIFO faulted 9 times at 3 slots and 10 at 4 slots (more cache, more faults) while LRU went 10 to 8, so policy matters when the trace is not a pure loop.
- Fix: Find the loop length first and size the cache to it, judge sizes by cost with the memory ceiling in the model rather than hit rate alone, and prefer a stack-property policy such as LRU when the trace may not be a pure loop.
- Why this fix: A pure loop gives identical hit counts for any reasonable policy, so only capacity moves the result; non-loop traces break that symmetry and FIFO can get worse as the cache grows, which LRU cannot.
- Rule of thumb: Size the cache to the loop length (5 slots gave 95 of 100 hits, 4 gave 0) and judge it by access cost under a hard memory ceiling, not hit rate; policy is irrelevant only on a pure loop, since FIFO faulted 9 then 10 going 3 to 4 slots on the Belady trace.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand: the sandbox ran and printed PASS on the first run. The 1/100/150 costs and the 6-slot ceiling are assumed model constants, and it is a hand-written simulator on tiny traces, not a real cache or workload.
