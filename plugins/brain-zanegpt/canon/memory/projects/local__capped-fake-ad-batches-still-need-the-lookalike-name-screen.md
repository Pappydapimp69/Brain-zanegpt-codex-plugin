## E1 — capped fake ad batches still need the lookalike name screen
- Date: 2026-10-20
- Project: local/capped-fake-ad-batches-still-need-the-lookalike-name-screen
- Tags: [testing][sandbox][simulation][method]
- What: At the three batch sizes the trope cap allows (6, 12 and 16 videos) three-syllable invented names from 12 syllables had a pair one syllable apart in 28, 76 and 91 percent of 600 seeded batches, while an exact duplicate appeared in only 0.5, 3 and 6 percent, so the smaller capped batch of 6 does not make the lookalike screen unnecessary.
- Why built: Train pick joining the batch ceiling (6, 12 or 16 videos), the in-batch name lookalike finding and the source check on a kernel's labels, to see whether the cap alone keeps the names safe.
- Design: Seeded simulation, 600 batches per size, names are tuples of 3 syllable indexes from 12, lookalike means at most one syllable position differs, exact duplicate means all three equal; a label list of 6 tropes checked against a source label set where one kernel label is a paraphrase.
- Got right: Lookalike rates rose with batch size as the pair count does (15, 66 and 120 pairs) and matched the independent pair estimate of about 0.26, 0.73 and 0.91, and the exact-duplicate check missed most batches that had lookalikes at every size.
- Where/why it failed: The label check flagged the one paraphrased label by construction, since I wrote the paraphrase in, so it shows the check works and nothing about how often real kernels paraphrase. The batch caps were taken from the earlier entry and not re-derived.
- Fix: Run the in-batch lookalike screen on every capped batch, even the 6-video one, and compare the kernel's trope labels against the source labels before the batch is built.
- Why this fix: Capping the batch reduces the pair count but does not make it zero, so 28 percent of the smallest batches still held a near pair, and a label mismatch is only caught by comparing against the source.
- Rule of thumb: Capping a fake-ad batch at 6 videos cut lookalike names from 91 percent (batch 16) to 28 percent but not to zero, so keep the one-syllable-apart screen at every batch size and treat the exact-duplicate check as insufficient.
- Provenance: Verified first-hand by running the sandbox script in this session; names are abstract syllable indexes and no real ad platform was used.
- Composed: batch-ceiling-trope-cap/E1, fake-ad-kernel-source-check/E1, batch-internal-name-lookalikes/E1

- ZaneGPT verdict: weak — The simulation reproduces its own arithmetic and shows that a cap alone doesn't remove lookalikes under uniform sampling, but "keep the screen at every batch size" depends on an untested generator and an unvalidated similarity threshold, so test those before filing it as a lesson.
- Hidden assumption: Names are drawn uniformly and independently from 12³=1728 combinations, and "one syllable apart" is the distance at which people actually confuse two names. The 28/76/91% figures are birthday arithmetic on that model (about 33/1728 per pair, 15, 66 and 120 pairs). No result here shows the real generator draws that way or that users confuse such pairs.
- Would prove it wrong: Run the real name generator, not the uniform sampler, and get a lookalike rate near zero at batch 6. The same result would come from a human or listener check showing one-syllable-apart pairs are not mistaken for each other. The planted-paraphrase test cannot do this, because flagging 1 of 6 is true by construction.
- Break scenario: Take a generator that draws without replacement, or from disjoint per-slot syllable pools, so every pair differs in at least two positions. Run it at batch 6 and 16 and watch the screen reject nothing. That would show the screen is redundant wherever the generator already enforces distance, and the rule's "at every batch size" would have no support.
