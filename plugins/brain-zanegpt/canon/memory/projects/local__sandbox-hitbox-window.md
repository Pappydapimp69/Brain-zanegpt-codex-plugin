## E2 — one hit per swing needs a swing scoped key not an enter transition
- Date: 2026-09-25
- Project: local/sandbox-hitbox-window
- Tags: [hitbox][gamedev][state][events][testing]
- What: The second half of the hitbox seed. sandbox-collision/E2 established that sub-stepping makes collision events burst with CONTACT DURATION, and that the fix is to deduplicate at the producer on the enter-transition. Claim tested: is that enter-transition dedup sufficient for "one hit per swing"? It is not — the enter-transition is the wrong scope in both directions. Too narrow: an engine that respawns the hitbox entity per active frame resets the dedup set, giving 6 hits for one sustained contact. Too coarse in the other axis: an attack whose active window has a GAP (two active slices in one swing, entirely normal for multi-hit-cancel moves) re-enters and fires twice, 2 hits where the design says 1.
- Why built: The parent entry needed to know whether the recorded collision-dedup fix transfers to attacks once the active window is in play. Both failures are invisible to a single-slice, single-frame-lifetime test fixture, which is the fixture anyone writes first.
- Design: Same deterministic sandbox (`sandbox_hitbox_window.py`, experiment 3) — no RNG, no real timing. Sustained contact: target parked 0.1 units inside the hitbox at v=0.001 so it stays overlapped for the whole swing; window [0.0, 6.0), n=8 sub-steps per frame, 8 frames. Three dedup keys compared on identical input: a set scoped to the hitbox ENTITY and recreated each active frame, a set scoped to the SWING, and a bare enter-transition edge detector with no set at all. Then the same three against a two-slice window: active on [0,2) and [4,6).
- Got right: A swing-scoped set is correct in every case run: 1 hit on the sustained contact, 1 hit across the two-slice window. The bare enter-transition also gives 1 on the sustained contact, which is exactly why it passes the fixture people write and ships.
- Where/why it failed: Entity-scoped: 6 hits. The dedup set's lifetime was tied to the hitbox object, and "spawn a hitbox on frame k, despawn it on frame k+1" — a common and otherwise reasonable engine shape — recreates the set every frame, so per-frame dedup is all you get and the sub-step dedup sandbox-collision/E2 asked for is silently undone. Bare enter-transition on the two-slice window: 2 hits. Contact never broke; the ACTIVE GATE broke, so `now && !was` saw a fresh enter that no amount of producer-side care can distinguish from a genuine second contact, because it is not a contact event at all.
- Fix: Scope the dedup set to the swing instance — an id minted when the attack starts and carried across every hitbox entity, every active slice and every sub-step of that attack — and store the victim in it, not the enter edge. Where a move genuinely should hit twice, that is a second swing id (or an explicit per-slice allowance), declared by the move data rather than emerging from a gap in the gate.
- Why this fix: The invariant is "one hit per attack per victim", and a dedup key is only correct when its lifetime matches the invariant's scope. The enter-transition's lifetime is the contact, and the entity's lifetime is the frame; both are shorter than the swing, so both leak, and they leak in opposite situations — which is why fixing either one alone makes the other look fixed. Deriving multi-hit from a gap in the active window is also the same mistake as the parent entry from the other side: it treats the gate's sampling as if it carried design intent.
- Rule of thumb: Dedup scope must match the invariant's scope, not the event's — "one hit per swing" needs a set keyed on the swing id, because an enter-transition expires with the contact (so a gap in the ACTIVE WINDOW re-fires: 2 hits) and a set on the hitbox entity expires with the frame (so a per-frame-respawned hitbox gives 6). Each failure hides the other.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand — built and ran experiment 3 of `sandbox_hitbox_window.py`; the counts 6 / 1 / 1 (sustained contact, entity-scoped vs swing-scoped vs enter-only) and 2 / 1 (two-slice window, enter-only vs swing-scoped) are printed observed output. Assumed: that per-active-frame hitbox respawn and gapped active windows are common engine/design shapes; both were modelled from the concepts, not audited against a shipped engine.

## E1 — hitbox active window must be substepped with position
- Date: 2026-09-25
- Project: local/sandbox-hitbox-window
- Tags: [hitbox][gamedev][physics][testing][determinism]
- What: Seeding the `hitbox` domain, which had 0 memory entries (`brain train new-domain hitbox`). A hitbox is not just a collision volume — it is a volume ANDed with an active window in swing time, and that second gate has its own sampling error. Claim tested: does sandbox-collision/E1's tunneling fix (sub-step the movement) carry over to a hitbox? It does not. Sub-stepping POSITION while reading the active gate once per frame does not merely stay wrong, it changes the SIGN of the error — it converts missed hits into phantom hits (8/16 false positives in the probe), because a gate sampled at frame start silently widens the active window to the whole frame. Attacks then land after they have ended.
- Why built: `brain train new-domain hitbox` reported the domain empty, and the canon's 19 collision lessons are all about geometry and continuity, none about the activation window that makes a hitbox a hitbox. The obvious move was to assume the recorded tunneling fix transfers. It had to be run, not reasoned.
- Design: `sandbox_hitbox_window.py` plus `probe.py` / `rule.py` — 1D, closed form, no RNG and no real timing. Stationary attacker hitbox (hx=0, hw=0.5), target hurtbox tw=0.5 moving at v ticks^-1, active window a continuous slice [t_a, t_b) of swing time that is allowed to be SHORTER than one tick (the ordinary case: 1-3 attack frames authored at 60 Hz, simulated at 20 Hz). Ground truth is the analytic intersection of the contact interval ((hx-R-x0)/v, (hx+R-x0)/v) with [t_a, t_b). Four detectors compared against it: per-frame `discrete`, `substep_pos` (position sub-stepped, gate read at frame start), `substep_both` (both sub-stepped), and the analytic solve. Swept speed 0.25..16 across four window lengths (3.0, 1.0, 0.667, 0.333 ticks), then swept sub-step count n = 1..8191.
- Got right: Sub-stepping BOTH is the right direction — 27/28 against truth versus 23/28 for per-frame and 25/28 for position-only. The dedup half held too: an enter-transition dedup scoped to the swing gives exactly 1 hit on a sustained contact where a set scoped to a per-frame-respawned hitbox entity gives 6.
- Where/why it failed: Two failures, and the second is the one worth keeping. (1) `substep_pos` — which is literally "apply the recorded tunneling fix to an attack" — produced 8 phantom hits out of 16 probe cases where truth was no-hit: window [1.00, 1.20), v=4 and v=8, the target arriving at the hitbox centre at t=1.5, well after the window closed, and it connects. Position-only sub-stepping is worse than the naive check, not better, because its error direction flips from "my attack whiffed" to "I got hit by an attack that was already over". (2) `substep_both` is still not correct at any fixed n, and the quantity n must resolve is NOT the frame and NOT the window length. Window 1.0 ticks with v=3 needs n >= 1003; the window-length rule predicts n >= 2. Testing the two candidate rules across 15 true-hit cases: "resolve the window" holds 8/15, "resolve window INTERSECT contact interval" holds 15/15. That intersection is a grazing hit at the window edge and shrinks without bound — 0.1 ticks needs n>=3, 0.01 needs n>=27, 0.001 needs n>=251, 0.0001 needs n>=2503.
- Fix: Solve the window analytically, do not sample it. Compute the contact interval in swing time and intersect it with [t_a, t_b); a non-empty intersection is the hit, and its start is the hit time. Where sub-stepping is unavoidable, sub-step the ACTIVE GATE at the same resolution as the position — never the position alone — and size n from the smallest window-INTERSECT-contact duration the game can produce, not from the window length or the frame.
- Why this fix: The analytic intersection has no resolution parameter, so there is nothing left to tune wrong, and it yields the hit TIME (which a trade/priority resolution needs anyway) rather than a boolean. The reason sampling cannot be made safe here is that the two intervals are independent: a designer shortening an attack's active frames and a systems programmer raising a projectile's speed each shrink the intersection, and neither is looking at the other's number. The position-only variant is worse than doing nothing because its failure is a false positive in a system players read as unfair, and false positives do not show up in a "my attack whiffed" bug report.
- Rule of thumb: A hitbox is a volume ANDed with a time window, so it has TWO sampling errors, not one — sub-stepping the position and not the window flips misses into phantom hits that land after the attack ended. Solve window-intersect-contact analytically; if you must sample, sample the gate at the position's resolution and size it from the smallest intersection, which no window length or frame rate bounds.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand — built and ran `sandbox_hitbox_window.py`, `probe.py` and `rule.py`; all numbers quoted above (8/16 phantom hits; 23/28, 25/28, 27/28 agreement; n>=1003 for window 1.0 at v=3; 8/15 vs 15/15 rule agreement; n >= 3 / 27 / 251 / 2503 for intersections of 0.1 / 0.01 / 0.001 / 0.0001 ticks) are printed observed output, not estimates. Assumed: that 1D generalizes to 2D/3D hitboxes — the time axis is the one being sampled and it is 1D regardless, but the contact interval for a rotating or animated hitbox was not modelled. Also assumed: that engines commonly read the active gate once per frame; this was modelled as a plausible implementation, not audited against a real engine's source.
