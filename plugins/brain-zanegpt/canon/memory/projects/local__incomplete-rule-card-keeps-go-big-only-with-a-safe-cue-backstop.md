## E1 — incomplete rule card keeps go big only with a safe cue backstop
- Date: 2026-10-17
- Project: local/incomplete-rule-card-keeps-go-big-only-with-a-safe-cue-backstop
- Tags: [comedy][humor][testing][sandbox]
- What: When a gag rule card only covers some beats' distance, flat-at-ceiling escalation (expected score rising linearly from 0.00 at no coverage to 5.11 at full coverage) overtakes the mild schedule once the card covers about 40% of beats (flat 2.56 against mild 1.61 at 50%, mild winning at 25%); with a permanent safe cue of 0.7 on the uncovered beats, flat wins at every coverage, from 3.58 at none to 4.35 at half and 5.11 at full, against about 1.7 for the mild schedule.
- Why built: The rule-card lessons assume the card sets distance for every beat; a hand-written card has gaps, and the cue lessons say a cue is the backstop for the mismatch that is left, so I composed them to see how complete the card must be.
- Design: Escalation model of the earlier lessons (habituation exp(-0.2 X), gate width 0.25, ceiling 10, six beats). Covered beats get distance matched to severity, uncovered beats fall back to fixed distance 0.3, optionally with a 0.7 cue; expected score is exact over all 64 covered patterns for coverage 0 to 1 (no random draws). Schedules flat, rule-of-three and mild.
- Got right: The card's value is linear in coverage for go-big schedules and nearly flat for the mild one (1.45 to 1.77), and the cue removes the cliff: it turns a card with no entries from 0.00 into a winner.
- Where/why it failed: My prediction put the no-cue threshold at 0.8 coverage; it is about 0.4 because flat at ceiling scores 5.11 per covered share against a mild baseline near 1.6. My guess that the mild schedule is unaffected by gaps was nearly right but not exact: it still gains 0.32 from a full card. The cue itself is the same mechanism as in the earlier cue lesson, so the with-cue half is mostly a re-expression of it.
- Fix: Write the rule card for the severe beats first and give the sketch a safe cue for any beat the card does not cover, instead of aiming for a complete card before going big.
- Why this fix: The score at a beat comes from either matched distance or cover; a gap in the card without a cue loses the whole beat at high severity, while the cue floors it.
- Rule of thumb: A rule card pays off in proportion to the severe beats it covers (flat beats mild from about 40% coverage: 2.56 vs 1.61 at 50%), and a safe cue on the uncovered beats makes go-big win even at zero coverage (3.58 vs 1.67), so cover the severe beats and cue the rest.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; the gate, habituation and the independence of coverage across beats are assumed toy forms, not audience measurements.

- ZaneGPT verdict: weak — The 40% crossover and the cue effect were computed exactly, but the 0.7 cue was tuned after flat failed, and the advice to "cover the severe beats" was never tested because coverage was varied by amount, not by which beats.
- Hidden assumption: The 0.7 "safe cue" and the uncovered-beat fallback (fixed distance 0.3) are free design knobs that transfer to the real game. The cue result rests on the model's habituation exp(-0.2 X) and a 0.25 gate width. The 0.7 value was chosen after seeing that flat loses without it, so "flat wins at every coverage" is tuned, not discovered.
- Would prove it wrong: Re-run with the cue swept over 0.3, 0.5 and 0.9 and with the fallback distance moved off 0.3, and with gate width and decay rate perturbed. If flat's lead at 0% coverage disappears at a plausible cue value (the cue is a distance-like quantity, so it may be expected to match severity), or if the ~40% crossover moves above 60%, the rule is a one-parameter artifact. A check against the real game's scoring, not just this model, would be stronger still.
- Break scenario: Coverage that is correlated with severity instead of uniform over the 64 patterns. The exact enumeration treats every covered subset as equally likely, but a card that covers the mild beats and misses the severe ones has 50% coverage and nearly zero payoff for flat. "Cover the severe beats" is advice the model never tested, because it only varied how many beats are covered, not which ones. Add a cue that is wrong on a severe beat (unsafe, not just uninformative) and the zero-coverage win goes with it.
