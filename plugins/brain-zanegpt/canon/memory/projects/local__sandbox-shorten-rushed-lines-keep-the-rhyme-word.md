## E1 — sandbox shorten rushed lines keep the rhyme word
- Date: 2026-10-15
- Project: local/sandbox-shorten-rushed-lines-keep-the-rhyme-word
- Tags: [sandbox][songwriting][rhyme][suno]
- What: Four 11-word-per-line stanzas written as AABB, ABAB, ABBA and XXAA were put through three fixes for a rushed vocal line (keep the first 5 words, keep the last 5 words, hyphenate every word) and re-read for rhyme shape with an orthographic last-vowel-group key.
- Why built: The hyphenate kernel's fix is shorter lines of 4 to 6 words, and the rhyme-shape kernel says the shape sets the emotional motion; the question is whether the length fix silently rewrites the shape.
- Design: Rhyme key = final word from its last vowel group to the end; letters assigned in order of first appearance; shape table AABB/ABAB/ABBA/ABCC(=XXAA). Words per line and vowel-group syllables per line measured for each variant. Hyphenation splits each word at vowel-group boundaries.
- Got right: Cutting from the front and keeping the last 5 words preserved 4 of 4 shapes while words per line fell from 11.1 to 5.0 and syllables from 15.8 to 7.1; hyphenation preserved 4 of 4 shapes.
- Where/why it failed: Cutting the end of each line kept 0 of 4 shapes (the rhyme words were dropped) and hyphenation did not reduce words per line (11.1) or syllables (15.8) at all, which matches suno-builder/E11 that a hyphen fix does not move the count. Keep-last-5 preserving the shape is nearly by construction, since the rhyme word is the last word; the real risk it leaves is lost sense, which this model cannot see.
- Fix: When a line is shortened to cure rushing, cut from the front and keep the end word or phrase that carries the rhyme; treat hyphenation as a timing hint on words, not as a way to shorten, and re-read the shape after any edit.
- Why this fix: Rhyme shape lives on line-final words, so edits that keep line ends cannot change it, while edits that remove ends rewrite it into a different scheme with a different emotional motion.
- Rule of thumb: Shorten a rushed line from the front so the rhyme word survives (4 of 4 shapes kept, 11.1 to 5.0 words) and never truncate line ends (0 of 4 kept); hyphenation keeps the shape but changes neither words nor syllable count, so it is not a length fix.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand: the sandbox ran and printed PASS first time on my own four stanzas. The rhyme key is spelling-based and the hyphenator is a vowel-group splitter (suno-builder/E8 shows such splitters miscount), and Suno's actual delivery was not tested; only the structural effect on rhyme shape is claimed.
