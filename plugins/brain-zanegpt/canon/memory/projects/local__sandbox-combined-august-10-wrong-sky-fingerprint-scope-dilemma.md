## E1 — sandbox combined august 10 wrong sky fingerprint scope dilemma
- Date: 2026-08-29
- Project: local/sandbox-combined-august-10-wrong-sky-fingerprint-scope-dilemma
- Tags: [testing][determinism][method][process]
- What: A deterministic sandbox (fixed scripted command sequence, no RNG, no wall-clock) running a dodge/i-frame gate correct and broken, fingerprinting the resulting trace at two scopes, and measuring both wrong-sky#E17's invisibility property and august-10#E16's golden-inversion property against the same defect.
- Why built: wrong-sky#E17 records that a broken dodge-gate fingerprints byte-identical to a correct one under replay (defect invisible). august-10#E16 records a different property of golden fingerprints — a golden recorded after the defect bakes it in, so the eventual FIX fails the test. Both are about the same artifact but were filed as separate lessons; this tests whether one defect can carry both properties, and what actually determines which one you get.
- Design: A scripted tick sequence with dodge windows and strikes; `gate_correct=False` reproduces wrong-sky#E17's leak (strikes landing during i-frames). The trace is fingerprinted at two scopes: tick cadence only, and tick+hp. Invisibility is measured as broken-vs-fixed fingerprint equality; inversion is measured by capturing a golden during the defect era and comparing it against the post-fix fingerprint.
- Got right: The first construction did NOT reproduce wrong-sky#E17's invisibility — including hp in the trace meant the leaked damage moved the fingerprint. Rather than declaring the composition confirmed on the inversion half alone, a second iteration scoped the fingerprint the way a real determinism suite does (cadence, not combat outcome), which isolated the actual controlling variable.
- Where/why it failed: The two properties turn out to be mutually exclusive functions of one variable — fingerprint scope. Scoped to tick cadence, broken and fixed fingerprint identically (7b08c0da570c030e both), reproducing wrong-sky#E17's invisibility while 3 strikes leak and hp diverges 50 vs 80. Scoped to tick+hp, the fingerprints differ and a golden captured during the defect era (3630f49b3a44d9fd) rejects the post-fix run (4867455df3227896), reproducing august-10#E16's inversion. A fingerprint cannot simultaneously catch this bug and accept its fix.
- Fix: Stop treating "does the fingerprint move" as the question and treat fingerprint SCOPE as the design decision it is — a fingerprint whose scope excludes a subsystem gives that subsystem no coverage at all (wrong-sky#E17), and one whose scope includes it converts every legitimate fix into a required golden re-record (august-10#E16). Pair a narrow, stable cadence fingerprint with per-subsystem purpose-scoped assertions (recursion#E4's separate-determinism-test idiom), and record each golden's provenance so a defect-era capture is identifiable as such.
- Why this fix: The two lessons are not two hazards to avoid independently; they are the two exits from a single dilemma, and neither can be escaped by tuning the fingerprint. Only splitting the guarantee — cadence stability in the shared golden, correctness in a dedicated assertion the golden does not mediate — gets both properties at once.
- Rule of thumb: A golden replay fingerprint's scope decides which failure you get, not whether you get one — exclude a subsystem and it is invisible (wrong-sky#E17), include it and the defect-era golden rejects the eventual fix (august-10#E16); the two are mutually exclusive on one artifact, so put correctness in a purpose-scoped assertion and keep the shared fingerprint narrow and provenance-tracked.
