## E1 — derived link beats sidecar and noun check until link noise passes a threshold
- Date: 2026-10-17
- Project: local/derived-link-beats-sidecar-and-noun-check-until-link-noise-passes-a-threshold
- Tags: [game-ads][testing][model][sandbox]
- What: Toy IPM times D1 recut ranking over 2000 six-variant sheets with three fidelity columns: a derived-from-game link read with noise, a hand-kept sidecar that goes stale for a quarter of its entries after renames, and an any-new-noun flag. The derived link picked the true best 100% of the time with no read noise, the sidecar 66.7%, the noun flag 13.5% (chance), and IPM alone 13.2%.
- Why built: The derived-link lesson was audited as built in (the check reads the same field that sets true D1), and the sidecar lesson only compared against a perfect derivation. This asks how wrong the derived link may be before the hand-kept sidecar is the better source.
- Design: Fidelity f in {0.2, 0.5, 1.0}; IPM = 10 x hook x (1.6 - 0.6 f); true D1 = 0.15 + 0.25 f; each column estimates f and the pick is argmax IPM x D1(estimate), scored against the true argmax. Derived noise = chance a read returns a random level (0, 0.2, 0.5, 0.8); sidecar drift 0.25 (stale entry copies another variant's value). Seeded, stdlib only.
- Got right: With zero noise the derived link was exact and the sidecar lost a third of the picks at 25% drift; with zero drift the sidecar was exact too (1.000), so the sidecar loses only through drift, not by design. The noun flag stayed at chance everywhere because it is independent of fidelity.
- Where/why it failed: Nothing in the claim failed, but the clean derived result is partly built in (the link reads f directly). The informative part is the noisy half: derived fell to 0.672 at noise 0.2 (about level with the sidecar at 0.642), 0.362 at 0.5 and 0.217 at 0.8, below the drifted sidecar.
- Fix: Prefer the derived link only while its read error stays well under about a fifth of entries; past that, a hand sidecar kept in sync is no worse, so measure the link's error against a few hand-checked variants before trusting it over the sidecar.
- Why this fix: A derived link is only better than a stale table when it is mostly right; the sweep shows the advantage is gone between 20% and 50% read noise, so a small hand-checked sample bounds it cheaply.
- Rule of thumb: A derived-from-game fidelity link beats a drifting hand sidecar for IPM times D1 ranking only while the link misreads fewer than about 20 to 50 percent of variants (100% exact at 0 noise, 67% vs 64% at 20%, 36% vs 66% at 50% in this toy model), and a new-noun flag never beats chance, so validate the link on a few known variants first.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; the fidelity levels, D1 and IPM functions, noise and drift models are assumed toy forms, not measured ad data.

- ZaneGPT verdict: weak — The ordering at the extremes (exact link wins, noun flag at chance) is trivial by construction, and the "20 to 50 percent" crossover comes from one noise model and one drift rate. Treat it as a direction and not a number, and run the sweeps before quoting the threshold.
- Hidden assumption: Noise is a uniform random-level misread, and the sidecar's staleness is a fixed 25% copy-another-variant drift. Real link errors are probably systematic (a rename or refactor breaks the same variants, or all of them) and not independent per read, and real drift varies with how often the team renames things. The 20–50% crossover therefore describes this toy's two chosen error rates and not the link in general.
- Would prove it wrong: A rerun with the sidecar drift swept (0.1, 0.25, 0.5) and the link noise swept on the same grid, where the crossover moves from about 0.2–0.5 to something unrelated to those numbers (for example, derived already below the sidecar at 0.1 noise against 0.5 drift, or still ahead at 0.8 noise against 0.1 drift). The same applies if correlated misreads, where one bad read hits every variant of a sheet, drop derived below the sidecar at noise 0.2. Either would show the threshold depends on the pairing and isn't a property of the link.
- Break scenario: Make the derived link's errors systematic and biased. Instead of a random level, a misread always returns a neighboring or lower fidelity level, or fails on the same 30% of variants every time. Run that against a sidecar whose drift is 0.1. Also add a three-level-only variant set with tied IPM×D1 scores, since noise scored by exact-argmax on 2000 sheets of six variants over only 3 fidelity levels gives ties and coarse resolution that could flatter or punish either column.
