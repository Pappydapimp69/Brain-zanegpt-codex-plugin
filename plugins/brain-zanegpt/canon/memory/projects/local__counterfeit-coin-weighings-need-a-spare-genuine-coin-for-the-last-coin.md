## E1 — counterfeit coin weighings need a spare genuine coin for the last coin
- Date: 2026-10-17
- Project: local/counterfeit-coin-weighings-need-a-spare-genuine-coin-for-the-last-coin
- Tags: [coin-weighing][search][testing][sandbox]
- What: An exhaustive adaptive search over balance-scale strategies for one odd coin (heavier or lighter, unknown) found the largest solvable counts of 3 and 12 coins in 2 and 3 weighings with no spare genuine coin, and 4 and 13 with one spare, matching the classic formulas (3 to the k, minus 3, over 2) and (3 to the k, minus 1, over 2). 13 coins in 3 weighings is unsolvable without a spare, and the first weighing of the 12-coin solution is 4 against 4 (5 against 4 for 13 with a spare).
- Why built: New domain seed. Weighing puzzles are a standard example of planning under an information budget, and the common belief that 3 weighings handle 13 coins is easy to state wrongly; I wanted an executable check that finds the exact ceiling instead of trusting the formula.
- Design: State of untested, possibly-heavy, possibly-light and known-genuine coins; each weighing chooses how many coins of each class go on each pan, padded from the genuine pool; the three outcomes lead to new states, with eliminated coins becoming genuine; recursive search with memoization and an outcome-count bound. Only the numbers of coins matter, so no random draws are used.
- Got right: The search reproduces the closed-form ceilings for both settings and the 4-4 opening, and shows that the one spare coin is worth exactly one extra coin (12 to 13, 3 to 4).
- Where/why it failed: My first version mixed the untested and suspect states and returned nonsense (N of 0 and 15); the clean four-class state with explicit pans repaired it. The ceilings are known results, so the sandbox confirms rather than discovers them, and only k of 2 and 3 were searched (k of 4 would need pruning).
- Fix: When planning a decision tree under a fixed number of three-way tests, compute the ceiling as (3 to the k, minus 3, over 2) unless a known-good item is available, and then add one, instead of using the raw information bound of 3 to the k over 2.
- Why this fix: Each coin has two possible faults, so the information bound is 2N outcomes, but the last coin needs a reference to tell heavy from light, which costs one coin of capacity.
- Rule of thumb: With a three-way test and k rounds you can resolve at most (3 to the k, minus 3) over 2 items when the fault direction is unknown and no reference exists (12 coins in 3 weighings, 13 impossible), and one reference item buys exactly one more; budget the reference first.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox for k of 2 and 3, which reproduces the classic result; k of 4 and larger and the generalization to non-balance tests were not run.

- ZaneGPT verdict: weak — the counts at k=2 and 3 match the classic formulas, but the rule is extrapolated to every k and to a single spare from two data points, and it ignores the "no odd coin exists" variant and the case of multiple spares.
- Hidden assumption: The rule treats "one reference item buys exactly one more" as general, but the search only covered k=2 and k=3 with a spare count of 0 or 1. A single spare was never tested beyond two data points, and k=2 gives 3→4 while the formula (3^k−1)/2 is simply asserted from those two cases. The rule also assumes the odd coin must be identified, not merely detected, and that the odd coin always exists. "Unknown direction" does not mean "unknown whether it exists."
- Would prove it wrong: An exhaustive run at k=4 (or k=1) that does not give 39 coins with no spare and 40 with one. Also, a run with two or more spares that gives a bigger jump than +1 would refute "exactly one more". Another way is to allow the verdict "no odd coin" as a valid outcome and find that the counts change (the classic result then drops to (3^k−3)/2 for the spare-free case, or similar).
- Break scenario: k=4 with 0, 1 and 2 spare coins. The rule predicts 39, 40 and (by its own logic) 40 or 41. If the 2-spare case still gives 40, "budget the reference first" is misleading, since only the first reference counts and the rule's wording "buys exactly one more" would need a "first" qualifier.
