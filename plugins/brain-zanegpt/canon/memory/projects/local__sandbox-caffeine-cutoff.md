## E1 — caffeine cutoff is a half life and dose claim not a clock time
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/sandbox-caffeine-cutoff
- Tags: [chronobiology][caffeine][sleep][simulation][testing]
- What: Seeded the new CHRONOBIOLOGY domain by modelling the common caffeine cutoff rule ("nothing after 2pm", "nothing within 6 hours of bed") with first-order absorption and elimination, to see what the rule really claims.
- Why built: Sleep advice states cutoffs as clock times; the brain had sleep kernels (injury risk under 8 h) but nothing on stimulant timing, so the domain had no records to detect gaps against.
- Design: One-compartment model, amount = dose * ka/(ka-ke) * (exp(-ke t) - exp(-ka t)), absorption half-life 0.25 h assumed, half-lives 2.5 / 5 / 10 h taken from the cited adult range. Six scenarios: same clock rule across half-lives, dose-blind lead time, residual-target cutoff vs closed form, step-size traps, repeated daily dosing, and a saturable-elimination breaker.
- Got right: Predicted 9.2 / 30.2 / 55.0 mg left at 23:00 for 100 mg at 14:00 and got exactly that (6.0x spread); predicted 16 h of lead for 400 mg to match 100 mg at 6 h and got 16.0 h; predicted the bisected numeric cutoff within 0.5 h of the closed form and got 0.37 h; predicted forward Euler at 1 h steps under-predicts by about 9% and got -9.1%.
- Where/why it failed: Prediction (b) of the break scenario was wrong: I expected the residual fraction to stay dose-independent within 2% up to 400 mg under saturable elimination, but with the illustrative Km of 1000 mg it was 20% higher at 400 mg than at 100 mg (0.370 vs 0.308), and only a Km of 100000 mg gave 0.2%. Linearity in the dietary range is therefore an assumption about an unmeasured Km. First sandbox draft also had two harness bugs (a lead-time search started where absorption makes the amount zero, and a half-life scan divided by ten twice); both fixed before the final run.
- Fix: Express the rule as a residual target, cutoff = bedtime - half_life * log2(dose / target), with bedtime back-solved from wake time + needed sleep, per-person half-life, and a printed previous-day result when the answer is negative. Keep the target an explicit assumption, and mark results above a few hundred mg as outside the linear model.
- Why this fix: It makes the two hidden parameters visible and editable; a clock rule is only correct for one half-life and one dose (a 14:00 cutoff passes the 200 mg / 25 mg target only for half-lives up to 2.7 h), while the formula degrades gracefully and reproduces the numeric search.
- Rule of thumb: Never ship a caffeine cutoff as a bare clock time or hours-before-bed number; state it as half-life, dose and residual target (9 h later, 100 mg leaves 9 / 30 / 55 mg across half-lives 2.5 / 5 / 10 h), and treat linear scaling as valid only in the dietary dose range.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand: all numbers above come from the shipped sandbox run (RESULT: PASS). Half-life ranges and the 400 mg study conclusion were read first-hand from Europe PMC records for PMID 24235903 and open full texts PMC13562836, PMC13516273 and PMC8914174; the 25 mg target, 15 min absorption half-life and Km values are stated assumptions, and the review figures are secondary sources.

It does compute rather than assume: residuals come from a one-compartment body() function and the bisected cutoff is compared with the closed form half_life * log2(dose / target). The numbers in the Rule of thumb (9 / 30 / 55 mg, 6.0x, 14:00 passing only up to about 2.7 h) are reproduced by that model. Two weaker spots. First, the S5a wake-up carryover check compares the sim with a recomputed sum that uses the same body() and the same dose times, so it cannot disagree and adds no independence. Second, the Rule's closing clause (linear scaling valid in the dietary dose range) is not tested but set by the parameter choice: S5b passes its under-2% condition only because Km 100000 mg was picked, while the illustrative Km 1000 mg shows 20% at 400 mg, and Km is unmeasured. The claim is about real human caffeine pharmacokinetics (half-lives, absorption half-life, target residual, sleep effect), and only a hand-written model with assumed parameters was run against it; the literature half-life range and the 400 mg study are cited but not computed.
