## E1 — leitner demotion rule depends on whether a lapse erases memory
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/sandbox-leitner-demotion
- Tags: [memorization][teaching][proxy][testing]
- What: A deterministic Leitner-box model (20 cards, 120 days, box gaps 1/2/4/8/16/32 days, hidden per-card strength, recall succeeds when the gap is within strength, success grows strength by 1 + gain * min(gap/strength, 1)) comparing two miss rules, reset-to-box-1 versus drop-one-box, against a review-everything-daily baseline.
- Why built: To seed the new MEMORIZATION domain with a tested claim instead of folklore. The common advice is that a miss should send a card back to box 1; the opposite camp says drop one box. I wanted to know which is cheaper and whether the answer is stable.
- Design: Pure stdlib, no RNG, no clock. Strength starts at 1, 1.5, 2, 3 or 5 days (five card types repeated four times). A miss sets strength to max(start, keep * strength) with keep 0.5 (savings) or 0 (full wipe). Gain swept 0.2 to 1.4 in steps of 0.1, plus a gain 0.0 control. Metrics: total reviews, misses, cards still recallable on day 120. Predictions were written in the file header before each run.
- Got right: The massed baseline held: daily review cost at least 2.03x either box rule at every gain and 17x once nothing misses. Misses disappear exactly at gain 1.0 and above, where strength grows by at least the doubling of the gap, and there both rules tie at 140 reviews. A wipe learner never made drop-one-box cheaper than reset by more than 12 percent.
- Where/why it failed: My first two predictions were wrong. Run 1 (gain 1.5) gave zero misses, so the rule comparison was empty. Run 2 predicted drop-one-box saves at least 15 percent; it saved 0.5 percent at gain 0.6 and cost 33 percent MORE at gain 0.9 (660 reviews and 300 misses against 496 and 176) because the card oscillates around the box it cannot hold. A fixed "always demote one box" or "always reset" rule is therefore not supported; the winner flips with the learner.
- Fix: Replaced the single-winner claim with a regime claim and swept gain finely (run 3). Result: below gain 1.0 the sign of the review difference flips (0.88x at gain 0.2, 1.44x at 0.7 under savings), and in the slowest regime drop-one-box left 4 of 20 cards unrecallable at the end.
- Why this fix: The sweep shows where each rule holds instead of averaging the two regimes together, and the end-of-term recall count keeps a cheap rule from hiding forgotten cards. Gain 0.0 behaves like gain 0.2 to 0.3, the no-difficulty-bonus control, so the mechanism is the growth rate, not the rule.
- Rule of thumb: Check the miss rate first: when recalled cards strengthen at least as fast as the gap doubles, the demotion rule does not matter and daily review wastes about 17x the effort; below that, test reset against drop-one-box on your own learners and judge by reviews plus end-of-term recall, since the winner flipped between gains 0.5 and 0.7 in this model.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox three times (runs 1 and 2 recorded as FAIL in the file, run 3 PASS). The strength-growth law is a modelling choice stated under Design; the sandbox uses no human recall data, and only five distinct card types are repeated, so thresholds like 1.0 are properties of this toy.
