## E1 — grid validator shares planners sampling blind spot
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/sandbox-grid-validator-sampling
- Tags: [algorithm][pathfinding][testing][gamedev][process]
- What: Composed pappydapimp69/dog/E34 (an obstacle grid built by testing only each cell's CENTRE misses a prop smaller than the cell; and "no path to a blocked goal" must not fall back to straight steering) with sandbox-path-smoothing-validation/E1 (validate a smoothed path against an independent continuous padded check, never the smoother's own abstraction) and sandbox-dog-pathfinding/E1 (string-pull smoothing can cut a corner through an obstacle). Question: when the regression check for a smoothed path is a grid-cell lookup, does fixing the grid (dog/E34) also make that check trustworthy, or does the grid-based check inherit the grid's blind spot exactly as smoothing-validation/E1 predicts? A deterministic sandbox shows the check inherits the blind spot while the grid is wrong, and stays trustworthy after the grid fix only while its own sampling step is no coarser than one cell.
- Why built: dog/E34 treats the centre-sampling miss as a planner bug and fixes the grid; smoothing-validation/E1 treats the validator-shares-abstraction hazard with a raw radius and a vertices-only check. Neither says what a grid-lookup validator does when the grid itself is the broken abstraction, nor whether the grid fix retires the need for an independent check.
- Design: stdlib-only sandbox (zero RNG, zero clock). 10x10 grid, cell 2, one circular obstacle of padded radius 1.0 swept over 20x20=400 positions inside one cell period; A* (8-connected, no corner cutting) from (1,9) to (19,9), greedy string-pull with a grid line-of-sight, then three checks: G (grid-cell lookup along the smoothed path, same abstraction as the planner), vertices-only, and C (continuous segment-vs-padded-disc distance). Scenarios vary the grid builder (centre-sample vs cell-square-vs-circle overlap) and the sampling step of the smoother and of G (0.1, 2.0, 3.0, 6.0), plus a goal placed at the obstacle centre (naive fallback vs nearest-open-cell).
- Got right: The cell-square-vs-circle grid from dog/E34 is conservative: with sampling step 0.1 or 2.0 (one cell) the smoothed paths had 0 C violations in all 400 layouts, and the nearest-open-cell fix gave 0 of 400 violations where the naive straight-line fallback violated in 400 of 400.
- Where/why it failed: With the centre-sampled grid, 95 of 400 positions (23.8 percent) left NO cell blocked. There G reported 0 violations in all 400 layouts while C found a real clip in 120 smoothed paths, so 120 false-green certifications (even the raw A* path clipped in 75). The vertices-only check also reported 0. With the corrected grid but a G and smoother sampling step above the cell size, G stayed at 0 while C found 19 clips at step 3.0 and 380 at step 6.0. A goal-inside-obstacle fallback is the one case G would still flag, since the goal cell is blocked in the corrected grid.
- Fix: Treat the obstacle grid as a unit that needs its own ground-truth test: build it with region overlap (cell square vs padded circle), never one sample per cell, and sample any grid-lookup segment check at a step no larger than one cell (preferably a fraction of it). Even then keep one continuous padded-geometry check (segment distance vs padded radius) in the regression suite, run against an obstacle deliberately placed at a cell corner so no cell centre falls inside its padded radius.
- Why this fix: The grid is the planner's abstraction and the grid-lookup validator is a second reader of that same abstraction, so any error in how the grid was built reaches the planner and the validator together and cancels out of the comparison; only a check that never touches the grid can disagree. Region overlap removes the centre-sampling miss, and a step at most one cell removes the second place the lookup can skip a blocked cell.
- Rule of thumb: A grid-cell lookup is not independent evidence about a smoothed path: it shares the grid's blind spots and its own sampling step. Fixing the grid (cell-square overlap, not centre sampling) makes it safe only when it is sampled at most one cell apart; keep one continuous padded segment-distance check and a probe obstacle at a cell corner. Tested here on one circular obstacle at one radius and cell size, not every obstacle shape.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand: built and ran the sandbox (deterministic, output byte-identical across two separate process runs): 95 of 400 blind positions, 120 false-green certifications, 0 violations on the corrected grid at steps 0.1 and 2.0, 19 and 380 violations at steps 3.0 and 6.0, goal-inside 400 vs 0. The first-run prediction missed on two points, recorded below and left unedited in the sandbox header. The scale of the dog/E34 incident (a real hydrant in the live game) was only read from the entry; the sandbox uses its own synthetic geometry.
