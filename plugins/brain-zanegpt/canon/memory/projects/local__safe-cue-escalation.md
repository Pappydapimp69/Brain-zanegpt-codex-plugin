## E1 — second safe cue lets a grounded sketch escalate but cannot rescue a beat with no violation
- Date: 2026-10-15
- Project: local/safe-cue-escalation
- Tags: [comedy][humor][narrative][sandbox]
- What: In the distance-gated escalation model a second, non-distance safe cue (a competing norm that says the harm is fine) lets a grounded sketch go big again: at fixed distance 0.3 flat-at-ceiling scored 0.02 and a mild schedule won (13.81); adding a safe cue of 0.7 made flat win (51.12). A safe cue with no violation scored 0.00 on every schedule.
- Why built: Train kernel pick: the severe-harm distance dial and the escalation-distance lesson were a strong pair, and the absurd-premise checklist (wrong cue plus safe cue) had never been run with them. Tests whether distance is the only way to buy escalation room or whether any safe cue counts.
- Design: Escalation model from the escalation-distance lesson (habituation exp(-0.2 X), X += 1/(1 + I/5), ceiling 10, six beats, schedules flat, linear, geometric, three, mild). Gate = exp(-(max(0, W - d - c)/0.25)^2) with W = I/C, d distance, c a competing-norm safe cue; value = habituation * W * gate, and W is zeroed when the violation is removed.
- Got right: Control reproduced the earlier flip (fixed d 0.3 gives mild best, flat 0.02; matched distance gives flat best). The checklist's two halves behaved as claimed: safe cue alone gives nothing without a violation, violation alone is gated away when severe.
- Where/why it failed: The composition is partly true by construction: c enters the gate additively with d, so safe cue substituting for distance is an assumption of the model, not a finding. The magnitudes (51.12 vs 13.81) depend on the gate width 0.25 and on c = 0.7. The sandbox says nothing about real audiences, and the source study has a failed replication.
- Fix: When a grounded or realistic comic piece needs a big peak beat, add a safe cue (competing norm, low stakes for the victim, or a clearly deserved target) rather than only cutting severity; and do not treat any cue as enough: the beat still needs an actual violation.
- Why this fix: Benign violation needs a reason-it-is-okay at the same moment as the wrongness; distance is one such reason and a competing norm is another, so either can scale with severity, but neither creates the violation.
- Rule of thumb: Escalation room is bought by safe cues of any kind, not only distance: at fixed grounded distance a 0.7 safe cue turned flat-at-ceiling from 0.02 into the winner (51.12), but a safe cue with no violation scored 0.00, so check both halves before escalating.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox below (4 scenarios, deterministic). The additive form of the safe cue and the gate width are my assumptions; no human audience data was involved.
