## E1 — two pass rhyme letters assonance mush boundary and closure distance
- Date: 2026-10-11
- Project: local/two-pass-rhyme-letters-assonance-mush-boundary-and-closure-distance
- Tags: [lyrical-composition][rhyme-tooling][algorithm][testing]
- What: Re-verifies the two-pass rhyme letter assignment on five hand-built stanzas and finds its boundary: with no resolved rhyme at all the lowercase assonance pass collapses four lines into one letter, and the one-pass mislabel also changes the closure-distance reading of the scheme from [2,2] to [1,1,1].
- Why built: To turn the prose-only link to the two-pass kernel into a declared, re-verified edge, using fresh stanzas instead of the single day/way/gaze case already recorded.
- Design: Words are vowel-and-coda pairs. One-pass gives a line the earlier letter of any same-vowel line. Two-pass gives uppercase letters to same vowel and coda groups, then lowercase to same-vowel groups among unlettered lines. A bounded variant pairs assonance one-to-one, nearest first. Closure distance is the line gap to the previous same letter.
- Got right: Two-pass recovered the intended scheme on 4 of 5 stanzas, including day, gaze, way, daze as ABAB where one-pass gave AAAA; closure distance read [2,2] for ABAB and [1,1] for AABB, so the mislabel flipped the shape reading to [1,1,1].
- Where/why it failed: The all-assonance stanza (day, gaze, fate, main) came out AAAA under two-pass, the same mush one level down, and the bounded variant gave AABB, still not the intended ABCD. One-pass recovered 3 of 5, not the 2 predicted, because it only fails when assonance precedes a perfect partner. The lexicon is hand-built, not a pronouncing dictionary.
- Fix: Keep two-pass but bound the second pass: pair assonance one-to-one, or require a stricter match than shared vowel, and report all-lowercase stanzas as unresolved instead of a scheme.
- Why this fix: Uppercase and lowercase only carry information if the lowercase pass does not itself pool every same-vowel line; the bounded variant removed the four-way collapse at no cost on the other four stanzas.
- Rule of thumb: Two-pass letter assignment fixes reading-order theft by assonance but needs its own bound on the assonance pass; a stanza with no resolved rhymes should be flagged unresolved rather than lettered, and a wrong letter silently changes any closure-distance reading built on it.
