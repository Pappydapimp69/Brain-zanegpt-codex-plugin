## E1 — sandbox combined opticon recursion per seed dependency under fixed suite
- Date: 2026-08-29
- Project: local/sandbox-combined-opticon-recursion-per-seed-dependency-under-fixed-suite
- Tags: [procgen][testing][gamedesign][method]
- What: A deterministic sandbox (LCG only, no library RNG, no wall-clock) generating a 20-room procedural map where each room independently may receive a door, validating a content dependency ("the map has at least one door") against the FINISHED map per opticon#E16, and running that validation over recursion#E14's fixed 0..N seed enumeration versus a 20,000-seed wide sample.
- Why built: opticon#E16 says probabilistic placement makes "the map has doors" a PER-SEED fact that must be validated against the finished map rather than the generator config; recursion#E14 says a fixed 0..N seed-suite validator is real completability coverage but not a growing fuzz corpus. Both were filed as separate lessons — this tests whether doing opticon#E16 correctly still leaves a real hole when its validation is executed over recursion#E14's fixed suite shape, i.e. whether the two lessons are independent or whether the second silently defeats the first.
- Design: Four placement-probability regimes swept. For each, the same per-seed finished-map dependency check runs twice: once over the fixed enumerated suite (seeds 0..99, recursion#E14's shape) and once over seeds 0..19999 to establish the true per-seed failure rate. A regime sweep (p = 0.15 through 0.30) located the boundary where the two verdicts diverge.
- Got right: Swept the probability parameter rather than picking one value — the first attempt (p = 0.12/0.06/0.03/0.02) did NOT reproduce, because at those rates failures were common enough that the 100-seed suite caught them, correctly reporting the suite as adequate. The sweep found the actual boundary and confirmed the mechanism is rate-dependent, not universal.
- Where/why it failed: At p >= 0.25 the fixed 0..99 suite passes completely clean (0 failing seeds) while real dependency-failure seeds exist in the wider space: 64 real failures at p=0.25 (rate 0.00320, first at seed 397), 30 at p=0.28, 17 at p=0.30 (first at seed 3100). A correct opticon#E16 finished-map validation, executed over a fixed enumerated suite, therefore reports full confidence on a generator that ships genuinely unreachable content to roughly 1 in 300 real players.
- Fix: A per-seed content-dependency validation needs its seed sample sized against the DEPENDENCY'S OWN expected satisfaction rate, not a round enumeration count — compute the analytic per-seed failure probability for each declared dependency (here (1-p)^n_rooms) and either sample enough seeds to detect it with confidence, or keep the dependency-free-content escape hatch opticon#E16 already prescribes so a failing seed degrades rather than breaks.
- Why this fix: The two lessons compose into a hole neither names alone: opticon#E16 correctly makes the check per-seed, and recursion#E14 correctly notes a fixed suite bounds coverage — but the consequence of combining them (a per-seed check whose sample is too small for its own failure rate yields false confidence proportional to how RARE the failure is) means the safest-looking generators, the ones where the dependency almost always holds, are exactly the ones whose fixed-suite validation is most misleading.
- Rule of thumb: When validating a probabilistic content dependency against the finished map (opticon#E16), size the seed sample from that dependency's own analytic failure rate — a fixed 0..N enumeration (recursion#E14) passes cleanest precisely when the failure is rarest, so a clean suite on a low-rate dependency is weak evidence, not strong.
