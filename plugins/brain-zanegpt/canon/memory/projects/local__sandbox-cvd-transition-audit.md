## E2 — animated background contrast dip endpoint audit misses
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/sandbox-cvd-transition-audit
- Tags: [accessibility][visualdesign][gamedev][testing][process]
- What: `brain train edge` paired sandbox-cvd-transition-audit (E1: a CVD audit that checks only endpoint colours is unsound once a colour animates; sample the trajectory) with pappydapimp69/dog (E104: name tokens by role against a background and measure the rendered pairs; E10: eased follows use `1 - pow(base, dt)`). Composed claim: when the BACKGROUND a text token sits on is itself eased (a day/night sky), an E104-style measured-pair audit of the two endpoint states passes while the in-between frames fail contrast, and the E1 trajectory method is the missing step.
- Why built: dog has a day/night clock, a HUD with near-white text on dark glass and near-black text on light cards, and eased values; E104 audits settled pairs and E1 audits colour paths, and nothing says whether the first needs the second when the surface under the text moves.
- Design: A stdlib Python sandbox with WCAG relative luminance (sRGB linearised), the Vienot-Brettel-Mollon deuteranope matrix, sRGB-space colour lerp, and the dog E10 follow `x += (1-x)*(1-base^dt)` at 15/30/60/144 fps. Night #0c1428 to day #9ccbf0 with role tokens #f5f5f5 (on dark) and #141414 (on light). Scenarios: S1 swap the token at the half-way point; S2 pick the better of the two tokens every frame; S3 pure black/white ceiling; S4 fixed scrim behind the text (built to BREAK the claim); S5 frame-sampled versus dense-path minimum at several frame rates; S6 CVD trajectory of day/dusk/night against dog-fur, grass and vent-glow; S7 control transition (night to day) with no hazard.
- Got right: The core shape held. Both endpoint pairs measured 16.80:1 and 10.72:1, yet the dense trajectory with a half-way swap bottomed at 3.55:1; the per-frame best-of-two rule bottomed at 4.11:1, still under the 4.5:1 AA floor, and even pure black/white tops out at 4.58:1 on this ease. A fixed scrim held 16.41:1 on every sample. The colour path of a `1 - pow` follow is identical at any frame rate, so the dense path is the ground truth. CVD: 4 of 18 transition/third-entity combinations flagged (dusk to night and its reverse against dog-fur: endpoint deutan-distance 0.223, trajectory 0.0158 at luminance ratio 1.04; against grass 0.176 versus 0.0356); the night-to-day control stayed clear.
- Where/why it failed: Two of the pre-registered numeric predictions missed. The half-way-swap dip was 3.55:1, not under 3.0:1, and at 144 fps the frame-sampled minimum overstated the dense minimum by 0.07, not within 0.05. Frame-sampled minima are also not monotone in frame rate: base 1e-6/s read 4.60 at 15, 30 and 60 fps (the swap frame jumped over the dip, overstating by 1.04) and 3.90 at 144 fps. The 4 CVD flags are 2 distinct hazards because each transition and its reverse share a path.
- Fix: For any text or glyph whose backdrop is eased (sky, fog, panel fade), audit the dense PATH of the backdrop colour (thousands of points on the lerp line, independent of frame rate) against each role token, and the CVD distance of that path against every other concurrent entity colour; do not infer it from the two settled pairs or from frames captured at one frame rate. Remove the dependency instead of tuning the swap: give the text a non-animated scrim or panel so the measured pair is constant.
- Why this fix: The `1 - pow` follow only changes how long each colour is visible, never which colours are visited, so the path audit is frame-rate independent and exact, while sampled frames depend on where the swap lands between frames. A constant text-on-scrim pair makes E104's measure-the-rendered-pair check sufficient again.
- Rule of thumb: When a backdrop eases between two states, E104's two endpoint contrast measurements are necessary but not sufficient: in this sandbox endpoints of 16.8:1 and 10.7:1 hid a 3.55:1 trajectory minimum (4.11:1 with a per-frame token pick), so audit the dense colour path, not sampled frames, or put the text on a fixed scrim. Tested on one sky ease and one token pair; the exact dip depth depends on the colours and the swap rule.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox twice with byte-identical output. The colours are invented stand-ins for dog's palette rather than values from the shipped game, and the VBM matrix and WCAG luminance are the same models E1 used. The sandbox RESULT line is FAIL because two numeric thresholds written in advance missed, as listed under Where/why it failed.

## E1 — cvd audit must sample transition trajectory not just palette endpoints
- Date: 2026-07-18
- Project: local/sandbox-cvd-transition-audit
- Tags: [accessibility][visualdesign][gamedev][testing][process]
- What: `brain train` (external-research-verified pass) composing pappydapimp69/the-game-the-recursion/E12 (audit a hand-curated palette for CVD confusion by checking each entity-color PAIR's perceived luminance and whether it sits on the red-green protan/deutan confusion axis) with local/sandbox-combined-accessibility-render/E1 (endpoint-safe colors are not sufficient — an eased color transition sweeps through mid-states that can be fully confusable with a THIRD entity; sample the whole trajectory at ~1-frame granularity, not just t=0/t=1). Verified, on recursion's ACTUAL palette, that E12's endpoint-pair audit is incomplete exactly where E1 predicts: a transition between two colors each CVD-distinct from a third ramp color at both endpoints passes through a deuteranopia-confusable midpoint with that third color.
- Why built: E12 audits STATIC pairs (its own palette, endpoint colors) and its process had no notion of transitions; E1 establishes the trajectory-sampling principle but on a synthetic/combined sandbox, not on a real shipped palette. Neither shows that E12's own real palette, passed through E12's own CVD criteria, hides a transition-only confusion that only E1's method surfaces — the composition is what turns "two separate good practices" into "E12's audit is unsound without E1's trajectory step."
- Design: `engine.mjs` (deterministic, no RNG, no timing) loads recursion's verbatim ramps (hollow ['#5c3540','#9e5866','#c88996'], warden ['#2f4a2e','#5c8a52','#9bc98a']), linearizes sRGB and computes WCAG relative luminance (0.2126R+0.7152G+0.0722B on linearized channels), and simulates deuteranopia with the Vienot-Brettel-Mollon 1999 single-matrix model (linear RGB, rows summing to 1). "Confusable to a deuteranope" = small Euclidean distance between deutan-simulated colors. For every ordered color pair as transition endpoints and every other color as a third entity, it eases (smoothstep) the transition in sRGB gamma space, samples 33 interior points, and compares each endpoint's deutan-distance to the third color against the trajectory's closest approach.
- Got right: The external model even SHARPENS E12: under proper WCAG luminance (linearized) rather than E12's rough gamma-space 0.299/0.587/0.114 luma, the hollow[1]/warden[1] pair is luminance 0.152 vs 0.211 and deutan-distance 0.080 — still confusable, so E12's conclusion survives the more-correct model. The demonstration also correctly leaves genuinely-safe axis colors (E12 noted diver/voice sit on blue/violet) out of the flagged case.
- Where/why it failed: An entity animating hollow[2] #c88996 -> warden[0] #2f4a2e is deutan-distinct from a third entity hollow[1] #9e5866 at BOTH endpoints (deutan-distance 0.304 and 0.190), so E12's endpoint-pair audit passes it. But at interior t=0.47 the transition color is deutan-distance 0.0076 from #9e5866 — 4% of the nearer endpoint's distance, at matched luminance (0.166 vs 0.152) — i.e. a deuteranope cannot distinguish the mid-transition entity from a different on-screen entity, precisely E1's "midpoint confusable with a third entity" shape, now on E12's real palette. Byte-identical across two `node engine.mjs` runs.
- Fix: A CVD-safety audit for a palette with any animated color transitions (hit-flash, morph, cross-fade, state tween) must simulate dichromacy over the full INTERPOLATED trajectory of every transition against every other concurrent entity color, not only the static palette endpoints — E12's pairwise endpoint check plus E1's trajectory sampling, using an external-standard model (Vienot-Brettel-Mollon deutan/protan + WCAG luminance) as the confusion criterion rather than an ad-hoc heuristic.
- Why this fix: The endpoint audit and the trajectory audit catch disjoint failures; a palette can pass the first and fail the second on its own colors. Grounding the confusion test in the published CVD model (not a hand-rolled red-green rule) makes the audit trustworthy and portable across projects.
- Rule of thumb: Auditing a game/UI palette for color-vision-deficiency safety by checking only static entity-color PAIRS is unsound if any entity ANIMATES between colors: an eased transition sweeps through intermediate colors that can be deuteranopia/protanopia-confusable with a THIRD entity even when both endpoints are safe. Sample the interpolated trajectory at ~1-frame granularity and test it, and every static pair, with the Vienot-Brettel-Mollon dichromat model + WCAG relative luminance (linearize sRGB first) as ground truth — not a rough gamma-space luma or a hand-rolled axis rule.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand — built and ran a deterministic computation (engine.mjs, no RNG, no timing, byte-identical across two process invocations) on recursion's verbatim palette; confirmed the hollow/warden pair stays confusable under the proper WCAG+Vienot model, and found a real transition (hollow[2]->warden[0]) whose t=0.47 midpoint sits deutan-distance 0.0076 from a third color while both endpoints are 0.19-0.30 away. The CVD model and luminance formula are grounded in external sources read this session: Brettel, Vienot & Mollon 1997 "Computerized simulation of color appearance for dichromats" (JOSA A) and the 1999 single-matrix simplification; the WCAG 2.x relative-luminance definition (W3C). Caveat: the specific hollow->warden cross-ramp tween is a demonstrative transition an entity COULD animate (recursion currently shades only +/-1 within a ramp); the finding is about the AUDIT-METHOD gap on real palette colors, not a claim that recursion ships this exact animation.
