## E1 — teal orange grade separates skin only when background luminance or hue differs
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/sandbox-teal-orange-grade-separation
- Tags: [color][visualdesign][accessibility][testing]
- What: `brain train kernel` (apply mode) built against the cinematography kernel that says a teal-orange grade (shadows to teal, highlights and skin to orange) exploits the largest complementary contrast and so separates skin from the background. Modelled it in OKLab with stdlib maths: 10 illustrative skin swatches (OKLab L 0.26 to 0.95, hue 44 to 85 deg), 12 backdrop hues at C 0.08 (plus saturated lit ones at C 0.15), two grades (a luminance-split tint and a hue-curve pull), and deuteranope/protanope simulation. Measured skin-vs-backdrop separation before and after. The claim only partly survives: the grade's gain is small, depends on the backdrop being darker than the skin or non-orange, and costs fidelity on the darkest skin.
- Why built: The kernel is a trade-press craft claim ("complementary, so it separates"); nothing in the canon had put numbers on it, tried the backdrops it should fail on (orange or lit like the skin), checked what it does to skin tone, or asked whether the separation survives colour-vision deficiency, where the CVD kernels say luminance survives and hue does not.
- Design: Stdlib Python, no RNG, no timing, byte-identical on two runs. sRGB to OKLab (published matrices, round trip error 1.4e-07), delta E as 100 x Euclid in OKLab, plus a chromatic-only dEab. Grade G1 = additive tint in OKLab a,b, teal weighted by shadow smoothstep, orange by highlight smoothstep, strength 0.03 (swept to 0.10). Grade G2 = rotate each hue 40% toward the nearer of an orange anchor (55 deg) and a teal anchor (195 deg). CVD = Vienot-style linear-sRGB dichromat matrices (rows sum to 1, white preserved, checked) with WCAG relative luminance, the same model sandbox-cvd-transition-audit/E1 used, re-implemented on new colours. Backdrops are gamut-clipped before grading (run 1 skipped this and was contaminated; fixed and stated in the script header). Six blind predictions were written first, four post-hoc ones after run 1 and labelled as such.
- Got right: (1) The kernel's direction holds on a dark backdrop for non-orange hues: mean chromatic gain by backdrop hue was +0.8 to +1.5 for hues 120 to 300 (teal-ish 180 to 210: +0.8, +1.1) and the improved share rose with strength (0.30, 0.42, 0.60 at s 0.03, 0.06, 0.10). (2) Matched luminance gives exactly zero median gain (median |gain| 0.000): an additive tint with equal weights on skin and backdrop cancels in the difference, so the grade only works through a luminance or hue difference that already exists. (3) Under deuteranope simulation the teal-ish backdrops kept 0.70 of their graded separation and blue to violet backdrops (240 to 300) kept about 1.0, matching the CVD kernel's blue-versus-orange claim; the grade left the skin-vs-backdrop WCAG luminance gap unchanged (0.356 to 0.354), so it adds a chromatic axis only.
- Where/why it failed: Six blind predictions, 2 held (the matched-luminance cancellation and the hue-curve sign split) and 4 refuted. P1 (at least 70% of dark-backdrop pairs improved) saw 30%. P4 (darkest skin hue shift at least 30 deg) saw 28 deg for the L 0.32 tone, but the L 0.26 tone moved from hue 61 to 145 deg (off the skin range entirely) while the lightest three moved 0 to 6 deg: the shadow-teal term drags the darkest skins toward green. P5 retention bound for red/green backdrops was 0.35 and saw 0.39 (teal-ish 0.70 held). P6 (grade gain shrinks under CVD) saw the opposite, a ratio of 1.64, because median normal-vision gain was only +0.21. Bigger point: the gain (median +0.2 to +1.7 chromatic units) is tiny against the baseline (full delta E about 43, mostly luminance) and against the spread that the backdrop choice alone makes: before any grade, separation at matched luminance ranges 4.5 (hue 75) to 12.0 (hue 255). Post-hoc: the separation peak is blue (255 deg), because skin sits at OKLab 44 to 85 deg and its complement is about 235 to 265; teal (195 deg) reaches 85% of the peak, so "directly opposite" is a colour-wheel picture, not a perceptual one. Moving the hue-curve teal anchor to 250 did not help (gain -0.15 against +0.22 at 195), and strength 0.10 did not push 3 or more skins off-range (2 of 10). Light skins (L above 0.8) improved in 57% of dark-backdrop pairs, dark skins (L below 0.45) in 0%. Limits: the 10 swatches are typed illustrative values, not a measured skin dataset; one fixed backdrop chroma; no spatial or masked grade; deutan and protan only (no tritan); delta E in OKLab is not CIEDE2000.
- Fix: Treat teal-orange as a nudge on top of backdrop design, not the separator: get luminance and hue separation from the backdrop and lighting first (a backdrop darker than the skin and in the blue-teal half gains most; an orange, red or skin-matched-luminance backdrop gains nothing or loses, e.g. mean gain -1.4 at backdrop hue 60, and a lit orange backdrop stays at +0.3 or -0.1). Keep the shadow-teal term off skin by masking or by limiting it to backdrop regions, and check the darkest tones for hue drift against the original skin range before shipping a grade; audit with a delta E and hue-angle table across a stated tone set and several backdrop hues, then repeat under dichromat simulation.
- Why this fix: The additive-shift cancellation shows the grade cannot create separation that lighting did not; the swept strengths show more grade buys more separation only at the price of skin drift and gamut clipping (clipped colours rose from 79 to 102 across the sweep); and the luminance gap staying fixed shows the CVD-robust axis (luminance) is untouched, so any deficiency-safe separation must come from the lighting.
- Rule of thumb: In an OKLab model with 10 illustrative skin tones and 12 backdrop hues, a teal-orange tint moved skin-vs-backdrop separation by only +0.2 to +1.7 chromatic units against a baseline of about 43 delta E, helped only dark or blue-teal backdrops, gave exactly 0 at matched luminance, lost ground on orange and red backdrops, and pushed the darkest skin from hue 61 to 145 deg; so size the separation in the backdrop and the lighting, apply the grade masked away from skin, and measure skin drift per tone instead of trusting "complementary". Tested on one grade form each and one backdrop chroma; the numbers are model-specific.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox twice with byte-identical output; the OKLab round trip and the dichromat matrix row sums were checked in the run itself. Everything quantitative above is the sandbox's own output. The colour values are illustrative swatches typed for this test and the matrix coefficients are the published values as recalled; what colourists do on real footage is external trade-press content outside this sandbox.
