## E1 — refusal operand disclosure is graded and priced in bits no free design
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/sandbox-refusal-operand-disclosure
- Tags: [governance][refusal-design][security][testing][tension]
- What: A deterministic model of a guard whose limit T (32..127, floor 32 published) is secret, refusing over-limit amounts. Fourteen refusal-disclosure designs were each measured on two axes: what a legitimate actor pays to repair a refused request (guard calls, escalations, utility kept, unwanted partial executions) over 96 refused requests, and how many probes a greedy-halving prober needs to isolate T. Designs: no operand, exact operand, w-wide bucket (4, 8, 16, 32), geometric bucket, excess rounded up, exact operand only for near misses, in-place clamp (exact and quantised), exact operand for the first K refusals per identity (with and without fresh identities), and a hint to split into chunks of the public floor. Extra worlds: a two-field guard (public field plus secret field), clean versus derived versus shared-pool limits, and a per-action versus cumulative guard.
- Why built: The open tension asks whether a refusal should return the matched operands to the refused party when the guard also defends a hidden state. The prior sandbox (sandbox-refusal-rule-not-bucket/E1) only compared two points, exact value versus none. The remaining question was whether an intermediate design (coarse operand, operand for non-secret fields only, per-actor rate-limited disclosure, a repair hint that names the fix without the value) gets most of the repair benefit without the leak. Supports pole B of tension 'should a refusal return the matched operands to the refused party when the guard also defends a hidden state?' for exact operands (every exact or near-exact disclosure collapsed recovery to 1 to 2 probes), while showing pole A can be bought back only at a priced, bounded leak.
- Design: Actor ladder: after the refusal it tries descending amounts in 5 percent steps down to 60 percent of the request, using a belief set over T that every message narrows; success needs utility 0.6, it prefers 0.85, otherwise it escalates. Prober: any amount 0..255 each probe, chooses the probe that minimises the largest remaining group, tracks per-candidate disclosure state, so stateful designs are handled exactly. Benefit is the share of the exact-operand call saving a design achieves; leak is the share of the exact-operand probe drop (mean probes) it incurs. Post-run extras: ladder step swept over 5, 10 and 20 percent, benefit split into repairable (overshoot up to 1.3x) versus hopeless (1.8x) requests, and a check that a refusal without an operand still narrows T under near-miss gating.
- Got right: No operand costs 4.51 calls and 24 of 96 escalations with 150 calls wasted on them, prober worst 7. Exact operand costs 1.75 calls, prober 1. Coarse buckets sit on a concave curve: 8-wide bucket keeps 0.86 of the call saving for 0.48 of the leak (mean probes 3.96, about 3.6 bits disclosed), 16-wide 0.63 for 0.30, geometric 0.67 for 0.31; benefit exceeded leak at every ladder step (margin 0.33, 0.24, 0.09). The in-place clamp quantised to 16 gave exactly the 16-wide bucket partition (worst probes 5 both). Rate-limited exact disclosure: prober probes 1 for any K of 1 or more, while legit calls rose from 1.75 to 3.92 at K=2 and 4.35 at K=1, so the limit costs the honest actor and saves no secrecy. Two-field guard with a truly public field: probes 7 (same as no operand), repair benefit 0.50 by construction.
- Where/why it failed: Three pre-registered predictions were refuted. (1) The 16-wide bucket kept 0.63, not at least 0.75, of the repair saving; only the 8-wide bucket met 0.75 with leak under 0.5. (2) Near-miss gating (exact operand only when overshoot is 25 percent or less) kept 0.90 of the benefit, not under 0.45, and leaked 0.77 (mean probes 2.32), because the operand-free refusal itself says the overshoot is large (candidates 96 to 48 at one probe), which also let the actor give up hopeless requests early: gating a disclosure on request shape makes its absence an observation. (3) The split-into-public-floor-chunks hint needed 4.75 calls, more than the 4.51 of the plain ladder, though it kept utility 1.0, no escalations and zero extra leak; and it is only valid when the guard is per-action: under a cumulative guard 96 of 96 chunked repairs escalated at utility 0.64. Breaks that worked as predicted: a field classed as public whose limit derives from T (T//2+10) gives probes 2; a shared-pool limit gives 1; an in-place clamp gives 1 probe and 1 call but executes 24 of 96 requests at a utility below tolerance with no consent.
- Fix: Treat disclosure as a graded budget, not a per-field yes or no. Disclose the operand at the coarsest resolution the legitimate actor's tolerance allows (a safe lower bound or a bucket, not the matched value); never disclose exact values for state-derived limits; gate nothing on request shape alone; do not rely on a per-actor disclosure cap; and audit each field the guard labels public for derivation from hidden state or shared pools. Keep the byte-identical or no-operand refusal where no coarse resolution is tolerable.
- Why this fix: It picks the point on the measured curve where benefit outruns leak (8-wide: 0.86 vs 0.48; 16-wide: 0.63 vs 0.30; both beat the diagonal at every actor ladder step), it avoids the three designs that failed (exact operand, rate cap, near-miss gating), and it prices the leak in the same unit the attacker spends: probes saved track bits disclosed. No design in the run had benefit of 0.5 or more at zero leak.
- Rule of thumb: Every bit of operand a refusal discloses is a bit the prober no longer has to probe for, so disclosure is graded and priced, not allowed or forbidden: a coarse lower bound sized to the honest actor's tolerance (8-wide of a 96-value range) bought 0.86 of the repair saving for 0.48 of the leak in this model, exact or near-exact values (including exact values for near misses, or for the first K refusals per identity) gave the whole secret in 1 to 2 probes, a rate cap only taxed honest actors (calls 1.75 to 3.92), and a mislabeled public field or a shared pool voids any per-field rule. Scope: one scalar secret, one actor ladder model, tolerance 0.6; the benefit side moves with actor skill (benefit fell to 0.59 for the 8-wide bucket when the actor used 20 percent steps) while the leak side does not.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by the sandbox run (last line RESULT: FAIL; three pre-registered predictions were refuted, every other check including six post-hoc checks held). Modelling choices of this sandbox, outside what the run showed: the actor ladder, the tolerance values, the greedy-halving prober (not proven optimal) and the 96-request population; live systems, real attackers and human repair behaviour were outside the run.
