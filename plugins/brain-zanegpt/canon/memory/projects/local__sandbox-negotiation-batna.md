## E1 — batna walk away alternative sets the floor willpower only buys price inside it
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/sandbox-negotiation-batna
- Tags: [negotiation][sandbox][research]
- What: A deterministic bargaining model tested the kernel "power comes from the walk-away alternative, not willpower". It reproduces the structural half (an alternative sets a floor and a ceiling, binds only above the no-option share, and caps what stubbornness can buy) but REFUTES the strong half: in a concession-pace model willpower still explains most outcome variance (within-BATNA r 0.96). This is model logic, not human behaviour.
- Why built: The kernel is a quoted-source claim with no applied test yet. Phrases like "power is not nerve" are easy to over-read as "nerve is worthless", so the model asks what each lever does when the other is held fixed.
- Design: Layer A is an exact alternating-offers solver (backward induction, 0.9 discount, 40 rounds, either side may exit to its own alternative; fine 10000-unit grid). Layer B has concession agents: willpower is a slow concession rate, the alternative is a reservation price (seller floor, buyer ceiling), no deal pays each side its own alternative. Scenarios: threshold sweep, patience-only seller, willpower premium vs buyer ceiling, tough-weak vs soft-strong head to head, no-agreement-zone control, non-binding-alternative control, opening-ask sweep, 60-round deadline, and two fixed-seed populations where toughness is correlated with BATNA.
- Got right: Seller payoff is flat (51.9 to 52.5) while its alternative is 0-40, then 56.8, 64.0, 82.0 at alternatives 52/60/80 (matches 100 - 0.9*(100-o)). Willpower premium over a soft seller is 7.1 at a buyer ceiling of 40, 27.1 at 60, 41.3 at 80+. With no agreement zone all 6 toughness levels end in impasse and the seller gets exactly its alternative. With non-binding alternatives, changing the seller alternative moves price by 0.00 while toughness moves it by 47.4. The opening ask raises price (60.2 to 71.0) and then flattens at the buyer ceiling.
- Where/why it failed: Three of my own predictions missed on first run: the 100-unit integer grid gave 50 not 52.6 (rounding, fine grid fixed it), a 60-round deadline turned tough agents into impasses and faked the premium result, and the confounded-population test (P6) is truly refuted: naive willpower r 0.97 vs 0.96 within BATNA strata, BATNA r 0.95, because toughness is a causal channel of the price here. Making counterparties' alternatives real (ceilings 45-65) only moved it to 0.82 (exploratory P7, also failed). Patience is a separate power source: 0.97 vs 0.85 discount gave the seller 83.7.
- Fix: Narrow the kernel when applying it: the alternative decides the floor, the ceiling and whether a deal exists; stubbornness buys price only inside the zone the counterparty's alternative leaves open, and costs impasse risk under a deadline. Do not tell a negotiator willpower is irrelevant, and do not read the correlation of BATNA with outcome as proof that toughness is not working.
- Why this fix: The model makes both limits visible at once: the premium disappears when the other side's ceiling is low, and BATNA changes nothing when it sits below the natural price. A bounded claim survives every control; the unbounded "not willpower" claim does not.
- Rule of thumb: In a bargaining model the walk-away alternative fixes the floor, the ceiling and the existence of a deal, while willpower only moves price inside that zone (premium 7.1 to 41.3 as the other side's ceiling rises), so improve the alternative first but never claim willpower is worthless; stubbornness-vs-BATNA variance shares are not separable when the two co-vary.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox (final line RESULT: FAIL 5/7, structural checks pass, P6 and P7 fail). The kernel's human-behaviour source is quoted prose that I took as given; this model supports only the logic of the structure.
