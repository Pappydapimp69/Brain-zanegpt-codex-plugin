## E1 — silhouette first needs a size floor and a backdrop outline
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/sandbox-silhouette-first-cue-audit
- Tags: [accessibility][visualdesign][testing]
- What: `brain train kernel` applied ideas:visualdesign/accessibility/silhouette-first-then-colour (order the cues silhouette, then held object, then colour; ask what it looks like in greyscale) against three memory entries: sandbox-combined-brain-viz-growth/E3 (hue-only categories collide after luminance projection), sandbox-cvd-transition-audit/E1 (Vienot-Brettel-Mollon trajectory audit) and dog/E104 (a fill measured against an eased backdrop dips). The kernel's direction held, but my pre-registered strong form failed on 3 of 5 predictions and the failures are the lesson. (1) Silhouette is a body-first channel: the small held prop is the weakest cue. (2) The channel needs its own luminance contrast against the backdrop, so an eased backdrop erases every silhouette on some frame. (3) Below a pixel-size floor silhouette loses to luminance-separated colour.
- Why built: the kernel argues silhouette also survives small sizes, low contrast and bad screens, and E3/E1/E104 each describe a colour or contrast failure; nothing said whether a shape-composed cast escapes those audits or inherits them.
- Design: stdlib Python, no RNG, no timing. A 16x16 canvas, four body masks (circle, square, triangle, bar) times two 6-pixel props (staff, hat) = 8 slots, invented stand-in colours. Condition pipeline: gamma to linear RGB, then normal, Vienot-Brettel-Mollon deutan or protan matrices, or linear luminance, then box downsample 1/2/4/8. Pair distance is the area-mean per-pixel difference, confusable if below 0.02. Casts: recolour-only (one body, eight hues at matched linear luminance 0.28), shape cast (same colours), luminance-spread recolour, near-identical bodies, 10 entities on 8 slots, and a 33-frame eased dark-to-light backdrop with and without a 1 px black-or-white outline. Post-hoc probes (labelled, not pre-registered): which pairs fail, body-only cast, and a peak-pixel metric for the size sweep.
- Got right: the headline comparison held cleanly. With a matched-luminance palette the recolour-only cast has 28 of 28 pairs confusable in greyscale, 5 under deutan and 2 under protan, while the shape cast has 0 under normal, deutan and protan and 4 in greyscale; the 4 body-only silhouettes have 0 confusable pairs in all four conditions. The backdrop prediction (P4) held exactly: with no outline the greyscale shape cast reaches 28 of 28 confusable on the frame where fill and backdrop luminance meet (fill-to-backdrop contrast 1.02:1); with an outline picked per frame as black or white the worst frame is 0 of 28 and the minimum outline-to-backdrop contrast is 4.75:1 (a floor near 4.58:1 follows from the pick, because the worst backdrop sits near linear luminance 0.18).
- Where/why it failed: P1 (shape cast has 0 confusable pairs) failed: all 4 confusable greyscale pairs share a body and differ only by the 6-pixel prop, so the held-object cue is the weak channel, and in normal vision colour is what separates them. P5 failed: 10 entities on 8 slots gave 8 confusable pairs with the matched palette and 1 with a luminance-spread palette (not the predicted 2 and 0); duplicate slots plus small props pile up. P2 failed as written: the area-mean metric is almost size-invariant under box downsampling (flat 4 confusable at every size), so it cannot show a floor. A post-hoc peak-pixel metric (threshold 0.05) does show one: shape cast 0/28 at 16, 8 and 4 px but 10/28 at 2 px, against 4/28 for luminance-spread recolour, so the floor is real but the metric chose whether I could see it. S3 (bodies 1-3 pixels apart) was 6 of 6 confusable, as predicted. The E1 touch found nothing: a same-slot colour transition stayed deutan-distinct from a third entity on all 101 samples, so E1 was exercised but its failure condition did not recur on these colours.
- Fix: audit the silhouette channel the way E3/E1/E104 audit colour: render the cast in greyscale, deutan and protan, include the eased backdrop trajectory, and add a contrast-safe outline (black or white chosen per frame, or a fixed scrim) so the silhouette keeps at least 4.5:1 against the backdrop. Make bodies the primary discriminator and size props to be a visible fraction of the body; treat prop-only pairs and any slot shared by two entities as needing a luminance-separated colour, not just a different hue. Check the smallest display size with a peak-pixel measure, not an area mean.
- Why this fix: the outline removed the 28 of 28 collapse in the sandbox; the body-only cast shows body shape is the strong channel; the prop-only failure pattern (4 of 4) points at cue strength, not at silhouette as a whole.
- Rule of thumb: Silhouette-first beats recolouring when hues are luminance-matched, but only bodies are a reliable channel: audit props, shared slots and the smallest size separately, and give the silhouette its own contrast floor against any animated backdrop (an outline), because a shape-composed cast inherits E104's background dip. Tested on 8 slots, 4 conditions and 4 sizes in 5 scenarios, so read "silhouette first" as "bodies first, props and colour verified", not as exempt from the colour audit.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox twice with byte-identical output; the first run failed on a scale artefact and a gamma-versus-linear matching bug in my own palette code, both fixed and recorded in the script header with the predictions left unedited. The palette, masks and 0.02 / 0.05 thresholds are invented stand-ins chosen by me, and the Vienot-Brettel-Mollon matrices are typed from the same model E1 used rather than imported.
