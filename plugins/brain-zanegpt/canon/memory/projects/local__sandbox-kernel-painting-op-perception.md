## E1 — op art shimmer needs eye shift and contrast but not fine scale
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/sandbox-kernel-painting-op-perception
- Tags: [painting][perception][sandbox][verification][measurement]
- What: Tested the kernel "op effects happen in the eye, not on the canvas" with a deterministic toy retinal-shift model (static pattern sampled at fixed sub-pixel offsets, regularised patch gradient-motion estimator) plus two primary-source checks. The model reproduces: zero signal when the eye does not move (energy 0.0000 at zero shift), signal strictly proportional to contrast (energy ratio 9.00 for contrast 0.9 vs 0.1), raw signal falling about 1/scale when the same design is painted larger (x1/x4 energy ratio 4.26), and death below a fixed detector floor (low-contrast design: 100% of patches active at x1, 0% at x4). It did NOT reproduce a distinct incoherent-direction signature for the Riley-like phase-modulated grating.
- Why built: The kernel states the eye-side claim and a scale rule ("paint the same design large and coarse and it dies") with the microsaccade mechanism flagged unsettled; the pass was a gap run to check what a sandbox can and cannot confirm of a perception claim.
- Design: Pattern functions (straight grating, phase-modulated grating, checker) sampled on a 128 px grid at 8 fixed drift directions of amplitude 0.5 px; patch (16 px) least-squares motion estimate; metrics: raw RMS luminance change, mean flow above a noise floor, vector coherence, circular spread of flow angle. Scenarios: rigid shift, zero shift, contrast sweep, grating-period sweep, whole-design scale sweep (x1/x2/x4 at two contrasts), aliasing break (shift near the period), modulation-steepness sweep, direction-spread sweep. Primary sources checked: the 1965 MoMA Responsive Eye press release PDF (fetched; the two quoted sentences match verbatim) and Hermens and Zanker, i-Perception 2012 (Europe PMC full text).
- Got right: Predicted the eye-dependence (0 at zero shift), the 9x contrast ratio and the 3-5x raw energy fall with scale; the MoMA quotes in the kernel are exact. The primary paper confirms contrast strongly affects the illusion and that the microsaccade account is contested (illusion also seen without microsaccades; slow drift suggested).
- Where/why it failed: Three of 13 pre-registered predictions missed. (1) Coherence: my first metric averaged zero-signal steps (drift along the lines), giving 0.75 for a straight grating; fixed by skipping those steps, after which straight=1.00 but phase-modulated=0.95 (predicted <0.90) because normal flows under a rigid shift all share the sign of the shift, so a vector-mean cannot cancel. (2) A circular-spread metric gave straight 0.0 deg, gentle bend 14.1, medium 2.7, steepest 3.2 deg: non-monotone, so the incoherence account was left unclear after three metric iterations. (3) Scale-normalised flow was not fully scale-invariant (0.71 at x1 vs 0.52 at x4, 28% off; x2 and x4 agree at 0.54 and 0.52, the x1 value is inflated by central-difference rolloff at period 8). Unverified here: human perception itself, the original 2010 psychophysics (read only through the 2012 paper summary, which says the illusion grew with modulation amplitude and period but was unaffected by grating wavelength), and any real eye-movement trace.
- Fix: Narrow the kernel's actionable line. Keep: the effect needs an eye shift and scales with contrast; a larger copy of the same design carries about 1/scale the retinal signal and falls under a fixed floor at low contrast. Drop or soften "fine, dense structure is what makes it work": the cited psychophysics reports no dependence on grating wavelength in the tested range, and the toy model's normalised velocity estimate is scale-invariant until the shift nears the period; design variables that mattered in the source were modulation amplitude, modulation period and contrast.
- Why this fix: The sandbox gives contrast and eye-dependence as mechanism-level facts, but the scale claim splits: raw energy supports it, normalised flow and the human data do not. Stating only what both sources agree on avoids shipping a rule that a painter would test and find wrong at their own scale.
- Rule of thumb: In a retinal-shift model a static Op pattern gives exactly 0 signal without eye shift and signal proportional to contrast, so specify contrast first; do not state a coarse-pattern-dies rule without a detector floor, because normalised motion estimates stayed scale-invariant until the shift approached the period and the cited human data show no grating-wavelength effect.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand: the sandbox run (deterministic, identical output on repeat) and its numbers; the MoMA 1965 press release text fetched and matched; the Hermens and Zanker 2012 abstract and methods text read in full via Europe PMC (PMC3485832). The incoherent-motion account and the microsaccade versus drift question were not settled by this run.

- Prediction: Written before the first run: straight-grating coherence >0.98 and phase-modulated <0.90; zero energy at zero shift; energy ratio 8.5-9.5 for contrast 0.9 vs 0.1; raw energy ratio 3-5 between period 8 and 32; normalised flow within 20% across period 8 to 32; low-contrast coarse design inactive; aliased shift gives wrong flow. Added after run 1 and before run 2: whole-design x1/x4 energy ratio 3-5, flow within 25%, low-contrast x4 inactive. Added before run 3: steepest-bend coherence <0.90 and then spread >20 deg monotone in steepness.
