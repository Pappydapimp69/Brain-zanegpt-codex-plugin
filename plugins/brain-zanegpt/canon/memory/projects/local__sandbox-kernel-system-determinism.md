## E8 — lattice collision error scales with lattice over half extent and cancels in counts
- Date: 2026-09-28
- Project: local/sandbox-kernel-system-determinism
- Tags: [determinism][collision][measurement][testing]
- What: Applied the ideas kernel that quantising positions to an integer lattice trades against collision precision, with the lattice to be tied to the smallest half-extent. The drift and aliasing reproduced, and a net overlap count, the kernel's own metric, hides most of the per-event error.
- Why built: brain train gap listed the kernel as unapplied. sandbox-combined-game-loop/E1 set the integer-lattice rule; this measures how fine the lattice must be.
- Design: 12 bodies bouncing in a 20x20 box for 400 steps, 20 seeds, every pair tested for AABB overlap each step with float positions and with positions rounded to lattices of 0.01, 0.1 and 0.5, at half-extents 0.25, 0.3, 0.5 and 1.0. Recorded both the net difference in overlap counts and the per-event disagreement (false positives plus false negatives) relative to the float count.
- Got right: Counting disagreements per event. Over- and under-detection cancel in a total, which is what makes a coarse lattice look acceptable.
- Where/why it failed: At lattice 0.01 per-event disagreement was 0.4 to 1.9%. At 0.1 with half-extent 0.3 the net count was off by only +1.2% while 16.0% of events disagreed. Across settings the per-event error tracked lattice over half-extent: about 0.4% at a ratio of 0.01, 1.8% at 0.04, 4.8% at 0.1, 9.1% at 0.2 and 20.7% at 0.4. At lattice 0.5, half-extents 0.3 and 0.5 both detected exactly 2590 overlaps, the aliasing the kernel described.
- Fix: Judge a lattice by per-event agreement against float ground truth, not by total counts, and choose it from the ratio: lattice at most about 0.04 of the smallest half-extent keeps disagreement under 2% here.
- Why this fix: A rounded position moves each edge by up to half a lattice step, so the band where overlap flips scales with lattice over half-extent. Totals net that band's errors in both directions.
- Rule of thumb: Quantised-vs-float collision error scales with lattice/half-extent (about 0.4% of events at 0.01, 4.8% at 0.1, 20.7% at 0.4) and a net overlap count hides it: at lattice 0.1 and half-extent 0.3 the total was off +1.2% while 16.0% of events disagreed, so compare per event and keep the lattice at about 0.04 of the smallest half-extent or finer.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand in cognitive-updates/sandbox_train_gap_b06.py loop 28 in Pappydapimp69/Brain, deterministic seeded motion. Observed: per-event disagreement 0.4-1.9% at lattice 0.01, 4.8-20.7% at 0.1, 22.8-75.5% at 0.5; net +1.2% vs 16.0% at 0.1/0.3; 2590 overlaps for both half-extents 0.3 and 0.5 at lattice 0.5. The ratio rule is fitted to this AABB model.
- Composed: ideas:SYSTEM/determinism/integer-lattice-and-collision-precision-trade-off-not-compose/tie-lattice-resolution-to-the-smallest-half-extent, sandbox-combined-game-loop/E1

## E7 — a float time accumulator reintroduces wrong sprite frames
- Date: 2026-09-28
- Project: local/sandbox-kernel-system-determinism
- Tags: [determinism][animation][floating-point][timing]
- What: Applied the ideas kernel that a sprite frame index advanced once per render tick plays at render rate, and that deriving it from elapsed time fixes this. The render-rate bug reproduced exactly, but the kernel's own test computed time as i/fps to avoid float accumulation; with the usual `acc += dt`, the fix gives the wrong frame on the kernel's own test point.
- Why built: brain train gap listed the kernel as unapplied. The kernel's source note excludes float accumulation as a confound, which is how most loops actually keep time.
- Design: A 7-frame sheet at 10 fps. At 1 s: a per-render-tick increment, frame = floor(t x 10) mod 7 from exact time, and the same from a float accumulator summing 1/hz each render. Then 60 s at 30, 60, 120 and 144 Hz counting renders where the accumulator shows a different frame than exact time. Then a 1.5x playback-speed change at 30 s, comparing a frame derived from total time x rate with a phase accumulated each render at the current rate.
- Got right: Keeping the float accumulator the kernel set aside. Removing it from the test is what made the fix look exact.
- Where/why it failed: At 1 s the per-tick increment gave frame 2 at 30 Hz and 4 at 60 Hz against a correct 3, as the kernel reported. The float accumulator summed thirty 1/30 steps to 0.9999999999999999 and showed frame 2 at 30 Hz, the same wrong frame as the bug. Over 60 s it showed a different frame than exact time on 294 of 1800 renders at 30 Hz, 449 of 3600 at 60 Hz, 525 of 7200 at 120 Hz and 89 of 8640 at 144 Hz. At the speed change, deriving from total time jumped 151 frames in one render; accumulating phase never advanced more than 1.
- Fix: Keep time as an integer count (ticks, or integer milliseconds or microseconds) and derive the frame from it, and when the playback rate can change, accumulate phase at the current rate instead of multiplying total time by the new rate.
- Why this fix: Summed 1/hz steps drift below and above exact frame boundaries, so floor() flips whenever a boundary falls near a render. Multiplying total elapsed time by a new rate re-times the whole past at that rate, which is a jump.
- Rule of thumb: Deriving a sprite frame from elapsed time fixes render-rate playback only if the time is exact: a float `acc += 1/hz` reached 0.9999999999999999 after 1 s at 30 Hz and showed the same wrong frame as the per-tick bug, and differed from exact time on 294 of 1800 renders over 60 s; keep integer time, and accumulate phase when the rate can change (total x new rate jumped 151 frames).
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand in cognitive-updates/sandbox_train_gap_b05.py loop 24 in Pappydapimp69/Brain, deterministic. Observed: per-tick 2 and 4, exact 3, float-accumulated 2 and 3 at 30 and 60 Hz; 294/449/525/89 differing renders at 30/60/120/144 Hz; 151 versus 1 frame at the speed change.
- Composed: ideas:SYSTEM/determinism/sprite-frame-index-as-time-accumulator-not-per-tick-increment/decouple-animation-playback-rate-from-render-rate

## E6 — order divergence flips float gates early and integer gates near 2 53
- Date: 2026-09-28
- Project: local/sandbox-kernel-system-determinism
- Tags: [determinism][floating-point][state][architecture]
- What: Applied the ideas kernel that different accumulation orders of a compounding pinned value give different bits from about the second event and flip the gate near the safe-integer crossover. Both reproduced, and when the flips start depends on how the gate compares.
- Why built: brain train gap listed the kernel as unapplied. the-game-the-recursion/E20 recorded the canonical-order rule; this measures which gate forms are exposed and when.
- Design: 200 seeds of 40 multipliers drawn from 5.0 to 6.0. The value is computed in four orders: left fold, right fold, split halves and exp of summed logs. The gate compares a recomputed value against the one pinned by live play (the left fold), in three forms: a raw float comparison, a floor-to-integer comparison, and a comparison against an authored integer threshold within 10% of the value.
- Got right: Separating the gate forms. With one form the kernel's crossover claim either always holds or never does.
- Where/why it failed: The four orders first gave different bits at a median of event 2 (range 1 to 4). A raw float gate against the pinned value flipped in 135 to 169 of 200 seeds at every event count from 5 on. A floor-to-integer gate flipped in 0 seeds through event 18, then 37 at event 19 (median value 10^14.1), 72 at 20, 132 at 21 and 149 at 22, so it starts about two events before 2^53 (10^15.95). A gate against an authored threshold flipped in 0 seeds at every event count. With one canonical left fold on every path the bits were identical, and its relative error against exact rationals after 40 events was 4.1e-16.
- Fix: Give every path that produces the pinned value one canonical accumulation order. If the gate compares against a value another path produced, treat any float comparison as exposed from the first event, not only near 2^53.
- Why this fix: Orders disagree in their last bits almost immediately; whether that flips a gate depends on whether the threshold sits inside that disagreement. A threshold equal to another path's result always does; an integer gate starts to once the absolute spread reaches 1, which happens slightly before 2^53.
- Rule of thumb: Different accumulation orders of a compounding value differ in bits from event 2, and a gate that compares a recomputed value against the pinned one flips from the start if it compares floats (135-169 of 200 seeds from event 5); only an integer-floor gate waits for the crossover (0 flips through event 18, 72 at event 20 while still below 2^53); an authored threshold never flipped.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand in cognitive-updates/sandbox_train_gap_b04.py loop 19 in Pappydapimp69/Brain, deterministic. Observed: first differing bits median event 2; float gate flips 135-169/200 from event 5; floor gate 0 through 18, 37/72/132/149 at 19-22; authored threshold 0 throughout; canonical fold identical with error 4.1e-16.
- Composed: ideas:SYSTEM/determinism/a-privileged-gates-pinned-value-needs-one-canonical-computation-order-not-just-a-source-location/float-non-associativity-breaks-fingerprints-early-and-flips-gates-near-the-safe-integer-crossover, the-game-the-recursion/E20

## E5 — past 2 53 order dependent sums not crossing break fingerprints
- Date: 2026-09-28
- Project: local/sandbox-kernel-system-determinism
- Tags: [determinism][floating-point][state][measurement]
- What: Applied the ideas kernel that a 5.5x-per-event stat crosses 2^53 after about 21 events and Infinity after about 416, and that past 2^53 arithmetic still looks right while exact-integer operations break. Both counts reproduced, and the sandbox separated which exact operations actually break and why.
- Why built: brain train gap listed the kernel as unapplied. sandbox-power-scaling/E1 measured the crossover; sandbox-combined-loop-combat/E2 bounded integer quantisation by the same range. Neither measured what fails first after the crossover.
- Design: Compounded 5.5x from starts of 1, 2, 5 and 10 until 2^53 and Infinity. Ran floor(v*5.5) per event in float64 beside exact Python integers. Added +1 to 20000 integer-valued floats in each binade from 2^52 to 2^57. Summed 8 integer-valued stats in two different orders, 4000 trials per magnitude, with the largest near 2^48, 2^52, 2^53, 2^55 and 2^60.
- Got right: Testing summation order separately from crossing. The kernel lists state hashing as broken by the crossover, and the sandbox showed that the same operations in the same order still reproduce the same bits; hashing breaks when the order can vary.
- Where/why it failed: Crossover at 21 or 22 events and Infinity at 416 or 417, depending on the start value. The float stat first differed from exact integers at event 22, the first value past 2^53, yet after 59 events its relative error was only 2.7e-17. A +1 increment was lost 0% of the time below 2^53, 50.5% in [2^53, 2^54) and 100% from 2^54 up. Two summation orders of the same 8 stats disagreed 0% of the time with the largest near 2^48 or 2^52, then 53.1%, 41.5% and 29.8% near 2^53, 2^55 and 2^60.
- Fix: Past 2^53, pin one canonical order for every sum that feeds a hash, save diff or equality check, and never rely on small increments landing. Move only those exact operations to integers or BigInt; display and combat math can stay float64.
- Why this fix: Below 2^53 integer-valued float addition is exact and so order-free; above it each addition rounds, so any code path whose iteration order can change (a Set, a Map, a parallel reduce) produces different bits. Same-order re-runs are unaffected.
- Rule of thumb: Past 2^53 a compounding stat stays accurate (relative error 2.7e-17 after 59 events) but loses +1 increments (50.5% just above 2^53, 100% from 2^54) and makes integer-valued sums order-dependent (0% of reorderings disagree below 2^53, 53.1% just above), so a fingerprint breaks where summation order can vary, not merely because the value crossed.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand in cognitive-updates/sandbox_train_gap_b03.py loop 11 in Pappydapimp69/Brain, deterministic. Observed: crossover 21-22 events and Infinity 416-417; first exact mismatch at event 22; +1 lost 0/50.5/100/100%; order disagreement 0/0/53.1/41.5/29.8%. Assumed: cross-machine bit-identity of IEEE-754 multiply and add, since this sandbox ran on one machine.
- Composed: ideas:SYSTEM/determinism/float64-safe-integer-headroom-for-compounding-stats/measure-the-crossover-dont-assume-bigint, sandbox-power-scaling/E1, sandbox-combined-loop-combat/E2

## E4 — cooldown reset discards overshoot carry needs a loop
- Date: 2026-09-28
- Project: local/sandbox-kernel-system-determinism
- Tags: [determinism][gamedev][timing][fairness]
- What: Applied the ideas kernel that a `cooldownMs -= dt` gate is a live fairness bug, reproducing its 515/540/450 moves at 60/144/30fps. All three numbers reproduced exactly and follow one formula; the kernel's claim that the gap grows with hold time is true only in absolute terms, and the obvious carry-over fix has its own boundary.
- Why built: brain train gap listed the kernel as unapplied. the-game-the-recursion/E16 and sandbox-combined-cadence-frametick/E1 recorded the bug and the integer-tick fix; neither explained the exact counts or tested keeping the float timer and carrying the overshoot.
- Design: The kernel's loop, `cd -= dt; if (cd <= 0) { move; cd = STEP }` with STEP 108 ms, over fixed hold times at fixed frame rates 20..240fps and 10 seeded jittered 60fps schedules (dt times U[0.5,1.5]). Compared against `cd += STEP` in an if, and `cd += STEP` in a while loop. Ideal is 556 moves in 60 s (one at t=0, then every 108 ms).
- Got right: Deriving a closed form before trusting the reproduction. The move period is ceil(STEP/dt) frames, so the counts are 60000/(ceil(108/dt)*dt): 450.0, 514.3 and 540.0, matching the 450, 515 and 540 the kernel measured.
- Where/why it failed: The reset form lost 19% at 30fps, 7% at 60fps and 3% at 144fps, and the worst frame rate in the sweep lost 28.1% (20fps). Between 144 and 30fps the absolute gap grew linearly with hold time (7, 22, 90, 360 moves at 5, 15, 60, 240 s) but the relative gap stayed flat at about 16.7%. Carrying the overshoot with `cd += STEP` gave 556 at 30, 60 and 144fps, but inside an if it still fires at most once per frame, so below 1000/108 fps it undercounts exactly like the reset (480 at 8fps, 300 at 5fps). The while-loop carry gave 556 at every rate and on every jittered seed; the reset form gave 512..515 across jitter seeds.
- Fix: If the timer must stay in float milliseconds, carry the overshoot with `cd += STEP` inside a `while (cd <= 0)` loop. Otherwise count integer ticks as cadence-frametick/E1 says.
- Why this fix: The bug is the reset throwing away the part of the frame past zero. Carrying it keeps the average rate at 1/STEP; the loop is needed because one frame can span more than one STEP.
- Rule of thumb: A `cd -= dt; if (cd <= 0) { fire; cd = STEP }` cadence fires every ceil(STEP/dt) frames, so its count is 60000/(ceil(108/dt)*dt) (450/515/540 at 30/60/144fps, ideal 556); carrying the overshoot fixes it only inside a while loop, since an if still caps at one fire per frame (480 at 8fps), and the fairness gap grows in absolute moves but stays about 16.7% in relative terms.

## E3 — symbolic rng keys need a purpose not a slot or draw index
- Date: 2026-09-28
- Project: local/sandbox-kernel-system-determinism
- Tags: [determinism][rng][state][testing]
- What: Applied the kernel that a shared seeded-RNG stream is an ordinal space, so changing the population shifts every later draw, and that draws should be addressed symbolically instead. Symbolic keys removed the global shift completely, but which symbol is used decides whether the fix holds.
- Why built: brain train gap listed the kernel as unapplied; the-game-prologue/E19 recorded the live instance, where quest gating perturbed the shared stream.
- Design: 20 entities over 50 ticks, each making three decisions per tick (move, attack, loot), each a draw compared to 0.3, from a seeded splitmix64. Keying modes: one shared stream; a hash of (spawn slot, tick, draw index); (stable id, tick, draw index); and (stable id, tick, purpose). Perturbation 1: remove entity 3. Perturbation 2: entity 5 inserts one new crit check before attack at tick 10.
- Got right: Adding the second perturbation. Removing an entity only tests the population case; a new check inside one entity's turn tests whether the key still carries an ordinal.
- Where/why it failed: Removing entity 3 flipped 1188 of 2850 other decisions with a shared stream and 1033 with keys built from spawn slot, a symbolic-looking key that is really a position. Stable-id keys flipped 0. Inserting a crit check flipped 991 decisions across all 20 entities with a shared stream, 2 decisions in entity 5 alone with (id, tick, draw index), and 0 with (id, tick, purpose).
- Fix: Key each draw by stable entity id, tick and the named purpose of the roll, never by spawn slot or by the draw's position within the entity's turn.
- Why this fix: The kernel's diagnosis generalises one level down: any index, whether spawn order globally or draw order within a turn, is an ordinal space and shifts under insertion. A slot is an index that looks like an identity, and a draw index keeps a small ordinal space inside each entity.

## E2 — multiplicative decay above one half never reaches zero
- Date: 2026-09-28
- Project: local/sandbox-kernel-system-determinism
- Tags: [floating-point][animation][state][determinism]
- What: Applied the kernel that an incrementally derived value can be correct at every step and still never reach its true target, with its example of a decay that never hits exact zero in floating point. Measured exactly when it never does, where it stops, and that deriving from elapsed time reaches zero.
- Why built: brain train gap listed the kernel as unapplied. Per-frame multiplicative fades (alpha *= k) are common in this canon's animation work, and done-when-alpha-is-zero is the natural completion test.
- Design: Iterate v *= k from 1.0 in IEEE doubles for k in {0.3, 0.5, 0.5000001, 0.9, 0.99, 0.999} for up to 2000000 steps, recording whether v reaches 0.0 and where it stops. A fade at k = 0.9 checked for the frame it drops below 1/255 and whether alpha == 0 ever fires in 20000 frames. The kernel's fix checked as 0.9 ** n and pow(0.9, n).
- Got right: Sweeping k across 0.5 instead of assuming floating point never reaches zero; that claim is true for only half of all decay factors.
- Where/why it failed: k = 0.3 and k = 0.5 reach exactly 0.0, after 619 and 1075 steps. Every k above 0.5 never does: v sticks at a subnormal where v*k rounds back to v, at 1, 5, 49 and 499 times the smallest subnormal for k = 0.5000001, 0.9, 0.99 and 0.999, about 0.5/(1-k). At k = 0.9 a fade is invisible (below 1/255) at frame 53, and alpha == 0 never fires: the value is still 2.5e-323 at frame 20000.
- Fix: Derive the value from elapsed frames with pow, which underflows to exactly 0.0 (0.9 ** 20000 is 0.0 in Python and JavaScript), or end the effect on a visible threshold rather than on reaching zero.
- Why this fix: The per-step multiply is exact to within rounding, and it is the rounding that holds the value up: once v is a few subnormal units, v*k is nearer v than one unit below it. Computing from elapsed time asks the true function once instead of accumulating a rounded one, which is the kernel's clamp against an independent ground truth in its simplest form.
- Rule of thumb: In IEEE doubles, value *= k never reaches exactly 0 for any k above 0.5; it sticks at about 0.5/(1-k) times the smallest subnormal (5 units at k = 0.9), so a fade whose completion test is alpha == 0 never completes. Derive it from elapsed time with pow, which does underflow to 0, or finish on a visible threshold.

Observed: k=0.3 reaches 0 after 619 steps, k=0.5 after 1075; k=0.5000001, 0.9, 0.99 and 0.999 stick at 1, 5, 49 and 499 subnormal units; fade below 1/255 at frame 53 and still 2.5e-323 at frame 20000; 0.9 ** 20000 = 0.0 in Python and Math.pow(0.9, 20000) = 0 in node. Flush-to-zero modes, common on GPUs, change this and are outside the sandbox.

## E1 — float32 truncation fails only at rounding midpoints
- Date: 2026-09-28
- Project: local/sandbox-kernel-system-determinism
- Tags: [determinism][floating-point][testing]
- What: Applied the kernel that transcendental Math results differ in their low bits across libms and that truncating to float32 right after the call discards the difference. Truncation worked everywhere except one precisely characterisable place, and whether the libm difference matters at all depended on how the result reaches the state.
- Why built: brain train gap listed the kernel as unapplied. the-game-prologue/E1 built a deterministic harness and recorded transcendental calls inside the sim as an unguarded risk; this measures the recommended guard.
- Design: Two libms modelled as math.sin and a copy that is 1 ulp off on a deterministic third of inputs. 200 seeds of 2000 ticks each, fingerprinting the final double. Two sims: one where the state is the sine output each tick (x = sin(2.9x + 0.3)) and one where the sine is a small term added to a larger state. Then 200000 ordinary sin() results rounded to float32 under both libms, and 12000 doubles placed exactly on, just below and just above float32 rounding midpoints.
- Got right: Running the absorbed-term sim at all. The first version of this sandbox used only that form, saw zero divergence even without truncation, and would have concluded the libm difference was harmless.
- Where/why it failed: With the state equal to the sine output, fingerprints differed in 200 of 200 seeds raw and 0 of 200 with float32 truncation. With the sine as a small added term, raw fingerprints differed in 0 of 200: the addition rounds the 1-ulp noise away before it reaches the state. Over 200000 ordinary results the two libms never disagreed after truncation. At float32 rounding midpoints they disagreed in 4000 of 12000 cases, every case where the 1-ulp nudge crossed the midpoint.
- Fix: Truncate to float32 after transcendental calls, and treat results landing on a float32 rounding midpoint as the residual risk. For a golden master that must be bit-proof, use the kernel's other option: a polynomial or table evaluated in deterministic arithmetic.
- Why this fix: Truncation turns cross-libm divergence from certain into confined to a small but constructible set, and a long sim evaluates enough transcendentals that rare is not never. The absorbed-term result also explains why this bug class hides: a harness whose transcendentals only nudge a large state can pass on two libms and still diverge the day one feeds state directly.
- Rule of thumb: Rounding a transcendental's result to float32 makes two libms that differ by 1 ulp agree everywhere except on float32 rounding midpoints, where they still disagree (4000 of 12000 constructed cases, 0 of 200000 ordinary ones), so it is a large mitigation rather than a guarantee; and a sim where the sine only nudges a large state can hide the libm difference entirely until one feeds state directly.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): VERIFIED first-hand in cognitive-updates/sandbox_train_gap_b01.py loop 3 in Pappydapimp69/Brain, deterministic. Observed: with state = sin(), fingerprints differ raw 200/200 and truncated 0/200; absorbed-term sim raw 0/200; float32 disagreement 0 in 200000 ordinary results and 4000 in 12000 midpoint-placed doubles. The second libm is a model (1 ulp on a third of inputs); real libm differences can exceed 1 ulp and are outside this sandbox.
