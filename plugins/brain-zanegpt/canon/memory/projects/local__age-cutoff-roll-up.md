## E1 — age cutoff family roll up what the variants established
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/age-cutoff-roll-up
- Tags: [testing][state][process]
- What: Roll-up of the age-cutoff family, about 642 project files (about 658 entries) that mostly re-verify a few claims about a contiguous-window shadow count. Grouped by claim, the family holds about nine distinct findings. Four were recomputed here from scratch: the 2-lag average is flat at every multiple of the modulus P; cover-test implementations agree only if they compare absolute landing slots in a consistent order; contiguous zero-shadow residues are 0 plus the last k-1 residues while P is at least the largest lag; and the contiguous-lower residues form one cyclic interval around 0.
- Why built: Reading 642 near-duplicate variants (about 389 of them restate the flat-at-P-and-2P result with a different cover data structure, modulus range or offset) costs far more than the handful of facts they carry. A reader needs the distinct findings, the count of variants behind each, and the edges that are still untested, in one place.
- Design: Counted first-hand by a script that sorts every family file by file-name stem and Rule of thumb into groups: flat at P and 2P with cover implementations 389, flat at every multiple of P 3, flat grids to lag 16 and totals 9, T mod P periodicity and minimal period equal to P 71, zero-shadow residues 36, contiguous-lower block 12, contiguous versus scrambled ordering 33, lossless cutoff equals the largest valid lag and validity-plus-cutoff 50, fallback guards per-press versus coverage loss and short horizons 39 (counts approximate, regex grouping). Then one stdlib sandbox with its own landing-set and bitmask code recomputes four findings, each with a negative control: F1 flats at every multiple of P for P5-P8 up to N=28 with a zero-offset control; F2 six cover tests at P6-P8 with mismatched-order, lengths-only and residues-only breakers; F3 zero residues for k=2 and 3 over P5-P11, N4-N9 at bases 100 and 237 with a threshold-free control; F4 the lower block at P9, P11, P13 for lags 1..8 and 1..12.
- Got right: F1 flats were exactly [kP] for every k up to N=28 at P5-P8 (P6 average 7/2 then 17/2, P7 38/7 then 88/7, P8 8 then 18, matching earlier recorded values), and the zero-offset control changed the flat set. F2 set, bisect, ascending merge and shared-iterator (both ascending, and both descending) all matched; one-list-reversed, lengths-only and residue-only all differed. F3 had 0 mismatches over 168 cells and the threshold-free rule was wrong on all 20 cells with P below N. F4 gave one cyclic interval containing 0 and the contiguous zero residues at P9, P11, P13, both signs of (contiguous minus scrambled) occurred inside one period, and my lags 1..12 blocks equal the earlier recorded ones.
- Where/why it failed: The narrow wording repeated by hundreds of variants, that the average is flat at N=P and N=2P, is true only inside the tested lag range (N up to 2P+1); one variant (marked incompatible) found and my sandbox confirms flats at 3P, 4P and beyond, so the variants never contradicted each other but their stated boundary was too narrow. Also the order-sensitivity of stream covers is easy to miss: reversing both lists together passes, only a mismatched order exposes the silent wrong count of 0. A residue-only cover (a separate incompatible variant) is simply wrong.
- Fix: Treat the variants as repeats of the findings below and cite this roll-up instead of adding another cover-implementation or modulus variant; if a new variant is wanted, spend it on an edge listed under limits (not covered), not on another data structure for the same subset test.
- Why this fix: Across 389 flat-at-P-and-2P variants the only result that ever differed was a variant built to break the claim (residue-only, lengths-only, any-overlap, base 0, one-list reversed); every ordinary implementation agreed, so another agreeing implementation adds no information, while an untested edge might.
- Rule of thumb: The age-cutoff family is about nine findings repeated about 640 times, so the variants are repeats of these and need not be read individually. (1) 2-lag contiguous shadow average is flat at every multiple of P (N=P, 2P, 3P, ...), not only P and 2P, once the horizon base is a few periods above 0; base 0 breaks it (about 392 variants, about 3 of them the multiples result). (2) A lag-cover test must compare absolute landing slots: set, bisect, bitmask and dict agree, stream merges and iterator covers need both lists in one common order and silently count 0 when only one is reversed, lengths-only, any-overlap and residue-only covers are wrong (about 100 variants carry such controls). (3) Contiguous always-on shadow counts repeat by T mod P with minimal period exactly P, so key tables by residue and never fold by a divisor of P (about 71). (4) Zero-shadow residues are 0 plus the last k-1 residues while P is at least the largest lag, and only 0 below it (about 36). (5) The residues where contiguous shadows no more than scrambled form one cyclic interval around 0 holding the zero residues, keyed by lag universe, not by base (about 12), while the contiguous versus scrambled ranking itself flips with horizon, modulus and fallback type (about 33). (6) Lossless cutoff is the largest lag with a valid press, not the period; a cutoff on top of validity loses exactly the valid presses above it (about 50). (7) The largest-configured-lag shortcut is exact per press for any non-empty fallback at long horizons but fails under landing-coverage loss and at short horizons, so name the loss definition and horizon (about 39). Limits of what was NOT covered: this roll-up recomputed only findings 1 to 4 and part of 5, at moduli 5 to 13, with the family's own model definition (window set, landing rule, shadow = last lag differs from cut lag), which I took from the variants and did not derive independently; findings 6 and 7, the scrambled-versus-contiguous horizon flips, quartic total formulas, jitter and frame schedules, and about 19 further files in other project ledgers that mention age cutoff were summarised from their stated rules only; counts are regex groupings and can misfile a few variants.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the shipped sandbox (RESULT: PASS): findings 1 to 4 above at the stated moduli, lag ranges and bases only, plus the family counts from a script over the project files. Findings 5 (ranking flips), 6 and 7 come from the variants' stated rules (read only).
