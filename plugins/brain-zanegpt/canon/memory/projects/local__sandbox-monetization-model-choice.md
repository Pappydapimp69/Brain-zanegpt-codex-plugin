## E1 — premium default hybrid scale up f2p needs fit revenue model
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/sandbox-monetization-model-choice
- Tags: [monetization][model][sandbox][economy]
- What: Built a deterministic 24-month revenue model of premium, free-to-play and hybrid (premium plus seasonal cosmetic drops) across a 5 by 4 grid of reach (1e4 to 1e8) and monthly retention (0.5 to 0.95), to test the kernel that premium is the default, hybrid the scale-up and free-to-play only works when the mechanics fit live ops. The ranking logic reproduced; two of my numeric guesses about where free-to-play and pay-to-win land were wrong.
- Why built: brain train kernel listed the model-choice kernel as untested (no memory entry built against it); its siblings on pay-to-win and virtual-currency spend pointed at the same revenue levers.
- Design: Premium nets buyers x price x (1 - refund) x (1 - platform cut) minus build cost. Free-to-play nets installs times a retention decay times payer share times spend, minus a live-ops bill per month, with a fit dial that scales payer share and retention. Hybrid is premium plus eight quarterly cosmetic drops bought by surviving owners, minus a per-drop cost. Reach-to-buyer and reach-to-install conversion, word of mouth tied to retention, and every cost are illustrative assumptions fixed before the first run. Then negative controls (zero live-ops cost, zero DLC attach, zero reach), a x1.5 spend multiplier, a pay-to-win pressure sweep, and a break scenario that collapses premium store conversion.
- Got right: Writing the prediction as counts first made the misses visible. Negative controls separated the model from a rigged result: with free live ops and full fit free-to-play won every reach, and with zero attach hybrid won 0 cells, so the premium-default result came from the cost structure rather than from the code.
- Where/why it failed: Three numeric guesses missed. I predicted free-to-play at full fit would win only at retention 0.85 and up with reach 1e7 and up; it won 7 of 20 cells, from reach 1e6 at retention 0.85 and 0.95 and at reach 1e8 down to retention 0.7. I predicted an interior pay-to-win optimum; in the drop-based hybrid pressure lost at every step (net 328.6k at zero pressure, 194.9k at full), and in a monthly-spend path full pressure still led at month 24 (1.59 vs 1.37 per owner). The counted miss in the prediction text was also mine: reach 1e5 and below is 8 cells, not 10.
- Fix: Keep the kernel and bound it with the numbers. At fit 0.5 premium won 12 of 20 cells (every cell at reach 1e5 and below) and hybrid 8, free-to-play 0; the reach where hybrid first beats premium fell from 1e8 at retention 0.5 to 1e7 at 0.7 and 0.85 to 1e6 at 0.95. Free-to-play needed fit near 0.8 (0.80 at reach 1e7, 0.78 at 1e8, retention 0.85), and a x1.5 spend multiplier at fit 0.2 won 0 cells. Premium lost its default only when store conversion fell to about half the base (0.0099 vs 0.020 at reach 1e7). Pay-to-win pressure stopped paying only past a churn slope of 0.18 (spend lift 1x), 0.36 (2x) or 0.72 (4x): the sibling kernel's burn needs that slope, it is not automatic.
- Why this fix: The three-part kernel survived every control, but its edges are set by two ratios: cost structure against reach, and spend lift against churn. Naming the ratios turns a practitioner slogan into something a team can plug its own numbers into.
- Rule of thumb: In this model premium is the cheapest-to-be-wrong default at small reach (12 of 20 cells at fit 0.5, all of reach 1e5 and below), hybrid overtakes it earlier as retention rises (reach 1e8 at 0.5 down to 1e6 at 0.95), and free-to-play needs mechanic fit near 0.8 at scale and wins 0 cells at fit 0.5 or with a x1.5 spend lever; re-run it with your own conversion, churn and cost numbers before moving off premium, since halving premium store conversion alone handed free-to-play 8 cells.
