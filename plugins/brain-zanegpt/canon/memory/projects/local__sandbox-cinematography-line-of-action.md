## E1 — line of action side test half open on line camera
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/sandbox-cinematography-line-of-action
- Tags: [film][geometry][testing][algorithm]
- What: A sandbox of the 180-degree rule as geometry: a real screen projection of two subjects is compared with a cross-product side test, over about 180000 pairs of camera cuts, plus the degenerate case of a camera sitting exactly on the line of action.
- Why built: The 180-degree kernel claims that crossing the line flips screen position; I wanted to see whether the side test matches a real projection and where a code check of it breaks.
- Design: Cameras on a 21x21 integer grid aim at the subjects' midpoint; screen order from a projection onto the camera right vector. Side by sign of cross(B-A, cam-A) with a half-open rule (positive is one side, zero or negative the other) versus an inclusive both-sides rule. A decimal line checked in floats against exact Fractions.
- Got right: Projection flip equals side change with 0 mismatches over 87990 pairs on a horizontal line and 91806 on a slanted integer line. The 19 on-line cameras project both subjects to the same screen x, so no flip is defined, and half-open assigns each exactly one side.
- Where/why it failed: The inclusive test put all 19 on-line cameras on both sides at once, so a cut involving one is both a crossing and not a crossing. In floats, 22 of 59 cameras placed exactly on the decimal line (0.1,0.2)-(0.7,0.9) got a nonzero cross product, while exact Fractions gave 0 of 59, so float side tests silently put on-line cameras on a side. My first two runs also failed on my own harness bugs (a boolean-set mistake and a decimal family that happened to be float-exact).
- Fix: Classify camera side with a half-open rule on an exact cross product (integers or Fractions), and treat on-line cameras as their own flagged case rather than as either side.
- Why this fix: Half-open gives every camera exactly one side, matching the point-in-polygon pattern, and exact arithmetic removes the float misclassification at the boundary.
- Rule of thumb: A line-of-action side test must use a half-open rule on an exact cross product, because an inclusive test double-assigns on-line cameras and float arithmetic misplaces about a third of exactly-on-line decimal positions; away from the line, the sign test matched a real projection in every pair tested.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; numbers copied from its output. This covers the geometry of the rule only, not the audience's perceived disorientation.

The "inclusive double-sided 19, half-open single 19" counts are true by construction (half-open always returns one of two values), so they illustrate the definition and do not test it. Swapping half-open for inclusive (>=0) leaves the off-line mismatch count at 0, which is expected because the two rules differ only on the line.
