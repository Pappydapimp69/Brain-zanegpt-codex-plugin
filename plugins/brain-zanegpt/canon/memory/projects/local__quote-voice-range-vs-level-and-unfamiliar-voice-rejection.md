## E1 — quote voice range vs level and unfamiliar voice rejection
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/quote-voice-range-vs-level-and-unfamiliar-voice-rejection
- Tags: [dialogue][speech][testing]
- What: Built from the paper's own means and SDs, pitch range separates direct quotes from narrative only moderately (AUC 0.76 over 34 vs 34 segments) and pitch level worse (0.67); pushing level up 80 Hz makes level the better cue (AUC 0.92), so the wrong dial works only when the shift exceeds natural drift; a listener cannot both accept known voices and reject a new voice 1 sigma away (best min of hit and reject 0.60), while at 4 sigma it can (1.00).
- Why built: The prosody kernel says quoted voices are marked by range not level, and the listener-limits kernel says new voices are almost never rejected; no memory entry had applied either, and together they predict how a teller should mark a character.
- Design: Deterministic quantile z-scores (no RNG) generate 34 direct and 34 narrative segments with the kernel's range stats (119 Hz SD 66 vs 61 SD 44) and level stats (205 vs 181 Hz, level SD assumed 40 Hz as the kernel gives none); contours are a fixed golden-ratio sawtooth; per-feature AUC for dense (40 points), wrong-dial (+80 Hz level) and short (3 points) segments; a three-voice listener with fixed noise offsets sweeps a reject threshold for a new voice at 1 and 4 sigma.
- Got right: Range beat level in direction (0.76 vs 0.67), the +80 Hz level push reversed it (0.92 vs 0.77 range), short segments did not weaken the range cue (0.79), and the near-voice listener showed the hit-versus-reject trade (0.60 best) that explicit marking would remove.
- Where/why it failed: My first prediction used absolute thresholds that were wrong: range AUC at least 0.80 (got 0.76), level at most 0.65 (got 0.67) and short lines below 0.75 (got 0.79); the paper's large SDs cap range at a moderate cue (a Gaussian check gives about 0.70), and a 3-point window of a sawtooth still spans a wide range, so the short-line degradation seen in the strip-test entry did not transfer to this cue. The second prediction was written after seeing run one and is stated as such.
- Fix: Treat range as a modest, additive marker, never a classifier by itself: combine it with the phrase boundary before the quote (the kernel's second independent cue) and, for a new character, add an explicit introduction rather than relying on a distinct voice.
- Why this fix: Range alone leaves about a quarter of segment pairs misordered at the paper's own variance, and the listener sweep shows a close voice cannot be both recognised and rejected by threshold, so a second cue and explicit marking carry the load.
- Rule of thumb: When a teller shifts voice, widen pitch range and break the phrase before the quote rather than raising or lowering pitch, but expect only a moderate cue (AUC about 0.76 at the paper's variance); a level shift works too only if it exceeds natural drift (about 2 SD, here 80 Hz), and a new voice within about 1 sigma of a known one needs explicit marking.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the shipped sandbox (RESULT: PASS on the second prediction; the first prediction failed on three absolute thresholds, reported above); the level SD, the sawtooth contour and the listener noise offsets are modelling assumptions, not measured, and nothing here was checked against real speech.
