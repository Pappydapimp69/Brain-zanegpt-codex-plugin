## E1 — bar quantized horizontal vs stem fade flip phase rate sweep
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/sandbox-bar-quantized-horizontal-vs-stem-fade-flip-phase-rate-sweep
- Tags: [music][audio][design][state]
- What: A deterministic integer-tick audio-timeline model (100 ticks per second, 2 s bar, 0.5 s beat, 64 s) renders sample envelopes for seven strategies on a binary combat state: horizontal bar-quantized cut, beat-quantized cut, immediate cut, bar-quantized linear crossfade, bar-quantized sequential fade-out-then-in, vertical stem fade, and vertical fade whose start is bar-quantized. It sweeps flip phase (0 to 1.75 s in quarter-second steps), flip rate (dwell 1 to 24 s), and fade length (0.25 to 4 s), and measures tracking error, onset and offset latency, off-beat step events, misaligned downbeats, harmonic-overlap clash and dead air from the rendered envelope.
- Why built: The open tension asks whether bar-quantized horizontal switching beats vertical stem fading for binary combat state, or only when flips land on the bar grid; an earlier tick model gave a few phases and periods only. Supports pole B of tension 'Does bar-quantized horizontal switching beat vertical stem fading for binary combat state, or only when flips land on the bar grid?' (B = wins only on the grid), with the qualifier that the crossover is set by the vertical fade length.
- Design: Each authored segment is a bar-periodic pulse envelope (calm: soft downbeat; combat: strong downbeat plus beats) over a constant pad, played on its own clock restarted at switch time; vertical uses one global timeline with a combat stem gain chasing the state at 1 over fade per second. Flips start at 4 s plus a phase and alternate with equal on and off dwell. Tracking error is the mean absolute gap between combat gain and state; latency is time to cross 0.5, capped at the next flip; meter break is the share of downbeat-sized onsets in the render that are off the bar grid. No random module, one fixed-seed linear congruential generator for a random-dwell trace.
- Got right: Tracking error of a bar cut is lag over dwell with lag equal to the time to the next bar line, so at dwell 8 s the bar cut error runs 0.000 at phase 0 and 0.219, 0.188, 0.156, 0.125, 0.094, 0.063, 0.031 at phases 0.25 s to 1.75 s, against 0.062 for a 1 s vertical fade and 0.124 for 2 s. The bar cut beats the vertical fade in 1 of 8 phases at 0.5 s fade, 2 of 8 at 1 s, 4 of 8 at 2 s, 6 of 8 at 3 s and 8 of 8 at 4 s, and the crossover fade per phase tracks twice the lag (3.75 s measured against 3.5 s predicted at the worst phase, within one grid step). Immediate and beat-quantized cuts track well (error 0.000 and at most 0.031) but break the meter: 94 percent of downbeats land off the bar grid at 7 of 8 phases for the immediate cut and 6 of 8 for the beat cut, while bar cut and vertical show 0.
- Where/why it failed: Two of my pre-written predictions failed and were not retuned. My click count only looked at downward envelope drops, and a cut always ends in a bigger upward onset, so it read 0 everywhere (an instrument defect, replaced by off-beat step events, which gave 312 for the immediate cut and 0 for bar cut and vertical). I also predicted that flips at or below one bar would defeat both strategies, but dwell 2 s with phase 0 is bar-aligned and the bar cut tracked it perfectly (error 0.000 against 0.232 vertical). Off-grid at dwell 2 s the bar cut is worse than chance (0.82 at phase 0.25 s against 0.50 for constant music) because sampling the state once per bar sees it nearly inverted. The model has no cost for a short vertical fade, so vertical wins whenever its fade may be short; the clash and dead-air measures separated only horizontal transition styles (crossfade clash 0.200 of the time, sequential dip dead air 0.099), never horizontal from vertical.
- Fix: Treat the choice as a comparison of two numbers, the time to the next bar line at the flip phase and half the vertical fade length: pick the bar cut only when the flips are bar-synchronous or when the vertical fade you can afford is longer than twice the typical wait for the next bar line; otherwise use a short vertical fade. Where flips can be as fast as the bar and are not synchronous, add hysteresis or a minimum dwell instead of a layering choice.
- Why this fix: With these measures the two strategies tie on every cost except lag, and the lag of the cut is a sawtooth in phase while the lag of the fade is a constant; so the winner is a closed form (cut wins a fraction of phases equal to fade over twice the bar) rather than a property of binary versus continuous state.
- Rule of thumb: For a binary combat state, a bar-quantized cut beats a vertical fade on tracking only when wait-to-next-bar is under half the fade (about 1 of 8 phases at a 0.5 s fade, 4 of 8 at 2 s, all at 4 s or longer), so on off-grid flips use a short stem fade; the cut also needs bar-synchronous flips of one bar or slower, since off-grid at one bar it tracks worse than constant music.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand: every number above was printed by the shipped sandbox, which is deterministic and was run repeatedly with identical output; the first-run failures are recorded in its docstring. Model assumptions, listed separately: that vertical stems are harmonically compatible by authoring, that segments restart at their first bar rather than at a phrase-aware entry point, and that horizontal segments offer musical contrast the metrics do not credit; listener perception is outside the run.
