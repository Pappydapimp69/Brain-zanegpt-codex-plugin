## E1 — fibonacci lag cutoff makes the gate only invalid count small not worst
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Project: local/fibonacci-lag-cutoff-makes-the-gate-only-invalid-count-small-not-worst
- Tags: [ui][input][state][gamedev][testing]
- What: On a Fibonacci-word legality signal over 600 ticks, the number of stale presses a gate-only policy would execute does not rise with lag as the periodic formula does: it falls from 229 at lag 1 to 4 at lag 55 and 3 at lag 89, against a median of 167 at the non-Fibonacci lags 40 to 70, so a lag that is lossless with validity is not the worst gate-only lag there.
- Why built: Train pick joining the lossless-at-max-lag lesson and the gate-only worst-cutoff lesson with the quasiperiodic near-recovery lesson, to see whether the "lossless cutoff is the worst cutoff without validity" rule survives a non-periodic signal.
- Design: Fibonacci word from the substitution 1 to 10 and 0 to 1 with legal meaning symbol 1; invalid(L) counts t in 0..599 where the signal is legal at t and illegal at t plus L; compared against the Fibonacci lags 1 to 89 and the 40 to 70 window; a period-12 width-5 signal is the control for the closed formula.
- Got right: Near recovery at Fibonacci lags carried over (invalid 4 and 3 at lags 55 and 89), invalid was not monotone in lag, and the periodic control matched (N/P) times min(L, W, P-L, P-W) exactly on lags 1 to 11.
- Where/why it failed: Nothing in the prediction failed, but the claim only covers one quasiperiodic signal and one window length; I did not test whether the lossless cutoff on this signal lands on a Fibonacci lag, which would decide how often the rule is actually broken in practice.
- Fix: Limit the worst-cutoff rule to periodic legality; on quasiperiodic legality scan the gate-only invalid count per lag instead of assuming it rises with the cutoff.
- Why this fix: The rule rests on a formula that increases with lag until a plateau, which is a periodic property, and the Fibonacci signal violates exactly that shape.
- Rule of thumb: The rule that a lossless cutoff is the worst gate-only cutoff is a periodic-signal result; on a Fibonacci-word signal the gate-only invalid count is not monotone in lag and is near zero at Fibonacci lags, so measure it per lag.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox in this session on one Fibonacci word of 720 ticks and one periodic control; no other quasiperiodic signal was run.

- Hidden assumption: The low counts at Fibonacci lags come from the signal being a Fibonacci word. That makes the result a property of its golden-ratio structure (distance of L·φ to the nearest integer) and says nothing about "lossless cutoffs" in general. It also assumes the t+L lookup past tick 599 is handled neutrally, with the signal generated to 600+L rather than truncated or treated as illegal or skipped.
- Would prove it wrong: Low counts at non-Fibonacci lags with small ‖Lφ‖ (Lucas lags such as 47 and 76) would show the drop follows irrational-rotation proximity, not Fibonacci lags as such. A count at lag 55 or 89 that rises by 3x or more once the signal is extended to 600+L, or when the window length changes, would show a boundary artifact. Gate-only invalid that stays high at Fibonacci lags on a signal that is not a Sturmian word would show the claim doesn't transfer.
- Break scenario: Take a non-Sturmian aperiodic signal with the same 0.618 density, such as a random Bernoulli(0.618) sequence or the Thue-Morse word recoded to that density. Run lags 1 to 89 and the window 40 to 70 over 600 ticks, with the signal generated to 689 ticks. If lag 55 and lag 89 land near the median instead of at 10 or fewer, the rule describes one sequence and not a gate-only property.
