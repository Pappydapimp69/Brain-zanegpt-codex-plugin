## E1 — home soil jar test stokes timing and leaf triage order
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/2026-10-09__home-soil-jar-test-stokes-timing-and-leaf-triage-order
- Tags: [garden][soil][testing][measurement]
- What: The home-soil kernel's jar-test timings (sand about 1 minute, silt by about 3 minutes, clay 24-48 hours) do not recover a 40/40/20 loam in a Stokes-law model: the readout gives 47.6 sand / 7.7 silt / 44.6 clay, a 32-point error, because only silt coarser than about 25 micrometres has settled at 3 minutes. Reading silt at 2 hours gives 47.6/33.8/18.6 (7.6 points worst error). The kernel's triage order does hold: a leaf-colour-only proxy sends fertilizer to the wrong condition in 4 of 14 cases, a pH-then-structure-then-leaf order in 0 of 14.
- Why built: The kernel was filed from extension sources but no memory entry had applied it; the three diagnostics are stated as cheap and reliable, and I wanted to check the numbers and the ordering with a model instead of trusting the prose.
- Design: Stdlib-only deterministic sandbox. S1 uses Stokes settling (10 cm fill, 20 C, also 10 C) over log-uniform particle grids per USDA size class and reads texture at chosen times. S2 builds a symptom table from the kernel's own statements (old-leaf yellow for nitrogen, purple older leaves for phosphorus, older-margin burn for potassium, new-leaf yellow for high pH, pooling for compaction, old-leaf yellow when low pH cuts nutrient availability) with 2 overlap variants per condition, and scores a leaf-only rule against a gated rule. S3 combines causes and compares one pass with a 3-pass retest loop. S4 checks the sibling compost kernel's 4:1 browns to greens by volume against a 30:1 carbon to nitrogen target using assumed dry-matter per litre.
- Got right: Triage ordering held: leaf-only was correct 10/14 and wasted fertilizer 4 times (compaction and low pH both look like nitrogen shortage, so three conditions collapse onto one reading); the gated order was 14/14 with 0 wasted. Compound causes (low pH plus N, compaction plus N, low pH plus compaction plus K) were fixed 1 of 2, 1 of 2 and 1 of 3 in one pass but fully in a 3-pass retest loop.
- Where/why it failed: The jar timings. Silt is not down in 3 minutes: 0.025 mm silt takes 3.0 min, 0.010 mm takes 18.6 min, 0.002 mm takes 7.7 hours. Reading silt at 3 minutes made silt 7.7 and clay 44.6 for the loam, 4/21 for a sandy mix (true 20/10), 7/62 for a clayey mix (true 30/50), and 8/46 in 10 C water. Silt read at 1/3/10/30/120 minutes rose 0.0/7.7/16.3/24.0/33.8. The compost sibling also missed: 4:1 browns to greens by volume gave C:N 41.1 (35.7 to 46.3 over a 25% density sweep), 1:1 was nearest 30 at 27.4.
- Fix: Take the silt mark at about 2 hours, keep the 1 minute sand mark and the 24-48 hour total, and treat the result as a rough estimate; run pH and a pooling or crusting check before reading leaves, and re-observe after each fix when two causes may overlap. Read the compost 4:1 as a starting point and adjust by pile temperature and smell rather than trusting the ratio.
- Why this fix: Settling time scales with the square of particle diameter, so the silt class spans from under a minute to several hours and no single early mark separates it; and the leaf-colour reading discards the pH and structure information that separates look-alike conditions, which is the same lossy-projection shape as a count standing in for a pattern.
- Rule of thumb: Check the clock on the jar test before trusting a texture percentage: in this model silt had settled only 7.7 of 40 points at 3 minutes and needed about 2 hours; and for yellowing plants, test pH and soil structure before choosing a fertilizer, because three distinct conditions here gave the identical leaf reading, then re-observe, since one fix can unmask a second cause.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox: Stokes times, texture readouts, triage tallies and C:N values are observed output. Assumptions named: log-uniform particle sizes inside each class, particle density 2650, a 0.2 micrometre clay floor (so the 48 hour total omits the finest clay and inflates sand), a hand-built symptom table taken from the kernel text (the triage result checks ordering logic, not plant biology), and assumed dry-matter per litre and nitrogen percentages for the compost mixes (so the C:N figure is model-dependent, not a measured pile).
