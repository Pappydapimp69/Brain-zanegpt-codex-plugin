## E1 — humor four style classes break when latent axes correlate dimensions survive
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/humor-styles
- Tags: [humor][psychometrics][simulation][typology]
- What: Seed entry for the humor-styles domain. A four-subscale humor frame (affiliative, self-enhancing, aggressive, self-defeating) can be modelled as a 2x2 of two axes: benign vs injurious use, and other-directed vs self-directed target. A deterministic simulation of 3000 respondents answering 32 original stand-in items (8 per style) tested whether the four styles are recoverable as hard classes (max subscale) and as dimensions, and showed that correlated latent axes make the hard-class reading unstable while a general score stays reliable.
- Why built: To seed an empty domain with a testable structural claim and a built-to-break case for the "four distinct styles" reading. The model covers measurement, scoring and stability only; it makes no claim about psychological truth or about any real person, and every number is a property of the stated generative assumptions.
- Design: Four axis latents (benign B, injurious J, self S, other O) with corr(B,J)=corr(S,O)=rho and cross-axis independence. Each style trait is c times the sum of its two axis cells over sqrt2, plus sqrt(1-c^2) of style-specific variance. Items load 0.55-0.80 on their trait, two per scale also cross-load 0.25 on a neighbouring style, and responses are cut to 5 points. Two independent administrations per respondent give retest class agreement. Scenarios: S0 null (items carry no signal), S1 clean (c=0.6, rho=0), S2 mid (rho=0.5), S3 BREAK (c=0.9, rho=0.8), a noise sweep x0.5 to x2.0, and a 3x3 grid over c and rho. Source frame is recalled from search-result summaries of the 2003 four-style questionnaire paper (Martin, Puhlik-Doris, Larsen, Gray, Weir): four scales of 8 items, two goals crossed with benign/detrimental manner. The paper's own correlation table was read nowhere here, so any real inter-scale value is unchecked.
- Got right: Predicted null agreement 0.25 and got 0.252. Clean case held: alpha 0.86, every one of 32 items correlates highest with its own scale (1.00), mean |inter-scale r| 0.17, retest class agreement 0.694, accuracy against the latent argmax 0.784. Break case: mean |inter-scale r| 0.65, retest class agreement fell to 0.525 and 68% of respondents had a top-two margin under 0.5 SD, while the four-scale sum retested at 0.96 and the two axis contrasts only at 0.68 and 0.70. Agreement fell monotonically with axis correlation at c=0.9 (0.624, 0.598, 0.527).
- Where/why it failed: One of seven predictions missed (P3). I expected the break case to push the mean disattenuated inter-scale correlation to at least 0.85 (the usual discriminant-validity alarm); it was 0.75 (latent 0.70), so the standard "scales are distinct" screen still passes while hard classes are unstable. Also the axis correlation alone is a weak lever at c=0.6 (agreement 0.697 to 0.646 as rho goes 0 to 0.8); it takes strong axis determination of the styles (c=0.9) plus high rho to reach 0.53, and item noise is as strong a lever (clean case 0.81 at noise x0.5 down to 0.51 at x2.0). The break parameters are a chosen extreme, not an estimate of any real sample.
- Fix: Do not print a hard type label from max subscale on its own. Report the four z-scores plus the two axis contrasts and a general-use score, show the top-two margin, and assign a label only above a margin band (a margin of at least 0.75 SD kept only 14% of respondents in the break case, with agreement 0.76 among them, versus 0.525 for everyone) otherwise report "mixed". Measure retest class agreement before trusting any label, and treat a passing disattenuated-r screen as necessary, not sufficient.
- Why this fix: Retest class agreement is the direct test of whether a label survives re-asking. Even the clean case reached only 0.694, so a label stability check is needed whatever the axis structure. Under correlated axes the info that stays reliable is the general score (0.96), and the contrasts are the weaker part (0.68-0.70), so reporting them with their reliability is honest while a bare class label hides it.
- Rule of thumb: For a four-subscale frame built on two correlated axes, check retest agreement of the max-subscale label and the top-two margin before reading the four styles as classes. In this simulation correlated axes cut agreement from 0.69 to 0.53 and a passing disattenuated-correlation screen (0.75) did not flag it, so report profile plus margin, and label only above a margin band.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand: the sandbox run (fixed seed, rerun-identical, stdlib only), which produced every number above under the generative assumptions stated in Design; 6 of 7 written predictions held and P3 is recorded as missed. Source-frame facts about the 2003 questionnaire are recalled secondhand and are labelled as such in Design. The simulated respondents are synthetic, so nothing here describes real people.
