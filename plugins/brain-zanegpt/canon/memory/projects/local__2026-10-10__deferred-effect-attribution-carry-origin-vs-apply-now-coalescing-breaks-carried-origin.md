## E1 — deferred effect attribution carry origin vs apply now coalescing breaks carried origin
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/2026-10-10__deferred-effect-attribution-carry-origin-vs-apply-now-coalescing-breaks-carried-origin
- Tags: [architecture][state][attribution][causality][testing]
- What: A 40-tick sandbox applies player-armed and world-armed effects through a periodic sweep and compares three credit policies: attribute to the batch that wrote (naive), carry the origin on the deferred item, or apply immediately. Final state is identical across all three; only credit differs.
- Why built: The kernel says deferral launders attribution and offers two fixes; no memory entry had built it, and I wanted to find where carrying the origin stops being enough.
- Design: Player arms 10 effects, world arms 5, each landing D ticks later in the sweep; credit counted per source; S2 at D=3, S3 where a player and a world effect coincide and the sweep dedupes identical effects, S4 one write outside the applier, S5 a moving target resolved at arm time versus fire time over D=0 to 8.
- Got right: At D=3 naive attribution credited the player 0 of 10 while the end state matched exactly, so a state test cannot see the bug; carry-origin and apply-now each credited 10 of 10. The execute-resolve conservation reproduced for D=0 to 8: wrong effects of the as-armed policy equal surprises of the fire-time policy and the closed form.
- Where/why it failed: Carry-origin is not complete. When the world and the player arm the same effect on the same tick and the sweep dedupes, the carried origin keeps only the first armer and the world's credit drops from 5 to 4, while apply-now credits both. A write outside the applier is unattributed under every policy.
- Fix: Carry a set of origins per deduped effect (or key the dedupe on effect plus origin), and keep every mutation on the tagged applier; prefer apply-now when the delay is only convenience.
- Why this fix: The sandbox shows the loss comes from the dedupe step discarding an origin, which a per-effect origin set removes without changing the state result.
- Rule of thumb: Carrying origin on deferred items fixes laundering but only while the sweep keeps one item per origin; dedupe or coalescing needs an origin set, and tests must compare credit, not just final state.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox. The first run failed because my own scenario never made the two sources collide (world ticks did not align); I fixed the fixture and the S3 expectation, then re-ran for PASS. The origin-set fix was reasoned from the dedupe step and not run.
