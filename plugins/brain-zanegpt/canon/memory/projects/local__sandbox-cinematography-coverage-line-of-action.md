## E1 — master shot safety net vs line of action cut counting
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/sandbox-cinematography-coverage-line-of-action
- Tags: [film][editing][sandbox][method]
- What: A discrete coverage model of one scene: 8 ordered beats, one shot chosen per beat, shot set = a master plus two singles plus an optional neutral insert. It counts the editor's valid shot-per-beat assignments (edits) and the distinct cut-position sets (pacing patterns, at most 128) with and without the master, with a line-of-action side rule that forbids cuts across the line, then breaks the master's safety-net role four ways.
- Why built: The coverage kernel says the master, shot first, lets the editor restructure pacing and patch gaps without reshoots; the 180-degree kernel says crossing the line disorients. I wanted to see how many cuts the master really buys, and which of the two claims fails when the master is on the wrong side or does not match the singles.
- Design: Pure Python, exact integer cross product for each shot's screen side per beat (half-open or neutral rule for a camera exactly on the line), a dynamic program over beats checked against brute force enumeration, prop-continuity drift (must be equal across a cut), performance deviation (within a tolerance across a cut), defect beats where a take is unusable, and a seeded linear congruential sweep of 300 random defect draws at four rates.
- Got right: With clean coverage and every camera on one side the master multiplies edits (11664 with, 576 without) but the pacing patterns are 128 in both, so the master adds options, not rhythms. With defects (A out on beats 2-3, B out on 3-4) the no-master set has 0 valid edits and the master set 1728. In the random sweep the no-master feasibility falls from 94 percent to 30 percent as the defect rate goes 0.1 to 0.4, while a clean right-side master stays at 100 percent. The line rotating under a fixed camera flips that camera's side after the move (side -1 to +1) while the master's does not.
- Where/why it failed: The master is a net for existence, not for pacing. A wrong-side master with no neutral shot leaves exactly 1 edit and 1 pattern (all master, zero cuts); the same fixture with a neutral insert on 2 beats leaves 16 edits and 4 patterns versus 1728 and 128. Prop drift on the master from beat 3 leaves 12 edits and 4 patterns with no cut after boundary 1 (pacing frozen), and a performance deviation of 2 beyond tolerance 1 leaves 36 edits versus 1728, cured at tolerance 2. A drifted or wrong-side master stays 100 percent feasible at every defect rate because the all-master edit is always valid, so my own first prediction that these break feasibility was refuted; only a defective master loses feasibility (60 percent at rate 0.4). In the sweep a wrong-side master with no neutral adds 0 or 1 pacing pattern per trial over having no master. On an on-line camera the half-open zero rule is a convention, not a fact: 21264, 7249 and 25455 edits for zero-to-negative, zero-to-positive and neutral. Two of my later mechanism guesses also missed: the full 128 patterns appear once a neutral shot covers 7 of 8 beats (not only 8), and only 9 of 256 neutral-coverage subsets saturate, every one with 7 or 8 neutral beats (odd beats alone give 40), so touching every cut boundary is not enough because one repeated neutral shot makes no cut against itself.
- Fix: Judge a master by the pacing patterns it keeps reachable, not by whether an edit exists. Shoot or flag the master on the same side as the singles, shoot a tolerance-matched take of props and performance, and plan neutral cutaways that cover most of the beats near any gap; treat an on-line camera as a flagged neutral case instead of letting a half-open convention decide the count.
- Why this fix: The counts show the master's value sits in feasibility and in cuts into and out of it; both vanish when the line-of-action or the continuity relation isolates it, and the neutral shot is the only measured way to rejoin the isolated master, at a cost of covering most beats.
- Rule of thumb: A master shot guarantees that some edit exists, but it restores a restructurable cut only if it sits on the singles' side of the line and matches their continuity and performance; otherwise it contributes one zero-cut edit, and only neutral cutaways that cover nearly all the beats win the pacing back.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; every count above is copied from its output, and the dynamic program matched brute force enumeration in each checked configuration. This is a discrete model with hand-picked fixtures and one seeded sweep; it describes edit counting only, and viewer disorientation or real production tolerances are outside what it measured.
