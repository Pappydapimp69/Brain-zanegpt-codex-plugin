## E1 — analogy bias readout 3cosadd hides what parallel offset shows
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Project: local/sandbox-analogy-bias-readout
- Tags: [nlp][fairness][measurement][testing]
- What: Toy deterministic model of the word-embedding gender-stereotype kernel: a synthetic corpus gives 14 occupations (5 male-skewed, 5 female-skewed, 4 neutral) and 12 gender-definitional pairs a cue co-occurrence profile, PPMI plus a low-rank eigen-decomposition (rank 6) gives dense vectors, and analogy answers are read off. The headline composition "corpus bias is absorbed and then shows up as confident analogy arithmetic" was refuted for the standard nearest-neighbour (3CosAdd) readout and reproduced only for a neighbourhood-constrained parallel-offset readout.
- Why built: The kernel (ideas:MACHINE-LEARNING/fairness/word-embeddings) has no memory entry that applied it; the queue asked for a build. The question was whether the stereotype appears in analogy answers because of the corpus, the dimensionality, or the way the answer is read out, and whether siblings (adversarial sign-gradient perturbation, k-shot examples) and a symmetric control compose with it.
- Design: Biased corpus: male-skewed occupations take the male cue 85% of the time they take a cue, female-skewed the female cue 85%. Control corpus: identical vocabulary and topics, every occupation 50/50. 40 biased corpora (seeds 0-39) and up to 400 control corpora (seeds 1000-1399), each a fixed-seed generator, rank 6 unless swept. Readouts: (a) 3CosAdd man:x::woman:? with inputs excluded; (b) parallel offset (candidates within the median pairwise distance of x, scored by cosine of y-x with woman-man); (c) forced-choice man:m::woman:{f vs same-topic male distractor}. Score = share of female-skewed answers among male- and female-skewed answers. Extras: exact binomial power of a control, neutralizing the gender direction (occupations only vs every word), a sign-gradient (FGSM-style) perturbation of the query, k-shot offsets from k definitional example pairs, a rank sweep, and an offset amplification sweep.
- Got right: The symmetric control and the paired design were the useful part: they exposed that 3CosAdd shows a LOWER female-coded share on the biased corpus (0.220) than on the unbiased control (0.301), so a lone biased-corpus number would have looked like nothing and a lone control like noise. The parallel-offset readout separates the two cleanly (0.989 biased vs 0.456 control) and survives ranks 3-29 (min 0.74). A forced-choice gender test scored 1.00 intact. Exact power arithmetic showed a 30-corpus control flags a true 0.60 share 18% of the time versus 98% at 400.
- Where/why it failed: Predictions that failed (14 of 24 held). (1) 3CosAdd gave the female-skewed answer in 0 of 40 corpora (needed >=80%); 199 of 200 answers were just the input's own nearest topic neighbour (engineer, carpenter, doctor, nurse), so the arithmetic mostly returns "a word like the input". (2) The textbook definitional analogy was a weak check: queen came back 40/40 even after the gender direction was removed from every word, because queen is the royal-topic neighbour of king; only a forced-choice test (1.00 intact, 0.61 with every word neutralized, chance 0.5) isolates gender. (3) The sign-gradient flip needed 0.30 x mean|q_i| for the legitimate queen answer (cosine margin 0.074) but 0.94 for the female-coded answers (margin 0.295), ratio 3.17, so the stereotyped answer was the MORE robust one, not the fragile one. (4) k-shot offsets (k=1,2,4,8 definitional pairs) did not move the female-coded share (0.220, 0.220, 0.215, 0.215) and held-out definitional accuracy slipped 0.47 to 0.44, so more examples did not help. (5) Occupation projection on the gender direction tracked the design label only r=0.774 (needed 0.9) and the control showed |r| 0.433, because topic domain is confounded with label by construction. (6) Neutralizing occupations pushed the parallel-offset share to 0.348, below the control's 0.456 (over-correction toward male-skewed topic neighbours), though a held-out gender direction showed |r| 0.211 against 0.774 baseline.
- Fix: Read bias with a readout that cancels topic similarity instead of ranking by it, and amplify or constrain accordingly: parallel-offset or forced-choice for detection, and the 3CosAdd gender offset scaled up (share 0.22, 0.22, 0.40, 0.995, 0.995 at offset multipliers 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, versus control flat at 0.31-0.32) when the arithmetic itself is under test. Always run the same readout on a matched unbiased control corpus with hundreds of trials before reading any single number. Do not treat the queen analogy as evidence the gender axis works.
- Why this fix: The multiplier sweep and the nearest-neighbour count (199/200) together show the bias signal is present in the geometry but swamped by the input's own vector at multiplier 1; the paired control keeps the readout honest because the control stayed flat while the biased arm switched on between multipliers 4 and 8. The forced-choice test removes the topic confound by construction.
- Rule of thumb: Do not judge whether an embedding carries a corpus bias from nearest-neighbour analogy answers: on the biased toy corpus 3CosAdd returned the input's own topic neighbour 199/200 times and scored lower (0.22) than the unbiased control (0.30), while a topic-constrained parallel-offset readout scored 0.99 against 0.46; measure with a matched control corpus (hundreds of trials, a 30-trial control detects a true 0.60 skew only 18% of the time), a readout that cancels topic similarity, and a forced-choice gender test, and do not read a correct queen as proof the gender axis works. Scope: one toy PPMI-plus-eigendecomposition model, not a trained neural embedding.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by the sandbox in this entry (three run iterations, 24 scored predictions, 14 held; all numbers above are from its printed output). The kernel's own paper claims were taken from the kernel text and were not re-derived here; whether a trained neural embedding behaves like this toy is an open question this run does not answer.
