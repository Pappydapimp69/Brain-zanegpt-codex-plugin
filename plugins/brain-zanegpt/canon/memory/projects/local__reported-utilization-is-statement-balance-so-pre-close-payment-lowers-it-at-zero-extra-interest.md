## E1 — reported utilization is statement balance so pre close payment lowers it at zero extra interest
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Project: local/reported-utilization-is-statement-balance-so-pre-close-payment-lowers-it-at-zero-extra-interest
- Tags: [personal-finance][testing][model]
- What: In a card cycle model with a 10000 limit and 3000 spent evenly, paying 2900 the day before statement close reports 1.0 percent utilization, while paying the same 2900 after close but before the due date still reports 30.0 percent, and neither route costs interest if the card is cleared by the due date.
- Why built: Gap pick on the credit utilization kernel, whose practical advice (pay before the statement closes, not just before the due date) had no build behind it; the interest and compounding kernels were added to price the alternative of carrying the balance.
- Design: 30-day cycle with 100 spent per day, close at day 30, balances reported as statement balance over limit; scenarios pay in full only at the due date, pay 2900 on day 29, and pay 2900 after close; a carried 3000 balance at 22.15 percent APR compounded monthly prices the interest route.
- Got right: Reported utilization came out 30.0, 1.0 and 30.0 percent as predicted, the carried balance cost 55.38 in month one and 24.5 percent effective annual rate, above the nominal 22.15 and the 10.47 percent market average.
- Where/why it failed: Nothing in the prediction failed, but the zero interest in the pay-in-full scenarios is a grace-period assumption written into the model, not a computed result, and real issuers report on different dates and scoring models differ, so no score effect is claimed.
- Fix: When the goal is a low reported utilization, schedule the paydown before the statement close date and keep paying the rest by the due date; do not carry a balance to lower the number, since carrying costs the effective rate.
- Why this fix: The bureau sees the balance at close, so only a payment before close changes the reported ratio, and the grace period means that payment costs nothing extra while a carried balance costs a compounded 20 percent plus.
- Rule of thumb: Reported utilization is the statement balance over the limit, so a payment made before close moves it (30.0 to 1.0 percent in the model) and the same payment after close does not; never carry a balance to manage the ratio.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox in this session; the statement-balance reporting rule and grace period are modeled assumptions from the kernels, not checked against an issuer, and no score formula was modeled.

- Hidden assumption: Issuers report the statement-close balance to bureaus, and the model's single card with one close date, steady 100/day spending and a payment that posts before close is how real issuers and scoring models behave. Real issuers vary on reporting date, posting lag, and whether a score uses the statement balance, the latest balance or a trailing average.
- Would prove it wrong: A real issuer's bureau file, or a scoring model that uses a trailing average or the current balance, showing about 30% after a day-29 payment of 2900. A payment that takes 2 days to post would show the same thing, because it would land after close.
- Break scenario: The issuer reports on a date other than the statement close, such as the day-15 balance of 1500, or the payment posts on day 31. Reported utilization then stays near 15% or 30%, and the model's "pay before close" rule has no effect.
