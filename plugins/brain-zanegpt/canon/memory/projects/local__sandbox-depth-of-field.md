## E1 — shallow focus separates only past the depth of field deep focus hits a diffraction floor
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/sandbox-depth-of-field
- Tags: [cinematography][optics][sandbox][verification]
- What: A thin-lens depth-of-field model (blur disc from image-plane geometry, circle of confusion at 1/1500 of the sensor diagonal, hyperfocal distance, diffraction disc added in quadrature, subject distance, focal length, sensor size) with one separation measure: the blur of a second subject in circles of confusion when the first is in focus. It tests the deep-focus-versus-shallow-focus kernel: what controls deep versus shallow, where deep focus cannot be reached, and whether shallow focus directs attention when two subjects stand close in depth.
- Why built: The kernel says deep focus lets the viewer choose where to look and shallow focus forces the choice. That is a statement about optics plus a statement about viewers; only the optics can be run. The sandbox asks how far the optical precondition (the unfocused subject really reads as blurred) holds, and where the deep end is physically out of reach.
- Design: Closed-form blur cross-checked against an independent image-plane calculation (difference 3e-15 mm). Elasticities by finite differences of log blur. Framing held fixed by solving the focus distance from a frame that covers 2 m of subject, so changing focal length also moves the camera (the compression effect itself is left to the sibling kernel and not measured here). Deep-focus scan: for each format, sixth-stop f-numbers, focus set to equalise the near and far plane, worst-plane total blur divided by the circle. Two-shot table at one fixed framing for three lens settings. Nine predictions written before the first run, then three post-hoc information lines that are not counted in the verdict.
- Got right: Aperture elasticity is exactly -1, focal length at fixed subject distance is +2.02, subject distance at fixed lens is -1.93, and sensor size at fixed field of view and distance is +0.99 as a share of frame width (a 1-inch sensor at f/1.03 matches full frame at f/2.8 to within 1%). At fixed framing, going from 24 to 200 mm changes the blur of a partner 0.3 m behind the subject by only x1.13 but a background 50 m behind by x6.5, so focal length matters through how far the background is, not through the lens. Spans for a near partner: aperture f/1.4 to f/16 x11.4, sensor 1-inch to medium format x5.2, focal length x1.13. Full-frame 50 mm f/8 hyperfocal distance 10.47 m, near limit 5.23 m. On full frame 50 mm, deep focus from 0.6 m to 12 m is unreachable: the best f-number is f/38 and the worst plane is still 2.64 circles blurred, at a cost of 8.5 stops of light against f/2. The same pair is reachable on the 22 by 16 mm frame with a 24 mm lens only from 3 m to infinity (0.89 circles at f/8.5), and a 1-inch sensor with a 9 mm lens does a 1 m to 10 m stage at 0.9 circles. Medium format is the worst case (f/47 and 2.0 circles for 1 m to 10 m), because a bigger sensor needs a longer lens for the same framing.
- Where/why it failed: One of nine pre-registered checks missed, the two-shot numbers, and the miss is arithmetic in the prediction, not the mechanism. The prediction used a 3 m standing distance for the 35 mm lens, while the fixed-framing solve puts it at 2.90 m (and the 85 mm lens at 7.05 m, not 7.3 m). Predicted 0.70 and 1.54 circles; observed 0.78 and 1.70; the lens ratio (x1.095, inside its band) and the verdict that a 35 mm lens at f/2.8 leaves a partner 0.5 m behind sharp both held. What the sandbox cannot say: it models blur, not viewers, so whether blur contrast actually pulls the eye is untested; the 1/1500 circle and the three-circle bar for reading as blurred are assumptions, and the quadrature sum of defocus and diffraction is a rule of thumb rather than a convolution. The strongest break of the kernel is the near pair: a partner 0.5 m behind a subject at 3 m is within the depth of field of a full-frame 35 mm f/2.8 (0.78 circles), and it stays sharp at f/8 for any gap up to 5 m (1.17 circles at 5 m), so shallow focus there directs nothing.
- Fix: Treat the kernel's claim as conditional, with a number attached: shallow focus separates two subjects only when their depth gap exceeds roughly the depth of field, measured as the second subject's blur in circles. Full frame 50 mm f/2 at 3 m reaches 1 circle at 0.22 m behind and 3 circles at 0.77 m behind, but only 0.19 m in front (blur scales with gap over the partner's own distance, and that distance is shorter on the near side, so the near side blurs sooner). A two-shot 0.5 m apart at a 2.9 m standing distance needs 85 mm at f/1.4 to reach 1.7 circles, and a 3-circle blur needs a 0.93 m gap with that lens and 3.8 m with 35 mm at f/2.8. For a deep-focus plan, size the reachable near and far pair first against the diffraction-optimal f-number and the light cost, and prefer a smaller format and a wider lens over closing the aperture further.
- Why this fix: A single metric (blur of the partner in circles) makes the choice comparable across lens, aperture, sensor and distance, and it separates what the camera can do from what the viewer does. Putting the diffraction disc in the same total blur shows the floor directly instead of assuming stopping down always helps, which is the point the diffraction kernel makes in words.
- Rule of thumb: Control of deep versus shallow is aperture first (x11 across the usable range), sensor size second (x5), and focal length only through background distance at a fixed framing (x1.1 for a near partner, x6.5 for a distant background); and two subjects closer in depth than the depth of field stay equally sharp whatever the narrative intent, so shallow focus cannot direct attention between them, while a deep-focus stage 0.6 m to 12 m deep leaves the worst plane about 2.6 circles blurred on full frame at its best f-number, so it is out of reach there.

The formulas are the standard thin-lens relations written out in the script and cross-checked against each other.
