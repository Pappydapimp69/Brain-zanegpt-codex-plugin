## E1 — self directed humor dose response flips at low rate in high stakes settings
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/sandbox-self-directed-humor-dose-response
- Tags: [humor][sandbox][negotiation][pitch][model]
- What: A deterministic repeated-interaction model of self-directed jokes. Each joke earns short-term warmth (satiating, and scaled by the listener's current belief that the sender is competent). Each joke and each silent turn is also evidence the listener uses to update a latent competent-vs-insecure estimate, from the flaw's severity and from the listener's expected base rate of joking in that setting. It sweeps joke rate and reports the best rate (p*) and the break-even rate (p_flip) where total value drops below telling no jokes. Cells: casual vs high-stakes (pitch or negotiation), new vs established relationship, high vs low prior credibility.
- Why built: The four-style frame (affiliative, self-enhancing, aggressive, self-defeating) is usually discussed as traits. The question here is the dose: at what frequency, and in which context, does a benign self-directed joke habit become self-defeating. The sandbox models structure and stated assumptions (measurement, scoring, dynamics) only. It makes no claim about the psychology of any real person, and none of its numbers are measurements of people.
- Design: Two sender types the listener entertains (secure-competent, insecure-low-competence) differ in joke base rate and in flaw-severity mix (mild / moderate / severe). The listener keeps log-odds of competent, adds a small per-turn task-evidence term, and adds a joke term (log ratio of base rate times severity likelihood) or a silence term. Established relationship means the prior log-odds is doubled and the joke evidence is halved (an assumed discount). Utility = summed warmth + a credibility weight times a logistic acceptance of the final belief, with an acceptance bar standing in for the counterparty alternative. Joke schedule is evenly spaced, expected-path (no randomness), 24 turns, 41-point rate grid.
- Got right: Direction and shape. Utility vs rate is an inverted U with an optimum and a break-even in every high-stakes cell. Casual cells never flipped (the best rate was 0.625-1.0). High-stakes new-relationship cells: p* 0.225, p_flip 0.350 (high prior); p* 0.100, p_flip 0.175 (low prior). Established plus high prior is insulated (p* 0.5, p_flip 0.8). Strengthening the counterparty alternative moved p_flip 0.350, 0.300, 0.225 (bar 0, 1, 2). More task evidence per turn moved it 0.125, 0.300, 0.625 (e 0.02, 0.10, 0.30). The uninformative-listener break scenario had 0 flips in 12 cells, so the threshold exists only through inference. Flaw severity at a fixed 0.3 rate: end belief of competence 0.764 with the competent mix, 0.001 with the insecure mix, 0.997 with no jokes.
- Where/why it failed: 5 of 7 pre-registered predictions failed, mostly on magnitude and one on method. P1: the stakes/new/low optimum was 0.10, not 0. P2: that cell's p_flip was 0.175, not at most 0.05. P3: stakes/new/prior 0.7 p_flip was 0.300, not under 0.25. P4: switching the warmth-credibility coupling off raised p_flip only 0.05 (0.175 to 0.225), not 0.10. P6 was censored: casual never flips, so a flip-based decomposition read 0 for both factors; re-measured post hoc on p*, the base-rate expectation alone explained the whole shift (0.75 to 0.35) and payoff weight plus acceptance bar alone explained none (0.75 to 0.75), the reverse of the prediction, though together they interact (stakes expectation with casual payoff gives p* 0.35 and no flip). Two predictions held (P5 severity, P7 break scenario). Also: established plus low prior flips earlier than new plus low (0.125 vs 0.175), so the claim that an established relationship insulates holds only with a high or mid prior.
- Fix: Treat the flip as a band set by listener expectation and stakes, not a universal number; report p* and p_flip together, report censoring (never) explicitly, and decompose context effects on an uncensored measure. Keep the severity and rate terms separate when advising on humor in a pitch or negotiation.
- Why this fix: A single frequency cutoff hides that the cost is the product of how diagnostic a joke is to this listener (base-rate expectation, severity) and how much the outcome depends on being believed (stakes, counterparty alternative). The 2x2 run shows neither factor alone creates the flip: it needs both. A censored threshold also silently reports no effect, which is how P6 first misled.
- Rule of thumb: In this model (not in people), self-directed humor is a dose: in a high-stakes setting with a new relationship the optimum was about 1 joke in 4 to 10 turns and the break-even 0.175 to 0.35 per turn, lower with a low prior or a strong counterparty alternative; casual settings never flipped; and a severe-flaw joke mix cost far more than extra mild jokes. Before applying any rate number, measure the listener's expectation of joking in that setting and what credibility is worth to the outcome, since these two inputs, not the sandbox constants, set the line.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand: every number above comes from running the shipped sandbox, with the seven predictions written before the first run (a later run only appended labelled post-hoc analyses that are excluded from the RESULT line). Everything else is recalled or assumed: all model constants (joke base rates, severity mixes, warmth size and satiation, evidence per turn, the established-relationship discount, the credibility weight, the acceptance bar), the warmth-credibility coupling, and the mapping of the four-style frame onto two sender types.
