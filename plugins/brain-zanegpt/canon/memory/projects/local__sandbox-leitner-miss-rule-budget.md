## E1 — leitner miss rule flip under a published style forgetting curve at fixed budget
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/sandbox-leitner-miss-rule-budget
- Tags: [memorization][teaching][proxy][testing]
- What: A deterministic expected-value Leitner learner (8 noise-free item difficulties, 240 days, box gaps 1/2/4/8/16/32 days times a schedule scale) with an FSRS-style power forgetting curve (recall 0.9 when the gap equals stability) and an exponential alternative, comparing reset-to-box-1 against drop-one-box on mean retention at a FIXED reviews-per-item budget, swept over starting stability s (0.5 to 8 days), stability-growth gain (0.2 to 2.5) and lapse keep (0.5, 0.2).
- Why built: To place real-curve learners on the axis that the earlier toy (E1 of the demotion sandbox) could only place by hand-set strength growth. Supports pole A of tension 'For real learners, is the better Leitner miss rule reset-to-box-1 or drop-one-box, and where is the flip?' (drop-one-box is at least as good as reset in 100 of 113 budget-matched cells) but only weakly: the reset-wins pocket is real, small, and the dearer-at-high-gain half of pole B did not reproduce.
- Design: Stdlib only, no RNG, no clock. Success of a review has probability R(gap, stability); the tree of success/lapse histories is expanded in expectation, merging states on a 6 percent stability bucket. Success grows stability by 1 + gain * (1.4 - difficulty) * S^-0.15 * h(R) with h larger at lower recall (desirable difficulty); a lapse sets stability to max(start, keep * S). Both rules are run over ten schedule scales; retention is interpolated at equal reviews per item (6, 9, 13, 20). Metric: mean retrievability over the horizon, with last-day retrievability as a check. Curve basis is recalled from memory of the spaced-repetition literature (power-law forgetting, Ebbinghaus-style exponential as the classic alternative), not re-fetched; every constant is a modelling choice and no human recall data were used.
- Got right: Matching the budget changes the story. Drop-one-box won (higher retention at equal reviews) in 100 of 113 cells at budget 13, including every starting stability at gain 0.6 with lapse keep 0.5 (edge +0.016 down to +0.007), and its edge shrank toward zero as gain rose (about +0.001 at gain 2.5), and the sign agreed between power and exponential curves in 153 of 169 cases. The rule is a second-order lever: |delta| was at most 0.04 in all 355 cell-budget pairs, so budget and schedule scale matter far more. The robust reset-wins pocket sits at gain 0.2 to 0.3, starting stability 1 to 2 days (fresh-item recall about 0.7 to 0.9 at the first gaps), most visible with lapse keep 0.2: reset beat drop-one by 0.004 to 0.024 in 7 cells beyond a 0.002 noise floor (the other 6 reset-win cells were within noise). A finer schedule grid kept the pocket cell negative (-0.0084), so it is not an interpolation artefact.
- Where/why it failed: My central prediction failed. I predicted a clean sign flip along starting stability at gain 0.6 (reset for weak learners, drop-one for strong) with the crossing between 1 and 4 days; there was no flip at gain 0.6 at all (delta +0.016, +0.012, +0.010, +0.009, +0.007 for s 0.5 to 8), and at gain 0.3 the weakest learner already favoured drop-one. I also predicted the rule would matter little (true) and reset would hold the weak side (false). The pocket is non-monotone in s (drop-one wins again at s 0.5 and at s 4 to 8 for gain 0.2) and it moves with budget: the same cell is -0.005 at 13 reviews per item and +0.028 at 20. A hard-threshold recall variant (success iff recall at least 0.6) gave exact ties for gains 0.6 and up because the best schedule under the cap never lapses, and reset-better only at gain 0.2 (-0.046); my predicted flip inside gains 0.2 to 1.5 did not appear. A first threshold test (cut-off 0.8) was vacuous, produced by interpolating a non-monotone frontier, and was withdrawn. The end-of-horizon metric disagreed with mean retention on sign in 88 of 355 pairs, so the winner depends on which retention is wanted.
- Fix: Replace the single-axis flip claim with a regime claim: default to drop-one-box when schedules are re-tuned to a fixed budget, expect reset to win only for slow-strengthening learners (gain around 0.3 or less) with first-gap recall near 0.7 to 0.9, and report the winner per budget and per retention metric instead of one flip point. The ledger's wording 'where is the flip' needs a budget axis added.
- Why this fix: Budget matching lets each rule choose its own schedule scale, which removes the cost bias of the earlier fixed-schedule comparison; the withdrawn threshold test and the fine-grid check mark where the numbers can and cannot carry weight (the sign near zero is inside interpolation noise).
- Rule of thumb: At equal review budget under a soft forgetting curve, prefer drop-one-box by default and treat reset-to-box-1 as the exception for slow-strengthening learners whose first-gap recall is already about 0.7 to 0.9; the difference is at most a few points of mean retention, so tune the schedule scale and check last-day retention before spending effort on the miss rule.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox three times (run 1 crashed on a print after the grid was computed, runs 2 and 3 completed, all three recorded as FAIL in the file header). The forgetting-curve family and all constants are modelling choices described under Design, and the claims hold only inside this model.
