## E1 — spelling eye rhymes act as stray merges
- Date: 2026-09-30
- Project: local/sandbox-spelling-eye-rhymes-act-as-stray-merges
- Tags: [songwriting][rhyme][testing]
- What: A spelling-suffix rhyme judge corrupted the lettering of every trap stanza I wrote (5 of 5) through eye-rhyme merges and missed true rhymes, yet the open/stable scheme verdict changed in only 1 of them, so a shape verdict hides most lettering corruption.
- Why built: Compose the -ough spelling false-positive finding with the one-stray-merge verdict-flip finding: an eye rhyme is exactly a stray merge, so how often does it reach the verdict?
- Design: Hand-written phonetic rimes for 28 words; spelling judge equals the last three letters; groups lettered by first appearance with singletons as X; verdict stable if at most one unrhymed line and every group is contiguous. Six 4-line stanzas: five containing eye rhymes (though/through, rough/cough, home/come, love/move, dear/bear) and one control where spelling and sound agree.
- Got right: Spelling letters differ from phonetic letters in 5 of 5 trap stanzas (XAXA read as AXAX or ABAB; ABAB read as AAAA) and agree on the control; the verdict flipped only for dear/bear/near/wear, from open ABAB to stable AAAA.
- Where/why it failed: I predicted at least 2 verdict flips; the final run gave 1. Run 1 showed 2, but only because my control stanza stay/day/fall/wall was not clean: stay and day differ in their last three letters, so the spelling judge also splits true rhymes (AABB read as XXAA, stable to open).
- Fix: Validate a rhyme judge by comparing its letters with phonetic letters line by line, not by comparing scheme verdicts; treat both eye-rhyme merges and missed differently-spelled rhymes as the failure class.
- Why this fix: 4 of the 5 corrupted stanzas kept the same verdict, so a verdict-level test would pass a judge that got every one of them wrong.
- Rule of thumb: A spelling rhyme judge acts as a source of stray merges and stray splits; in this run it corrupted 5 of 5 trap stanzas but moved the open/stable verdict only once, so test the letters, never just the verdict.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; numbers are copied from its output. The pronunciations are hand-written, and the two-pass part of my first prediction was not exercised because this judge has no slant tier.
- Composed: sandbox-lyrical-composition/E6, sandbox-one-stray-merge-flips-open-verdicts/E1, sandbox-two-pass-letters-cannot-rescue-a-spelling-rhyme-judge/E1

- Observed: RESULT: PASS spelling judge letters 5/6 stanzas differently and flips the open/stable verdict in 1/6; control stanza agrees=True
