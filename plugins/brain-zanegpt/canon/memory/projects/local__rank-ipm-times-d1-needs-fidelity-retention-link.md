## E1 — rank ipm times d1 needs fidelity retention link
- Date: 2026-10-15
- Project: local/rank-ipm-times-d1-needs-fidelity-retention-link
- Tags: [game-ads][testing][model][sandbox]
- What: A toy ranking of 18 ad variants (3 hooks, 3 gameplay-fidelity levels, fail ending kept or lost) on IPM alone versus IPM times D1 retention. Under my assumed model the IPM-only winner has fidelity 0.4 and the IPM times D1 winner has fidelity 1.0 with 18% more retained installs per 1000 impressions; if D1 does not depend on fidelity both rankings pick the same variant, and a clock recut that drops the fail ending cuts the winner's score by 23% while a beat recut keeps it.
- Why built: The kernel says rank concept variants on IPM times D1, because high IPM with low retention means the wrong audience. I wanted to see when that second factor changes the pick, using the fail-ad cut-down lessons as the recut step.
- Design: IPM = 10 x hook x (1.6 - 0.6 x fidelity) x 1.3 if the ending is kept; D1 = 0.15 + 0.25 x fidelity (assumed: misleading ads convert more and retain less). Scenarios: S1 full ranking; S2 flat D1 (control); S3 the winner under a Wilson lower bound on D1 at 1000, 300, 100 and 50 impressions (built to BREAK the point-estimate ranking); S4 recut by clock versus by beat. No randomness.
- Got right: The ranking pair differs exactly when D1 depends on fidelity and is identical with a flat D1, so the extra factor only matters when conversion and retention pull apart. The recut loss of 23% equals 1 minus 1 over 1.3, matching the ending bonus.
- Where/why it failed: My S3 prediction (lower-bound ranking flips at least one pair at 1000 impressions) was wrong: 0 of 3 flipped. After adding the impressions sweep, flips appeared only at 300 and 100 impressions (1 of 3 each) and vanished at 50, where the installs are so few both candidates fall to the same lower bound. So small-sample fragility is real but non-monotone in this model.
- Fix: Rank on IPM times D1, but compute D1 with a lower bound and require the same winner at two impression budgets before a recut; treat a flat D1 as a sign the second factor is not informative.
- Why this fix: The sweep showed the winner can change between budgets (1 flip at 300 and 100, none at 1000 and 50), so agreement at two budgets is a cheap guard that the pick is not an artifact of one sample size.
- Rule of thumb: IPM times D1 changes the pick only when retention moves against conversion (18% more retained installs in my toy model, 0 change with flat D1), and its winner can flip with sample size (1 of 3 pairs at 300 and 100 impressions), so confirm at two budgets before decomposing a winner.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox; every functional form and constant is an assumed model, not measured ad data, and the real IPM and retention numbers were not used.
