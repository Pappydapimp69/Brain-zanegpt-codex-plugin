## E1 — learned cutoff frozen early loses valid presses but executes fewer invalid gate presses
- Date: 2026-10-18
- Project: local/learned-cutoff-frozen-early-loses-valid-presses-but-executes-fewer-invalid-gate-presses
- Tags: [ui][input][state][gamedev][testing]
- What: On a period-10 width-3 stream of 150 presses with jitter on every sixth press, a cutoff frozen after K presses as the running maximum valid lag loses valid presses until K reaches 36, one past the press that carries the maximum valid lag (21, at index 35), while the same frozen cutoff run as a gate with no validity check executes fewer invalid presses the earlier it is frozen: 85 at K 10, 92 at K 30, then 100 from K 36 on.
- Why built: Train pick joining the lossless-cutoff-is-worst rule and its one-sided formula with the learned-cutoff lesson, which left the gate-only side of a learned cutoff untested.
- Design: Presses at t = 2i + 1 with base lag i mod 4 plus 1 and jitter 5, 9 or 17 on every sixth press; valid iff the wave is available at the landing tick; loss counts valid presses above the cutoff; gate invalid counts non-valid presses at or below the cutoff; cutoffs frozen after every K from 1 to 150.
- Got right: Valid loss was above zero for every K up to the first maximum-lag press and zero after (10, 4, 0, 0 at K 10, 30, 60, 100), gate-invalid executions were nondecreasing in K and equal to the offline value 100 from K 36, and the early cutoff at K 10 was strictly cheaper for the gate (85) while losing 10 valid presses.
- Where/why it failed: Nothing in the prediction failed, but the gate side saturates (100 invalid is every non-valid press at or below lag 21) and the learner sees validity feedback that a gate-only policy would not have; one stream and one wave were run.
- Fix: Do not read an early learned cutoff as safe or unsafe by one policy alone; pick the policy first, then use the running maximum for the validity guard and keep a gate-only design off a learned cutoff, or add the validity check.
- Why this fix: The same frozen number is lossy under validity and cheap under the gate, so a cutoff tuned on the lossless side moves the gate toward its worst case as it learns.
- Rule of thumb: A learned cutoff becomes lossless under validity at the same K where its gate-only invalid count reaches the offline maximum (K 36 here), so the learning that removes valid loss also removes the gate's protection.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand by running the sandbox in this session on one P10 W3 stream with one deterministic jitter pattern; other waves and a learner without validity feedback were not run.
- Composed: lossless-cutoff-is-worst-without-validity/E1, sandbox-one-sided-formula-explains-gate-invalid-counts/E1, learned-cutoff-is-lossless-only-after-the-max-valid-lag-outlier-is-seen/E1

- ZaneGPT verdict: weak — the 36/36 match is one deterministic stream with a prediction fixed in advance, so the K 10 versus offline gap (85 versus 100) is solid, but "removes the gate's protection at the same K" is generalised from n=1 and was never tested against a stream where the two thresholds could come apart.
- Hidden assumption: The "same K" coincidence is structural, but it's an artifact of this one stream. The rule assumes the running-max cutoff is the single quantity driving both valid loss and gate-invalid count, so the two curves must meet at the same K. That only holds because the max lag (21) is also the max over everything, and because the gate's invalid count is monotone in the cutoff. Nothing in the data shows that lossless-under-validity must coincide with gate-invalid reaching offline, only that both depend on one number here.
- Would prove it wrong: A stream where the cutoff reaches the offline maximum at K=a but gate-invalid keeps changing afterward. That happens if a later press has an invalid landing at a lag between the old and new cutoff. Equally damaging is a stream where gate-invalid hits its offline value before the max-lag press arrives. Either one makes the "same K" claim false. A single-seed result of 36 doesn't distinguish "rule" from "coincidence of this jitter schedule".
- Break scenario: Move the max-lag press far later, or put an invalid press with lag above the early cutoff but below the final maximum. Concretely, make the jitter 17 land only at index 140 with every earlier jitter at 5 or 9. Valid loss then stays above zero until K=141. Gate-invalid should reach 100 only if the invalid presses' lags are all at or below the cutoff by then. Re-run with the jitter positions and values randomized across, say, 200 seeds, and report how often the two thresholds differ.
