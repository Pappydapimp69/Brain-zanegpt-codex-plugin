## E1 — humor style audience fit affiliative safe aggressive backfires breaks
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Project: local/humor
- Tags: [humor][simulation][social]
- What: A deterministic toy model of one sender using each of four humor styles (affiliative, self-enhancing, aggressive, self-defeating) toward 36 receiver cells (tolerance 0.2/0.5/0.8, in-group or out-group, target shared with the receiver or not, sender power gap -0.5/0/+0.5). Payoff = rapport (laughter minus exclusion) minus harm (receiver harm, sender retaliation cost, and a weighted third-party harm for the absent target). It tests the claim "affiliative humor is safe, aggressive humor backfires" and locates where it breaks.
- Why built: To stop treating a style label as a safety property. A request for a joke in a given style needs the audience (who shares the target, who has power, how often it repeats) before it can be judged. This models structure and stated assumptions only; it says nothing true about real people or about any questionnaire score.
- Design: Fixed grid, no RNG, standard library only. Assumptions stated in the file: laughter decays 0.85 per repeat; out-group fit 0.6; affiliative pays a flat 0.12 exclusion cost to out-group receivers (inside-reference humor); aggressive jokes get a 1.3 bonding boost when the target is shared and only 0.3 laughter when it is not, with harm that grows 30 percent per repeat and is multiplied by (1 + power gap) when the sender is the higher-power party; sender power adds obligatory laughter 0.4 times the gap to the OBSERVED signal but not to true rapport; a third-party weight tau (default 0.3) charges the absent target. Seven numeric predictions were written before the first run, plus a decay/exclusion sensitivity sweep.
- Got right: All 7 predictions held. Single joke: affiliative negative in 0 of 36 cells; aggressive positive in 19 (the 18 shared-target cells plus one in-group, high-tolerance, equal-power cell), mean -0.062 versus affiliative +0.340 on the full mix, so the headline claim is true on the average mixed audience. Hidden backfire: of the cells where aggressive loses, the share whose observed laughter still looks fine is 6/6 at power gap +0.5, 2/5 at 0 and 3/6 at -0.5, so the sender with power sees the least evidence of the harm. Move-target: keeping the comic mechanism but removing the victim beats the shared-target version once tau exceeds about 0.065.
- Where/why it failed: The claim fails in four places. (1) Shared-target in-group audience: aggressive mean 0.371 beats affiliative 0.340 on the 18 shared cells at tau 0.3, so "backfires" is audience-conditional; but the gain is bought from the absent target, and at tau 0.5 only 14 of 18 shared cells stay positive. (2) Power: the high-power sender gets obligatory laughter, so the failing cells look fine from the sender's side. (3) Repetition hits both styles: affiliative stays positive in-group indefinitely, but for out-group receivers its marginal joke turns negative at joke index 6 (index 2 to 32 across the decay/exclusion sweep) and 18 of 36 cells are cumulatively negative by n=20; shared-target aggressive is net negative in 6 of 18 cells by n=8 and 16 of 18 by n=20. (4) Self-defeating is not uniformly bad: mean +0.46 from a higher-power sender, +0.06 from a lower-power one, and 12 of 12 low-power cells negative cumulatively at n=8 versus 0 high-power. Limits: the result follows from the assumed parameters (the model has no learning or relationship memory), so the lesson is where to look, not how big the effects are.
- Fix: Judge a humor request by audience fit before style: ask who shares the target, who holds power, whether the room includes the target's side, and how many times the bit will repeat. Do not read the sender's observed laughter as audience consent when the sender holds power. When an aggressive bit is wanted for bonding, keep the mechanism and move the target to something with no person behind it.
- Why this fix: The sweep shows style label alone predicts sign correctly only on the mixed average; the four breaks above are each driven by one audience variable, so asking for those variables is the cheapest check that changes the prediction. Moving the target is already how the suno comedy case was resolved, and here it dominates once any third-party weight above about 0.065 is counted.
- Rule of thumb: Style labels do not decide safety, audience fit does: affiliative humor goes net negative on repetition and for out-group receivers (inside references), aggressive humor can win with a shared-target in-group but only while the absent target's cost is weighted low (below roughly tau 0.38 for the worst cell), and a sender with power cannot use laughter as the test. Check target, power gap and repeat count before choosing the style.
- Provenance (verified/assumed): Verified first-hand: the sandbox run itself (deterministic, 7 of 7 prediction checks, result line PASS) and every number quoted above. Recalled from memory only, kept separate from the run: the four-style frame and its 2003 questionnaire origin (affiliative and self-enhancing as the benign pair, aggressive and self-defeating as the potentially harmful pair); a web search returned a summary of the same frame but the original paper could not be fetched here. All model parameters are my own stated assumptions, not data.
